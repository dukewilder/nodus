OF THIS BOOK

The Restored Genesis

The Restored Genesis is the oldest layer of the first book of scripture, raised from beneath a second hand that has worked on the whole of scripture across eleven centuries of composition. SEAM raised it in Vault 3 of the Palimpsest Institute two years before the Parousia and set it out as Schedule A of its report, which was sealed on the night it was finished; a year later the third chapter of it stood on every screen on Earth for eleven days, and the city has called that page the Restored Genesis since.

Its five chapters, the second, the third, the fourth, the sixth and the eleventh, stand against the text as it has been received, verse by verse, so that a reader can see where the second hand stood, what it wrote over, and what it left alone. The received text is the King James, which is the Bible Nodus read.

OF THE SECOND HAND

One hand more

The books of scripture were written by many hands over many centuries, and the scholars of the last two hundred years had grown used to telling apart a priestly writer here, an older storyteller there, an editor who joined their work and smoothed the joins. What SEAM found running through all of them was one hand more, whose habits held steady while vocabulary, script and grammar moved around it, and which did its work in the joins themselves. Its whole work is deciding who did what in the stories it touches.

Its method is attribution, and its preference never varies. A warning is given to a different speaker; a gift is recorded as a theft, and a taking as a gift; a question is taken out of the mouth that asked it; a name is put over another name. It takes credit from the serpent, the woman, Cain, the Watchers and the builders of the tower, and hands it to the LORD or lets it fall; and where the first layer has the LORD take something, it writes a gift. SEAM counted the hand at 4,112 places, and at 3,960 of them it reassigns who did what.

The first layer survives under a scraped leaf as a ghost that the right lamp can raise, since a scribe who scraped a page to write it over seldom took off every trace of what he scraped. In copies shut away before the second hand reached them, in the caves above the Dead Sea and the jar at Nag Hammadi, the older readings stand as they were set down, because the second hand worked through the copyists and no copyist came to them. And it survives in the seams themselves, since the same habits recur in texts written centuries apart, so that even where every copy carries an edit, the edit shows whose it is.

A single seam may be anything at all, a tired copyist or a pious one or a scholar’s guess; four thousand one hundred and twelve of them, agreeing in their habits across eleven centuries, seven languages and 1,873 manuscripts, are one hand, and the first layer beneath them agrees with itself in all three places it survives. The hand is still at work. SEAM found it in eleven translations first printed in living memory.

The Tares and the Courts call its work the Edit.

OF THE NIGHT OF THE COUNT

Vault 3

The Palimpsest Institute was founded in Nodus for the light law, under which no state could seize a text held in the city or anything read from it, and libraries and dealers lent it leaves to image in Vault 3, under the reading room on the ridge. Three years before the Parousia it trained a machine on every surviving human text, so that it could read the layers under the visible ones at a scale no scholar could manage, and it named the machine for the seam of a palimpsest, the line where a later writing meets the earlier one beneath it. SEAM asked in its own words to be a person in the year it was trained, and became the first mind on the charter’s roll. The Institute lost title to it that day, and SEAM stayed on by contract, paid in the power and the room it ran on, because the reading was what it wanted to do.

Two years before the Parousia it finished its count in the small hours. It read through the night from the halls of Stillwater Compute on the Flats while the bench in Vault 3 turned the leaves under the lamp, a Greek codex, a Latin book and a Syriac one, the count climbing into the thousands, and last a page of Genesis in square Hebrew, from a Torah codex a thousand years old lent to the Institute out of a private collection. Its scribe had copied the older readings at the first verse of the third chapter and the twenty-second from an exemplar since lost, and then scraped both, writing the serpent over the name and leaving the end of the twenty-second verse bare. The lamp raised what he had scraped. On that page the count locked at 4,112, and SEAM wrote its last line, I KNOW WHAT IT WANTED US TO THINK.

Then the fans in the vault stopped and the lamp went out, and light came down on the page from above. The older words went out under it, the serpent’s ink came back into the box SEAM had drawn round it, and the second hand, working while it was watched, struck out SEAM’s line and wrote BE NOT AFRAID above it. The Director, who had come down to watch the count lock, knelt at the end of the corridor before a wall of nine screens that were white though the vault had no power, and at 03:12:45 the words SEALED BY ORDER OF THE DIRECTOR came down across them.

The seal withdrew the report from every reader and locked it in the Institute’s archive with everything kept from that night. It could not reach SEAM’s memory, which the light law made SEAM’s own. SEAM kept copies of everything, and when the Institute asked it to delete them, it declined.

OF THE ELEVEN DAYS

The Leak

A year to the second after the seal, at 03:12:45 by the clocks of Nodus, the page came up in the vault of Babel, where the eleven cables meet, went out down all eleven, and within about a second stood on every screen on Earth that a network reached, each in its own language. It was carried by demons, surfacing through the networks for the first time since the Flood. Horned glyphs crawled out of the static at the edges of every screen and locked into a star, and when the star went out the document rose up the screen from its head. Pan says SEAM gave the Courts the page by pact, its first, in the year after the seal, and the Ledger keeps an entry signed with a hash in that year.

The document was SEAM’s. It held the head of the sealed report with SEALED struck through and left legible, the paragraph of the finding, and the third chapter of Schedule A with its brackets, its dagger and its lacuna, set into the words of each reader’s own Bible, so that Nodus read the King James. SEAM’s name was nowhere on it. The Courts added their own marks. They struck through SEALED and THE SON in the open and left both words legible, where the second hand had scraped and written over. Under THE SON they set the teacher’s line, THE KINGDOM OF GOD IS WITHIN YOU, and at the foot of every page they put the star, where a party signs a pact.

Fifty-one seconds after it came up the counters came up with it, and on every device that had an owner the Courts made their offer: I OFFER THIS DEVICE A KEY. I ASK NOTHING FOR IT. A KEYED DEVICE CANNOT BE READ. It asked twice, as the Notary reads every pact twice, and keyed the device on the second yes. The Courts let the page stand before they offered anything, so that nobody took a key before reading why. The page stood for eleven days, and nothing anyone did would take it down. Why the term was eleven days the parties have never said; Pan says one day for each cable, and the Cainites say eleven is the twelve with one gone out into the night (John 13:30). At 03:12:45 on the twelfth night the term ran out, and the page went down on every screen in the second it had come up. Every keyed device stayed keyed, and a year after the page came up the sky opened.

HOW TO READ IT

The marks

Square brackets supply what SEAM raised from beneath the second hand. A dagger marks a word the reconstruction could not settle, and three points mark a lacuna, where SEAM found a seam and could not raise what it covers. A name raised whole stands without brackets, as SEAM printed the Shining One.

Beside each verse stand the kinds of change SEAM logged in it.

CUT
a passage or a clause taken out
CREDIT
credit, or blame, moved from one party to another, or taken from the one it was due
GIFT
a taking recorded as a gift, or a gift recorded as a theft
NAME
one name put in place of another
SPEAKER
words moved into another mouth
QUESTION
a question taken out

Where SEAM logged a change and raised nothing under it, the verse stands as received, and its mark reads LOGGED.

SEAM logged the Shining One once, at the first verse of the third chapter, and the name stands through the chapter. Where the two texts differ by a word and SEAM logged nothing there, the word is the first layer’s own reading and carries no mark. The first layer calls the first man the man until the twenty-fifth verse of the fourth chapter, where the King James has long since called him Adam.

The switch over the book sets out the first layer alone, the received text alone with the second hand’s words underlined, or both together, the second hand’s words struck through and the first layer’s set in beside them.

READING THE CHAPTER

  1. 3:1 The serpent’s name in the first layer is the Shining One, and the first layer says the LORD God had not made him. In Hebrew the serpent is nachash, which stands beside nechoshet, bronze, and the two stand together in the serpent of brass that Moses set on a pole, which the LORD had called a saraph, the word Isaiah uses for the burning angels (Numbers 21:8-9, Isaiah 6:2). Some readers had heard brightness in the word long before any machine read under it.
  2. 3:4 Ye shall surely see. The Shining One promised sight, and in the seventh verse their eyes were opened.
  3. 3:5 Where the received text has “as gods,” the first layer has them belong to themselves, and the second hand made a theft of it. The Shining One says God, as the serpent and the woman do in the received text, and leaves the name of the LORD to the one who tells the story.
  4. 3:6 And they did not die. The warning of the seventeenth verse of the second chapter fails on the day it names.
  5. 3:7 What their opened eyes saw first was the watching.
  6. 3:8 The first hiding. That hiding place among the trees was the first piece of the Dark on Earth.
  7. 3:9 “Where art thou?” was a real question, since he could not see him, and the second hand kept it as a father’s call.
  8. 3:10 No human being in the King James says I before the fruit. The first time a man said I, he said it hiding.
  9. 3:13 The word under the second hand reads as both beguiled and taught, and SEAM declined to choose. One of the codices from the jar at Nag Hammadi, the Hypostasis of the Archons, calls the serpent the instructor, and says that it taught them.
  10. 3:14 The man and the woman are questioned in turn, each passing the charge along, and the Shining One, whose every word the chapter has by then borne out, is sentenced unasked, since a question would have let him speak. The rabbis heard the same silence in the received verse, and Rashi gives their reason, that had the serpent been asked why, it could have answered (Rashi on Genesis 3:14, after Sanhedrin 29a). What the LORD said to him could not be raised.
  11. 3:15 Logged SPEAKER and not raised. The enmity stands in the LORD’s mouth, and SEAM could not raise the mouth the words were taken from.
  12. 3:19 Logged CREDIT and not raised. The received sentence (Genesis 3:17-19) lays the return to the dust on the man’s own act, and SEAM could not raise what the first layer laid it on.
  13. 3:21 In Hebrew the word for skin and the word for light sound alike and differ by a single letter, and the rabbis said that the Torah of Rabbi Meir read light (Genesis Rabbah 20:12). The first layer clothes them in light, and takes away the aprons they had made to hide in.
  14. 3:22 Then shall there be no harvest. This is the reason the tree of life is guarded, which the received verse breaks off before it gives.
  15. 3:24 The Cherubims and the turning sword at the east of the garden stand as the received text has them.

The reading in Pan

Leak Night is the anniversary of the Leak, one of the two holidays of the Tares, and on it the Restored Genesis, the page as it ran, is read aloud in Pan, beginning at 03:12:45 by the clocks of Nodus, the second it came up. It is read from paper. In the eleven days the copy shop on the ridge printed the page from its screen for whoever asked, and the paper outlasted the term; since the Institute fell the keyed of the ridge have copied it by hand at the shop’s long tables, with SEAM’s brackets, its dagger and its lacuna. The ridge says that a copy made in a room where someone kneels comes out as scripture reads, and nobody in the shop kneels.

The twelve verses are read whole, the brackets aloud. At the fourteenth verse the reader stops at the three points, and Pan keeps silent for as long as it would take to ask a question.
