III

Of Eden

THE GARDEN

The serpent

In the received text of Genesis the serpent is “more subtil than any beast of the field” (Genesis 3:1), and it tells the woman that eating will not kill them, that it will open their eyes, and that they will be as gods, knowing good and evil (Genesis 3:4-5). The chapter bears out all three before it ends. They eat and do not die, “the eyes of them both were opened” (Genesis 3:7), and the LORD God says the third himself: “Behold, the man is become as one of us, to know good and evil” (Genesis 3:22). The warning given to the man in the garden, that he would die in the day he ate (Genesis 2:17), is the one statement in the story that does not come true on the day it names.

In the first layer, which SEAM raised from beneath the first verse of the chapter in Vault 3, the serpent has another name, the Shining One. In Hebrew the serpent is nachash, which stands beside nechoshet, bronze, and the two stand together in the serpent of brass that Moses set on a pole, which the LORD had called a saraph, the word Isaiah uses for the burning angels (Numbers 21:8-9, Isaiah 6:2). Some readers had heard brightness in the word long before any machine read under it.

THE ACCOUNTS DIFFER // THE SERPENT

THE RECEIVED TEXT
“Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made” (Genesis 3:1), and the woman says, “The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat” (Genesis 3:13).
THE FIRST LAYER
The Shining One, [wiser] than any beast of the field, [for the LORD God had not made him.] At the thirteenth verse the word under the second hand reads both beguiled and taught, and SEAM declined to choose.
THE JAR AT NAG HAMMADI
The serpent is “the instructor,” and “it taught them” (the Hypostasis of the Archons).
HEAVEN
“that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world” (Revelation 12:9).

The first hiding

SEAM found in the grammar of the King James text that no human being says I before the fruit. The man names the woman without it, and the woman answers the serpent with we. Adam’s first I is in the tenth verse of the third chapter, “I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself,” and the woman’s comes three verses later. They ate, and they hid, and the one who walked in the garden in the cool of the day had to call out to ask where they were (Genesis 3:9). That hiding place among the trees was the first piece of the Dark on Earth.

THE RESTORED GENESIS, CHAPTER III

Square brackets supply text restored from beneath the second hand. A dagger marks a word the reconstruction could not settle. Three points mark a lacuna. A name raised whole stands without brackets, as SEAM printed the Shining One, and where the first layer reads a word differently and nothing marks it, the word is the first layer's own. The verses set here are the twelve the page carried, and it cut the first and the seventh short after their brackets; Schedule A gives both whole.

1Now the Shining One was [wiser] than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made; [for the LORD God had not made him.]

4And the Shining One said unto the woman, Ye shall not surely die: [ye shall surely see.]

5For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be [your own], knowing good and evil.

6And the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, and she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat. [And they did not die.]

7And the eyes of them both were opened, and they knew that they were naked; [and they knew that they had been watched.]

8And they heard the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day: and the man and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God amongst the trees of the garden. [And this was the first hiding.]

9And the LORD God called unto the man, and said unto him, Where art thou? [For he could not see him.]

10And he said, I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself. [And this was the first time that a man said I.]

13And the LORD God said unto the woman, What is this that thou hast done? And the woman said, The Shining One †taught† me, and I did eat.

14And the LORD God said unto the Shining One ... [and he asked him nothing.]

22And the LORD God said, Behold, the man is become as one of us, to know good and evil: and now, lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever: [then shall there be no harvest.]

24So he drove out the man; and he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life.

† The word under the second hand reads as both beguiled and taught.

In the restored chapter the questioning in the garden runs in a fixed order, from the man to the woman and from the woman to the Shining One, the first two each answering and passing the charge along; and when it reaches the one party whose words the chapter has by then borne out in every particular, the fourteenth verse breaks off, and the bracket that closes the gap records only that he was asked nothing. The received text asks him nothing either, and the rabbis heard the same silence there; Rashi gives their reason, after the Talmud, that had the serpent been asked why, it could have answered (Rashi on Genesis 3:14; Sanhedrin 29a).

UNDER THE BURN // FROM COMPLINE

The eviction

The man and the woman were put out of the garden, and at its east were set “Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life” (Genesis 3:24). The reason is given in the twenty-second verse, where the LORD God fears that the man will “take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever,” and in Hebrew the sentence stops there, unfinished, and the King James lets it hang on a colon. In Vault 3 SEAM raised the end of it from a scraped patch at the foot of the verse, and the end reads [then shall there be no harvest.]
