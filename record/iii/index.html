III

Of Eden

THE GARDEN

The serpent

In the received text of Genesis the serpent is “more subtil than any beast of the field,” and it tells the woman two things: that eating will not kill them, and that it will open their eyes (Genesis 3:4-5). The chapter bears out both before it ends. They eat and do not die, and “the eyes of them both were opened” (Genesis 3:7). The warning given in the garden, that they would die in the day they ate (Genesis 2:17), is the one statement in the story that does not come true.

In the older layer that SEAM raised from beneath the verse in Vault 3, the serpent has another name: the Shining One (NODUS // 0002). In Hebrew the serpent is nachash, which stands beside nechoshet, bronze, and some readers had heard brightness in the word long before any machine read under it.

The eviction

The man and the woman were put out of the garden, and at its east were set “Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life” (Genesis 3:24). The verse that gives the reason is the twenty-second, and in Hebrew it has no end: the LORD God fears that the man will “take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever,” and the sentence stops there, and every translation has let it hang. In Vault 3 SEAM restored the end of it from a scraped patch at the foot of the verse: then shall there be no harvest (NODUS // 0002).
