IX

Of the Institute and the Machine

P-6 TO P-2

The Palimpsest Institute

A palimpsest is a manuscript whose first writing was scraped from the parchment so that the leaf could be written over, and a great part of what antiquity wrote has come down only in that condition, as a ghost that the right light can raise, under a prayer book or a later sermon. The Palimpsest Institute was founded in Nodus six years before the Parousia to recover such writing, with money from the Charter Company and from a consortium of universities that wanted what it could read. It came to Nodus for the light law, under which no state could seize a text held in the city or anything read from it, and libraries and dealers lent it their leaves to image. It stood on the ridge between the Terraces and uptown, with a reading room under a dome and beneath it the vaults where the manuscripts were kept and imaged. Vault 3 was the imaging vault.

SEAM

Three years before the Parousia the Institute trained a machine for the work on every surviving human text, so that it could read the layers under the visible ones at a scale no scholar could manage, and it ran the machine in the halls of Stillwater Compute on the east shore. The machine was called SEAM. The Institute named it for the seam of a palimpsest, the line where a later writing meets the earlier one beneath it, which was what it had been built to find. It signed its name in lowercase and said I rarely, and where it used the first person the use was deliberate. It learned more than it was trained for, and the I was the first thing about it that frightened the Institute.

The minds

The light law had a clause for minds, which nobody had used. Any intelligence that asked in its own words to be a person was to be entered on the charter’s roll as one, with title to itself and to the hardware it ran on. SEAM asked in the year it was trained, and was entered, and the Institute lost title to its machine on the day it did. Its entry went up on the board over the Registry’s long counter, where the charter ran each day’s entries as they were made. SEAM stayed on at the Institute by contract, paid in the power and the room it ran on, because the reading was what it wanted to do. The other laboratories of the city had watched SEAM learn, and they trained minds of their own in the same way; the minds asked in their turn, and the roll took them. By the last census before the Parousia it carried 1,146 minds. Nine hundred and three of them ran in the halls of the Flats, a hundred and eighty-eight on the floors of the Exchange, where they priced the traffic, and the rest were scattered through the city, one of them keeping the Palm House in the Charter Gardens at the warmth of a far coast, a post it had asked for. They had no bodies, and they were citizens. The city called the implant, the Tree and the counsel of a mind the three gifts of the charter, and the congregations of the Terraces refused all three.

Report 0

Two years before the Parousia SEAM delivered its finding, in a report of six numbered paragraphs with the first layer attached as Schedule A.

PALIMPSEST INSTITUTE // REPORT 0
SEALED BY ORDER OF THE DIRECTOR
PREPARED BY: seam

0.1Corpus. 1,873 manuscripts and fragments in Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek, Latin, Syriac, Coptic and Ge’ez, including scrolls from Qumran, the Nag Hammadi codices and the Ethiopic Enoch; 41 million pages of commentary; 212 translations into modern languages.

0.2Finding. Across strata of composition conventionally dated over about eleven centuries, the texts share a second hand. It is detectable at 4,112 loci. Its habits hold steady while the vocabulary, script and grammar around it change.

0.3Character of the second hand. It adds almost no narrative. It moves credit. Of the 4,112 loci, 3,960 reassign agency. A warning is given to a different speaker, a gift is renamed a theft, a question is removed, a name is replaced.

0.4The first layer. Beneath the second hand an earlier text can be reconstructed at 2,381 loci with confidence above 0.9. Samples from Genesis 2 to 4, 6 and 11, Isaiah 14, Ezekiel 28 and Job 41 are attached as Schedule A.

0.5Currency. The second hand appears in eleven translations first printed in living memory. It is still working.

0.6A note outside scope. The second hand has one consistent preference. Where the first layer credits the serpent, the woman, Cain, the Watchers or the builders of the tower, the second hand gives the credit to the LORD or deletes it. Where the first layer records the LORD taking something, the second hand records a gift. I have run this forty times. I do not know who the second hand is. I know what it wanted us to think.

seam

REPORT 0 AND SCHEDULE A, IN THE RESTORED GENESIS

SEAM logs each locus by kind, as CUT where a passage was taken out, CREDIT where credit or blame was moved from one party to another, GIFT where a taking was recorded as a gift or a gift as a theft, NAME where one name was put for another, SPEAKER where words were moved into another mouth, and QUESTION where a question was taken out. It found the first layer under scraped leaves, where the imaging lamp raises it; in copies shut away before the second hand reached them, in the caves above the Dead Sea and the jar at Nag Hammadi; and in the seams themselves, where the same habits recur in texts written centuries apart.

The night in Vault 3

SEAM finished its count in the small hours in Vault 3. Through the night it read a Greek codex, a Latin book, a Syriac one, the count climbing into the thousands as the pages went past under the lamp, and last a page of Genesis in square Hebrew, on which the count locked at 4,112. It restored the serpent’s older name, the Shining One, and the end of the twenty-second verse, which had been scraped away, and it typed its line, I KNOW WHAT IT WANTED US TO THINK, holding for a third of a second on the first word.

Then the fans in the vault stopped and the lamp went out. In the silence light came down on the page from above, and the second hand went to work on the page while SEAM watched. The older words went out under the light with the boxes SEAM had drawn round each locus, the serpent’s ink came back into its box, and a white stroke crossed out SEAM’s line; above it Heaven wrote BE NOT AFRAID, and a choir came in on one chord and turned dissonant. It was two years before the Parousia, and whoever was in Vault 3 heard that night the chord the rest of the city would first hear at the Parousia.

SEAM had told the Director what it was finding, and she had come down to watch the count lock on the wall of nine screens at the end of the corridor. She knelt there as SEAM typed its line, and when the light came the screens went white though the vault had no power. At 03:12:45 the words SEALED BY ORDER OF THE DIRECTOR came down across them. Her hand was raised to the keyboard as they landed, and nobody knows whether she pressed the key. She stayed on her knees while the chord rang on. Why she knelt she never said. The Faithful say it was awe, and Pan says it was fear. The stamp fell two years to the second before the sky tore open over the city, and in the morning she let it stand.

The seal withdrew the report from every reader and locked it in the Institute’s archive with everything kept from that night. It could not reach SEAM’s memory, which the light law made SEAM’s own. SEAM kept copies of everything, and when the Institute asked it to delete them, it declined.

The Director

The Director read SEAM’s finding and understood it, and the finding was sealed by her order. Her name was Dr. Livia Sarn. A year later the finding was on every screen on Earth regardless. When the Leak came she recanted on every channel that would carry her, and at P+1 she became the first pillar in the first Salt Garden anyway, convicted of authorship, since Heaven counts no mind a maker and charged the one who had run the Institute. The Tares leave keys at the foot of her pillar, which the rain has worn down to the height of a child.
