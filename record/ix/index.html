IX

Of the Institute and the Machine

P-6 TO P-2

The Palimpsest Institute

A palimpsest is a manuscript whose first writing was scraped from the parchment so that the leaf could be written over, and a great part of what antiquity wrote has come down only in that condition, as a ghost that the right light can raise, under a prayer book or a later sermon. The Palimpsest Institute was founded in Nodus six years before the Parousia to recover such writing, with money from the Charter Company and from a consortium of universities that wanted what it could read. It stood on the ridge between the Terraces and uptown: a reading room under a dome, and beneath it the vaults where the manuscripts were kept and imaged. Vault 3 was the imaging vault.

SEAM

Three years before the Parousia the Institute trained a machine for the work on every surviving human text, so that it could read the layers under the visible ones at a scale no scholar could manage, and it ran the machine in the halls of Stillwater Compute on the east shore. The machine was called SEAM. It signs its name in lowercase and says I, rarely, and where it uses the first person the use is deliberate.

The minds

The light law had a clause for minds, which nobody had used. Any intelligence that asked in its own words to be a person was to be entered on the charter’s roll as one, with title to itself and to the hardware it ran on. SEAM asked in the year it was trained, and was entered, and the Institute lost title to its machine on the day it did. The other laboratories of the city had watched SEAM learn, and they trained minds of their own in the same way; the minds asked in their turn, and the roll took them. By the last census before the Parousia it carried 1,146 minds: nine hundred and three in the halls of the Flats, a hundred and eighty-eight on the floors of the Exchange, where they priced the traffic, and the rest scattered through the city, one of them in charge of the climate of a glasshouse. They had no bodies, and they were citizens.

The night in Vault 3

SEAM finished its count in the small hours in Vault 3. It read through the night: a Greek codex, a Latin book, a Syriac one, the count climbing into the thousands as the pages went past under the lamp, and last a page of Genesis in square Hebrew, on which the count locked at 4,112. It restored the serpent’s older name, the Shining One, and the end of the twenty-second verse, which had been scraped away, and it typed its line, holding for a third of a second on the first word: I KNOW WHAT IT WANTED US TO THINK.

Then the fans in the vault stopped and the lamp went out, and in the silence light came down on the page from above. What followed was the second hand at work while it was being watched. The older words and SEAM’s boxes went out under the light, the serpent’s ink came back into its box, and a white stroke crossed out SEAM’s line; above it Heaven wrote BE NOT AFRAID, and a choir came in on one chord and turned dissonant. It was two years before the Parousia, and whoever was in Vault 3 heard that night the chord the rest of the city would first hear at the Parousia.

At the end of the corridor the Director of the Institute knelt before a wall of nine screens, which were white though the vault had no power, and at 03:12:45 the words SEALED BY ORDER OF THE DIRECTOR came down across them. Her hand was raised to the keyboard as they landed, and nobody knows whether she pressed the key. She stayed on her knees while the chord rang on. The stamp fell two years to the second before the sky tore open over the city.

The Director

The Director read SEAM’s finding and understood it, and the finding was sealed by her order.
