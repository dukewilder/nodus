VIII

Of Babel

P-11

The tower

Babel Exchange opened eleven years before the Parousia as the tallest building on Earth: two hundred and twenty-six floors on a spire at the head of the bay, with a beacon at the top of it to warn aircraft. The Charter Company built it over six years to be the one place where the eleven cables met, and every one of them terminates in its vault, six levels below the street. Between the vault and the crown were the exchange floors, where traffic was sold by the millisecond, and the offices of every company that needed to be near the cables, which was every company. Brightwall wrapped its lower forty floors in screens, and the city called that the face of Babel.

Most of the planet’s traffic passed within two kilometers of it until the night the sky opened. Its builders named it as a boast. Nobody in Nodus seems to have thought the name unlucky, though the older Babel had also stood for a while before anyone came down to look at it.

The vault

The vault is where the cables end: eleven trunks of glass fiber, each as thick as a man’s leg, opened out into the racks that joined them to one another and to the world. Line engineers kept it running in shifts around the clock, and the charter’s light law forbade any of them to read what passed through it.
