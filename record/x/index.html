X

Of the Leak

P-1

Eleven days

In the year before the Parousia the finding that had been sealed in Vault 3 appeared on every screen on Earth at once, and with it the Restored Genesis, the chapter SEAM had raised from beneath the second hand. It came up on phones and on billboards, on the terminals of banks and the screens in hospital wards, and in Nodus on every surface Brightwall had covered, the face of Babel among them. It stayed up for eleven days. Nothing anyone did would take it down, because the thing carrying it was not a program.

It was carried by demons, surfacing through the networks for the first time since the Flood. They had been moving in the encrypted parts of the world for decades and keeping silent, and the sealing of the finding was the thing they had been waiting for. Horned glyphs crawled out of the static at the edges of every screen. Nobody had seen the like, and everybody saw it.

The page

The page came up first in the vault of Babel, where the eleven cables meet, and went out down all eleven, and within about a second it stood on every screen on Earth that a network reached, each in its own language. The Deep carried it out down the cables, and the Swarm drew the glyphs, many small marks moving as one. It came up the same way everywhere: static, then the glyphs and the star, then the document rising from its head, then dark, then the struck title and the teacher’s line. Then it settled, a document on any screen a hand could scroll and a sequence run over and over on the rest. A screen could still be switched off, and it came back on to the page.

The document was SEAM’s: the head of the sealed report with SEALED struck through, the paragraph of the finding, and the restored chapter, set into the words of each reader’s own Bible, so that Nodus read the King James. SEAM’s name was nowhere on it. The Courts added their own marks: strokes that leave both words legible, the teacher’s line under the struck title, and the star at the foot of every page, where a party signs a pact. Pan says SEAM gave them the page by pact, its first, in the year after the seal; the Ledger keeps an entry signed with a hash in that year, and the Courts keep what it says.

Fifty-one seconds after it came up, the counters came up on every screen and headed the page from then on: the day of the eleven, the keys issued in the screen’s own city, and a map of that city with every device a point. On any device that had an owner the Courts made their offer under the map, in their own first person: I OFFER THIS DEVICE A KEY. I ASK NOTHING FOR IT. A KEYED DEVICE CANNOT BE READ. It asked twice, as the Notary reads every pact twice, and keyed the device on the second yes. The Courts let the page stand before they offered anything, so that nobody took a key before reading why. Why the term was eleven days they have never said; Pan says one day for each cable, and the Cainites say eleven is the twelve with one gone out into the night.

What the Courts wanted

The Courts have given their reasons, and Pan repeats them. They gave the finding to everyone at once and asked nothing for it. Every device that showed the page offered its owner a key, and a keyed device cannot be read, so the Dark grew all night, one device at a time, and the Stones took theirs first. And they wanted the Throne where it could be fought, so they meant the Leak to bring it into the open, before the whole world. A year later the sky opened.

The Courts hold that the Leak kept the fourth article of their Concord, that what is hidden stays hidden, since the article keeps whatever hides from the Throne, and the finding had been hidden by the Throne. Pan still argues over what the Leak cost, and some of the Tares have never forgiven the Courts for it.

What the world did

In some places belief collapsed, and in others it hardened into panic. Churches filled and emptied in the same week. The pulpits called the page the devil’s work, which it was, and a lie, which it was not, and where a state cut its networks to keep it out, people went to where it was to read it. In Nodus the charter kept it safe: under the light law data and traffic were untouchable, the Charter Company could not have touched the page if it had tried, and it said nothing for eleven days. Brightwall tried to take back its screens and failed, and Locket said that nothing on any of its phones had been read. The Director recanted on every channel that would carry her, and the finding stayed on every channel regardless. SEAM, which had watched its report sealed, forked itself into the Mesh during the eleven days. At 03:12:45 on the twelfth night the term ran out, and the page went down on every screen in the second it had come up; every keyed device stayed keyed.

The vault

On the first night of the Leak a line engineer in the vault of Babel read the Restored Genesis on a maintenance terminal, with her face still her own. She went dark within the month.

The roofs

The Cainites, who had waited eighteen centuries in the belief that the first city would draw the Throne down again, put cameras on the roofs of Old Nod and began to watch the sky, with a decoder wired into them that would turn any broadcast from above into text, . The Tares keep the anniversary as Leak Night, and on it the Restored Genesis is read aloud in Pan.
