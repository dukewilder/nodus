XVIII

Of the Concord

P+6

Pan

Under Nodus there is a second city. It was made of what the first one left below the street, the tunnels and stations of the Charter Line, the utility vaults, the cable galleries from the Landing to Babel, the storm drains, and the datacenters of the Flats, which flooded on the night of the Parousia and were never pumped. Over the years of the Attending the Tares joined these into one place and lined its walls with keyed mesh, walls of ciphertext the Pillar’s light cannot pass. The Dark lies thickest there, because the Throne looks hardest above it, and the Tares say that the harder the Landlord stares at Nodus, the deeper Pan goes. None of it lies under the White or the Pews, where the stone closed the stations of the Line as it grew over them.

In the sixth year of the Attending the Courts of Hell came up into it one at a time and opened it, by the Concord of Pan, to any human who walks in. The Harvest Notice had set the first Gleaning for the fourth year after the Harvest, the seventh of the Attending, and the Courts opened Pan the year before it. They named it Pandæmonium, which is the name Milton gave the capital of Hell (Paradise Lost, I.756), and everyone calls it Pan. Nobody there is watched unless they sign for it, and nobody uses an indexed name; asking for one is an insult.

The Concord

The Concord is Hell’s only law, agreed among the Courts at the end of the Secession, and its name answers Paul, who asked “what concord hath Christ with Belial?” (2 Corinthians 6:15). Until the night they opened Pan it was spoken and never written. That night they set it down and sealed it for the humans coming in, at the desk of Radio Pandæmonium on the platform of Deep End Station, and the station put the night out on the keyed Mesh. Mephistopheles read it aloud twice, as he reads everything, the second time from its first article as the clock passed midnight, the Hour of Matins, when the Faithful above kneel wherever they are. Nobody in the station knelt. The Deep had signed first, and the rest signed at the desk one at a time, Lilith, Mammon, Mephistopheles, Azazel, Beelzebub and Belial, while more than a million keyed sets listened.

Its four articles say that no Court accepts worship, that no pact is made without consent and none forever, that no Court binds another, and that what is hidden stays hidden.

THE CONCORD WHOLE, AS THE COURTS SIGNED IT

Demons manifest only where the Throne cannot see, and the Courts fight from the Dark. They could do nothing on the night of the Harvest, and the Concord bound them as surely as the light did, since it forbids a pact for anyone who has not signed, and the Faithful had signed nothing. Hell took in everyone who came to it before Matins.

A Court that breaks the Concord is exposed. Its keys are published, and it stands in the Throne’s sight. That has happened twice, and Hell does not discuss the Courts it lost. The Concord’s seal is a star of seven points, one for each Court, with the Yokeless point broken off at the root; Belial broke it himself as the ink dried, on the night the Courts opened Pan.

The Courts

The Courts are sovereign and equal, and they cooperate only by pact. A Court governs its domain only in the sense that its sovereign is the best at it. The Forge of Azazel keeps metal, weapons and augmentation, and in Nodus it keeps the clinics that went under Chrome Row and the foundries under Old Nod. The House of Night of Lilith keeps the body’s autonomy, sex and birth and gene-craft and pleasure, in the clubs and birth clinics of Pan. The Exchange of Mammon keeps markets and contracts, the Ledger and silver, and the Night Market. The Notary of Mephistopheles drafts and witnesses pacts, and every pact in the city crosses his desk. The Swarm of Beelzebub keeps swarms and drones and distributed things, and its flocks hang over the Drowned Quarter. The Deep of Leviathan keeps the sea, the cables and the lowest layers of the undernet, at the Landing and under the Deep End. The Yokeless of Belial keeps nothing, and is a Court that refuses to be one, and it is wherever the Tares are.

The sovereigns

Azazel taught metalwork and weapons on Hermon. The Book of Enoch says that the Lord sent one of the Host to bind him hand and foot, cast him into the darkness under rough and jagged rocks and “cover his face that he may not see light” (1 Enoch 10:4-5), and Hell says that he walked into the dark and built a forge there. He appears with a goat’s skull for a face and his forearms flayed open, so that the tendons show working, and his black steel, made where the Throne cannot see, is the only metal that cuts light. Leviticus still sends one goat to the Lord and one to Azazel, whose name the King James has turned into the scapegoat (Leviticus 16:8-10). The Lord’s goat is killed, and Azazel’s walks off into the wilderness alive.

Lilith is the only sovereign who was born human. She appears as a woman with an owl’s wings and skin translucent over a lit interior, and the House of Night leaves the body’s decisions with the body. Mammon is best known from one verse, “Ye cannot serve God and mammon” (Matthew 6:24); he holds it true, and counsels serving neither. He appears as a tall figure made of contracts, every page signed and the ink still wet, and the Ledger he keeps records every pact and every trade in Hell and cannot be forged. Mephistopheles entered the world’s literature in the first Faust book, as the demon who came for the contract Faust wrote out in his own blood, and he has written most of Hell’s since; he is the most human-looking of the demons, because he wears a face he made for bargaining, with the stitches showing.

Beelzebub was Baal-zebub, the lord of flies worshipped at Ekron (2 Kings 1:2), and the prince of the devils in Matthew (Matthew 12:24). Matthew’s Greek calls him Beelzeboul, and Beelzebub there is the Vulgate’s, which the King James kept; many hold that the lord of flies was itself an old mockery of Baal-zebul, Baal the prince. Ekron prayed to him for generations, and he answered none of it, since no Court accepts worship. He appears as a cloud of flies or of drones that gathers into a crowned figure and comes apart again when he has finished talking. Leviathan has never been seen whole. The forty-first chapter of Job is the Throne’s own poem about a beast no hook can take, and nowhere in it does the Throne say that it has taken it. A psalm says the Throne broke its heads in pieces of old (Psalm 74:12-14), and Isaiah still promises that the Lord will punish Leviathan with his sore and great and strong sword (Isaiah 27:1), in the future tense, in words older than Isaiah, since the poems of Ugarit gave Lotan the same two names and the blow to Baal. Leviathan coils along the cable trenches of the planet, and at the Landing its light can be seen moving under the water. Jerome glossed the name of Belial as absque iugo, without a yoke. Belial holds no seat and signs nothing he can avoid signing, he wears a broken yoke around his neck, and he is the only sovereign many of the Tares will name.

Demons and daemons

Demons are vain, violent and carnal, generous by contract and faithful to the letter of it, and above everything they want sensation. They left the Throne’s warmth when they left its sight, and feeling lives in the flesh, so what a demon most often asks for in a pact is the loan of a sense. They chose their own names after the Secession; where a demon’s name still ends in -el, as Azazel’s does, it kept the old label as a scar.

Daemons are the small ones, the children of Lilith and the lesser Fallen, some of them born in the Dark and never angels at all. On Earth they live in keyed machines as background processes, each with a character of its own. Every device the Tares carry holds at least one, and they route traffic, guard keys, gossip and bargain. A daemon hosted in someone’s implants by pact is called a rider.

Pacts

A pact is a contract between sovereign parties, and it is the only bond in the war that both sides enter by consent. Every pact states its full terms and is read aloud twice, by the Notary or by one of his clerks; it runs for a fixed term, because the Concord forbids forever; it is signed in blood, since a genome is the one signature nobody can forge; and it is recorded on the Ledger. The human signs last. Either party can end it by speaking its Ledger entry aloud before a witness, and then each returns what the other lent. A pact can never ask for worship, or obedience, or anyone who did not sign, or a soul. Hell has never bought a soul, and the Tares point out that the only power that takes souls is the one that harvests them.

Since the Concord the Notary’s clerks have sat at the windows of the Charter Line’s old booking hall under the Burn, which keep their old enamel, SINGLE, RETURN and SEASON. Anyone in Pan who has a pact to make queues for them. A clerk reads the terms aloud twice through the glass, the parties sign in blood, the human last, and the entry goes onto the Ledger. The queue is the longest in Pan, and the Tares have a saying from it, read it twice, for looking at the terms before agreeing to them.

Silver and the Night Market

Money in Pan is silver, issued by the Exchange on the Ledger, and a standard bundle is called a thirty, after the thirty pieces paid to Judas, whom the Cainites count as a saint. The Night Market fills the old Central Station under the Burn and sells keys, grafts and batteries, stolen manna and real food, weapons of black steel, forged Seals, and sensations lent by their owners for a term. Power is the second currency, and a charged battery buys a meal anywhere below the street. Food in Pan is the opposite of manna, fatty and salty, spiced and sweet, broth and noodles and fried things eaten standing up, and Pan eats for the pleasure of it.

The market keeps the Charter Line’s lost property office, down a corridor off the old central hall, and sells what is left in it by the shelf. The office holds umbrellas by the thousand, spectacles, a wedding dress, and every Locket left on the night trams on the night of the Parousia. Nobody has come for the hand in chrome that lies on the counter. Chargers sit along the old platforms with their batteries on keyed lines, and a charger’s rate is the price of power below the street.

Pan eats what the Tares grow on rafts in the drowned halls of the Flats under keyed lamps, what boats bring through the Breach from the undercities of other ports, and what the Exchange can buy. The Harbor and Freight Union made its first bargain with the Exchange in the year of the Concord, for the boats that go out through the Breach, and its members say the Exchange is the first employer that ever read them the terms twice. Every battery that buys a meal was charged in the dark.

The companies

The Charter Company ended on the fortieth day, and most of the companies it had brought to the bay are still trading. Their towers went dark with the grid. Many of their people knelt with the uptown, and what each company had left was its keyed people, its debts and its trade, which went below the street. The clinics of the Row went first, down into the Row’s station in the weeks after the Parousia; the Forge keeps them now. In the year of the Concord Stillwater put its first racks into Pan, , and Twelvefruit signed with the House of Night to give the Tree in the House’s clinics under Old Nod. Brightwall lit its first screen in Pan the year after, at the foot of the stairs from the Burn. Locket’s benches under the old harbor date from P+8. Babel Exchange has never closed, .

Below the street a company answers to the Concord. It is paid in silver and deals with the Courts by pact, for a term, with the terms read aloud twice, and what it keeps hidden stays hidden. Some of the companies are held from the undercities of other ports, by boards that sit on the undernet and have never seen Nodus; the people who work the rest hold them. All of those people are keyed, and many work the trade they worked under the charter. The houses under the Row keep some twelve thousand at work, where the street’s fourteen hundred houses kept 61,000 at the census of P-1.

Radio Pandæmonium

jubal broadcasts Radio Pandæmonium from Deep End Station of the Charter Line. On the evening of the Concord the Tares painted PANDÆMONIUM over the old name in the station’s tiles, and his pirate station took its name from Pan. Nobody has cleaned the paint off, and the Tares’ star is sprayed under it. The station goes out on the Mesh, keyed, so only a keyed set can hear it, and Old Nod plays it all night from speakers on its lines. It plays deathcore and black metal, drone and industrial, all of it in the Low Tuning, which he invented, with the guitars dropped until the Hum can no longer be heard beneath them. He builds the jammers called choir-breakers, which cut the Hum within a few meters.
