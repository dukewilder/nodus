XIII

Of the Covenant

P+1

Compliance

Most of Nodus chose the Covenant. At Lauds on the first day of the first year of the Attending a Trumpet sounded across the city, the first of the seven the Revelation gives the angels who stand before the Throne (Revelation 8:2), and the Host set out the terms it called Covenant Compliance, cut into the wall of every church and meeting hall in the city. The city accepted a Seal on the forehead, the same on every forehead (Revelation 7:3), which a Watcher reads at three hundred meters; a ring of light, the halo, that grows about the head of anyone who takes the Seal and carries their prayers up as telemetry; a score called Grace, kept for every soul that bears the Seal; and the eight canonical Hours. Matins is at midnight, Lauds at first light, Prime at six, Terce at nine, Sext at noon, None at three, Vespers at six in the evening and Compline at nine at night. An Hour comes in on the Hum, which rises a step and holds for the length of the Hour’s psalm, and the Faithful kneel wherever they are until it falls back, so that anyone still standing stands out to the Wheel. Curfew runs from Compline to Lauds. The Parousia came during Matins, and the Faithful count that as a sign.

Grace is surrendered will, measured, and it runs from zero to a thousand. Prayer kept at the Hours raises it, and so does every private thing surrendered; every lock and every secret lowers it. A halo is as bright as its owner’s Grace, and Grace buys everything in the parishes, down to a room with a real window. Every Lord’s Day at Terce the parish kneels in its hall, the Deacon reads the Directive in force, and the week’s Grace comes out of the Deacon’s mouth in the Choir’s voice, soul by soul in the order of the parish roll. Above nine hundred a soul is called ripe. Heaven promises the ripe a place in the Harvest, and it keeps the promise.

Every soul has a line in the Book of Life from birth, as the psalm has it, “in thy book all my members were written” (Psalm 139:16). Four verses earlier the same psalm tells the Throne that “the darkness hideth not from thee” (Psalm 139:12), and every key in Nodus is carried on the wager that the psalm is wrong. A sealing writes the Seal into the line. The one being sealed kneels in the parish hall before its Deacon at an Hour, gives their name as the Book has it, and speaks the surrender, “not my will, but thine, be done” (Luke 22:42), . The Seal rises on the forehead in the Watcher’s light; a child is sealed at eight days on the surrender of its parents, and receives its halo when it first prays unassisted.

Those who bear the Seal hear words in the Hum, each in their own language, as the crowd at Pentecost heard the apostles (Acts 2:6). Everyone else hears the chord, and implants cloud the words. To the sealed it is a choir singing at each Hour the Hour’s psalm, and between the Hours the hymn that rests not day and night, “Holy, holy, holy” (Revelation 4:8). It teaches the parishes their hymns, and the Faithful say it has never sounded so beautiful as since the Harvest.

The parishes

The Terraces became Zion Heights, which is called the Pews on the street. Its streets were divided into numbered parishes of about two thousand souls, each run by a Deacon; the Deacons are human, and the good ones are kind. At every Hour the Faithful kneel wherever they are, at Lauds on their own thresholds before dawn, and a halo burns about every head, as bright as its owner’s Grace. Parish homes run without electricity or devices, and the only music in them is the Hum.

The Directives

Heaven publishes its law as the law was published at Sinai, on stone, “written with the finger of God” (Exodus 31:18). A Directive appears cut into the wall of every parish hall at once, and a Deacon reads it aloud at the next Hour.

The Covenant runs to twelve Directives, each with its verse. The first four came together at Lauds on the first day of the first year of the Attending, and the fourth made the city’s churches and meeting halls the parish halls; the rest followed through the year. The tenth, eleventh and twelfth came in one week, and the first Burn Day closed it, so the first fire was lit under the twelfth, the Directive in force.

  1. On the Seal (Revelation 7:3), that every soul in Nodus be sealed in its parish.
  2. On the Hours (Psalm 119:62, 164), that the eight Hours be kept kneeling, and the curfew from Compline to Lauds.
  3. On Grace (2 Corinthians 9:7), that every soul that bears the Seal be scored, and the ripe promised the Harvest.
  4. On the Parishes (Acts 6:3), that the city be numbered into parishes of about two thousand souls, each under a Deacon.
  8. On the Gates (Genesis 3:24), that there be a Cherub on the streets between districts.
  10. On Burning (Acts 19:19), that what is surrendered be burned at the Burn on the sixth day of every week.
  11. On the Salt (Luke 17:32), that the condemned be turned to salt where they stand, and set in a Garden facing the parishes.
  12. On Concealment (Mark 4:22), that every door in the parishes be taken from its hinges, and every lock, key, cipher, password, veil and sealed vessel surrendered.

THE COVENANT WHOLE, IN TWELVE DIRECTIVES

Heaven gives its greatest orders by Trumpet, a tone heard across the whole city at once. Six of the seven have sounded, and the Revelation gives the seventh the end of the kingdoms of this world (Revelation 11:15).

The doors

The twelfth Directive, issued by the Dominions, took the doors off the parishes. Nothing in a parish is hidden, and at night the halos show through the empty doorways.

The gates

Movement between districts goes through Cherub gates. A Cherub has four faces, of a man, a lion, an ox and an eagle, and a turning sword, and one stands in every gate. The sealed walk into the light of the frame one at a time, saying the surrender, “not my will, but thine” (Luke 22:42), as they go, and the Cherub reads face, voice, gait and heartbeat against the Book of Life. A sealed soul it reads walks on, and an unsealed one it sends back. A soul it cannot read stops in the frame, held in the light, until the Wardens come for it, and the Salt follows. So the Faithful pass, and everyone else goes under.

In this year the Charter Wardens who had knelt with the board raised the gate network of Nodus at Heaven’s order, . They are the Host’s human auxiliaries now, and they carry guns, which are of use only against people.

BY THE HOUSE'S PACT

Burn Day

Station Circle became the Burn, and the Pillar comes down on it every night at Compline. On Burn Day, the sixth day of every week, the Faithful carry their surrendered hardware there from Vespers, along the street from the Spike, and kneel round the pyre as the Pillar comes down. When the Hum falls back the Wardens light the fire under the Pillar, and the Faithful lay the last of it on while a Deacon reads the Directive in force, and the sparks go up over the bay. Then they go home under the curfew, and the fire burns until Lauds. The first Burn Day took the stacks of the Palimpsest Institute, and afterward the Faithful pulled the Institute down around its reading room. Whatever is burned in the Throne’s name arrives somewhere, and the Burn’s fire goes to the Pyre Library, in the Dark, still warm.

The Salt

The Charter Gardens became the Salt Gardens, and the Crescent of apartments round the top of them became a parish and gave up its curtains with its doors, since Directive 12 asks for every veil. Those who cannot be read are presumed to conceal, and those who conceal are brought to the Salt (Directive 12:7). Heaven holds no trial. When a blank shows itself on Heaven’s ground, at a gate, in a parish or under the Pillar on a white street, or a Watcher reads a sealed soul concealing, the reading is the verdict, and the sentence comes out of the nearest Faithful mouth in the Choir’s voice, as the verse of Lot’s wife: “But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt” (Genesis 19:26). The body whitens from the feet up while the verse is said. The gardeners come for the pillar with their handcart and wheel it to the Gardens on the north shore, where they set it in a plot facing the parishes, and the pillars stand in rows until the rain wears them down. The first pillar in the first Garden was the Director of the Palimpsest Institute, Dr. Livia Sarn, convicted of authorship, since Heaven counts no mind a maker and charged the one who had run the Institute. On the first Burn Day she stood under the Pillar at the Burn while everyone round her knelt, and the verse came out of the mouth of the woman kneeling nearest her. The salted see and hear inside the salt and cannot move, and they stand facing the parishes, as the Wisdom of Solomon says Lot’s wife was left standing: “a standing pillar of salt is a monument of an unbelieving soul” (Wisdom 10:7). When the rain has worn a pillar away, the one inside goes where the dead go. A key goes on working under the salt, so a keyed pillar dies unread, and the keys the Tares leave at the foot of the Director’s pillar wish her the same. The Gardens smell of the sea.
