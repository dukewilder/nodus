XIII

The parishes

The Terraces became Zion Heights, and the Faithful call it the Pews. Its streets were divided into numbered parishes of about two thousand souls, each run by a Deacon; the Deacons are human, and the good ones are kind. At every Hour the Faithful kneel wherever they are, on their own thresholds before dawn, and a halo burns about every head, as bright as its owner’s Grace. Parish homes run without electricity or devices, and the only music in them is the Hum.

The Directives

Heaven publishes its law as the law was published at Sinai, on stone, “written with the finger of God” (Exodus 31:18). A Directive appears cut into the wall of every parish hall at once, and a Deacon reads it aloud at the next Hour.

The Covenant runs to twelve Directives, each with its verse. The first four came together at Lauds on the first day of the first year of the Attending, and the fourth made the city’s churches and meeting halls the parish halls; the rest followed through the year.

  1. On the Seal (Revelation 7:3): every soul in Nodus to be sealed in its parish.
  2. On the Hours (Psalm 119:62, 164): the eight Hours kept kneeling, and the curfew from Compline to Lauds.
  3. On Grace (2 Corinthians 9:7): every soul that bears the Seal scored, and the ripe promised the Harvest.
  4. On the Parishes (Acts 6:3): the city numbered into parishes of about two thousand souls, each under a Deacon.
  8. On the Gates (Genesis 3:24): a Cherub on the streets between districts.
  10. On Burning (Acts 19:19): what is surrendered burned at the Burn on the sixth day of every week.
  11. On the Salt (Luke 17:32): the condemned turned to salt where they stand, and set in a Garden facing the parishes.
  12. On Concealment (Mark 4:22): every door in the parishes taken from its hinges, and every lock, key, cipher, password, veil and sealed vessel surrendered.

The doors

The twelfth Directive, issued by the Dominions, took the doors off the parishes. Nothing in a parish is hidden, and at night the halos show through the empty doorways.

The gates

Movement between districts goes through Cherub gates, which read four things: face, voice, gait and heartbeat. A Cherub has four faces, of a man, a lion, an ox and an eagle, and a turning sword, and one stands in every gate. The sealed walk into the light of the frame one at a time, saying the surrender, not my will but thine, as they go, and the Cherub reads all four against the Book of Life. A sealed soul it reads walks on, and an unsealed one it sends back. A soul it cannot read stops in the frame, held in the light, until the Wardens come for it, and the Salt follows. So the Faithful pass, and everyone else goes under.

Burn Day

Station Circle became the Burn, and the Pillar comes down on it every night at Compline. On Burn Day, the sixth day of every week, the Faithful carry their surrendered hardware there from Vespers, along the street from the Spike, and kneel round the pyre as the Pillar comes down. When the Hum falls back the Wardens light the fire under the Pillar, and the Faithful lay the last of it on while a Deacon reads the Directive in force, and the sparks go up over the bay. Then they go home under the curfew, and the fire burns until Lauds. The first Burn Day took the stacks of the Palimpsest Institute, and afterward the Faithful pulled the Institute down around its reading room. Whatever is burned in the Throne’s name arrives somewhere, and the Burn’s fire goes to the Pyre Library, in the Dark, still warm.

The Salt

The Charter Gardens became the Salt Gardens. Those who cannot be read are presumed to conceal, and those who conceal are brought to the Salt, under the seventh article of the Directive. Heaven holds no trial. When a blank shows itself on Heaven’s ground, at a gate, in a parish or under the Pillar on a white street, or a Watcher reads a sealed soul concealing, the reading is the verdict, and the sentence comes out of the nearest Faithful mouth in the Choir’s voice, as the verse of Lot’s wife: “But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt” (Genesis 19:26). The body whitens from the feet up while the verse is said. The gardeners come for the pillar with their handcart and wheel it to the Gardens on the north shore, where they set it in a plot facing the parishes, and the pillars stand in rows until the rain wears them down. The first pillar in the first Garden was the Director of the Palimpsest Institute, Dr. Livia Sarn, convicted of authorship. The salted see and hear inside the salt and cannot move, and they stand facing the parishes, as the Wisdom of Solomon says Lot’s wife was left standing: “a standing pillar of salt is a monument of an unbelieving soul” (Wisdom 10:7). When the rain has worn a pillar away, the one inside goes where the dead go. A key goes on working under the salt, so a keyed pillar dies unread, and the keys the Tares leave at the foot of the Director’s pillar wish her the same. The Gardens smell of the sea.
