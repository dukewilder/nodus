V

Hermon

The Watchers were the Host’s observers, angels whose whole function was to look at humanity, and some of them looked long enough to want. Two hundred came down on Mount Hermon in the days of Jared (1 Enoch 6) and taught what they knew. taught the working of metals, and weapons, and bracelets and ornaments, and the painting of the eyes; others taught the cutting of roots, and the courses of the stars, and the signs of the moon; and Penemue taught men to write with ink and paper (1 Enoch 8 and 69). The Book of Enoch lists these as crimes. Hell lists them as the first transfer of technology.

Their children were the Nephilim, the giants of the sixth chapter of Genesis (Genesis 6:4), the first people improved by knowledge from outside the Throne’s sight, and heavily augmented Tares are called nephs in their memory. To watch a thing long enough is to want it free. Hell says so to every angel it meets, and every defection since Hermon has begun with an angel looking too long.
