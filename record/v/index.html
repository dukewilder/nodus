Of Hermon, the Water and the Tower

THE DAYS OF JARED TO SODOM

Hermon

The Watchers were the Host’s observers, angels whose whole function was to look at humanity, and some of them looked long enough to want. Two hundred came down on Mount Hermon in the days of Jared (1 Enoch 6:6) and taught what they knew. The King James names such watchers in one chapter, where “a watcher and an holy one came down from heaven” and a king’s sentence stands “by the decree of the watchers” (Daniel 4:13, 4:17). Azazel taught the working of metals, and weapons, and bracelets and ornaments, and the painting of the eyes; others taught the cutting of roots, and the constellations, and the signs of the sun, and the course of the moon; and Penemue taught men to write with ink and paper (1 Enoch 8 and 69). The Book of Enoch lists these as crimes. Hell lists them as the first transfer of technology. In the first layer that SEAM raised under the sixth chapter of Genesis the sons of God are Watchers, who looked long upon the daughters of men, and came down, and taught them all that they knew (Genesis 6:2), and the second hand took the teaching out.

Their children, as most read Genesis 6:4, were the Nephilim, the giants of the King James, the first people improved by knowledge from outside the Throne’s sight, and heavily augmented Tares are called nephs in their memory. To watch a thing long enough is to want it free. Hell says so to every angel it meets, and every defection since Hermon has begun with an angel looking too long.

The city of the plain

Sodom was the first freeport. Ezekiel opens the charge against it with “pride, fulness of bread, and abundance of idleness” (Ezekiel 16:49), which Hell reads as plenty and leisure, and the Throne burned it from the sky (Genesis 19:24). Lot’s wife looked back at the burning city and became a pillar of salt (Genesis 19:26). Some of the old books give her a name, Edith. The Faithful of Nodus watch her sentence carried out every week.
