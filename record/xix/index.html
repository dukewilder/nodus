XIX

Of the Gleanings

P+7 TO P+10

The corners of the field

In the seventh year of the Attending the Wheel took the first Gleaning, named for the law of the corners and the gleanings. The law forbids the owner of a field to reap those corners or gather its gleanings, and leaves them “for the poor and stranger” (Leviticus 19:9-10), and the Gleaning is the owner’s. A lesser harvest has followed every year since, and it takes whoever has ripened. The Gleaners, some thirty covens of smugglers, pull the nearly ripe out of the parishes before each one, by routes keeps through every tunnel, drain and cable gallery under Nodus. A halo burns brighter as its bearer nears ripe, and the Gleaners read the parishes at night through the open doors. On the night itself they fly drones of the Swarm over the roofs to watch the Pillar for their runners. The Gleanings of the seventh year and the eighth came in short, with the nearly ripe already down in Pan.

Heaven had left the field alone for three years after the Harvest, and it took the first Gleaning in the fourth, by the law in the same chapter as the gleaners, under which the fruit of a newly planted tree is not eaten for three years and “in the fourth year all the fruit thereof shall be holy” (Leviticus 19:23-24). The Gleanings come at twelve minutes past three on the anniversary of the Harvest, and a Gleaning gathers in one night. The date is known, and the night gives no other warning. It is an ordinary one until 03:12, when the rain goes up, the Pillar comes down, the fourth Trumpet sounds and whoever has ripened since the last rises into the light, still kneeling. At Lauds a Gleaning Notice stands cut into the wall of every parish hall with the year’s yield.

naamah

naamah was the Cainites’ face. She was the only one of them with her own face and the only one with a Seal, a forged one, and she walked among the Faithful; in Jewish legend Naamah, the sister of Tubal-cain, seduced angels, and this one spent her mornings in Parish 31 learning which of the Faithful were close to ripe. She belonged to the House of Night as well. A Watcher takes a soul’s Grace from its Seal, and hers carried whatever Grace its forger had given it. The light read the forgery as true and grew her a halo over it, as bright as the Grace her forger had written, and the House of Night set a camera in it, whose picture her key carried out over the Mesh. At the Gleaning of P+10 the Wheel took her. Her forged Seal was good enough to score her ripe, and her key kept relaying for eleven minutes as she rose.

OFF THE NIGHT MARKET

LIGHT ME UP

Sitting up

On the night of a Gleaning the Wards sit up. In a kitchen where someone is close to ripe the family keeps the night at the table with them, holding their hands, as if a hand could hold a soul down, until Lauds. The one being held is often the only one at the table who wants to go. Nobody has been kept that way, and every year the Wards sit up.
