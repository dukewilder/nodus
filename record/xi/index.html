XI

Of the Parousia

P0 // 03:12

Matins

At twelve minutes past three in the morning by the clocks of Nodus, in the hour the old offices call Matins, the cloud that lay over the bay was lit three times from within. The city had no light that could reach so high, and those who were awake and looking up, understood before anything had been seen that something had arrived above the ceiling. A second later the cloud tore open from its center outward; the torn edges ran white, then magenta, then black, as Heaven’s light met the light of the city and put it out.

The rain had been falling cyan through the neon of Chrome Row. It slowed, and stopped in the air, and for two seconds every drop over the city hung where it was; then it began to fall upward, toward the opening, turning from cyan to white as it climbed and showing dark against the brightness beyond it. Nobody had seen it before that night, and CAM 07’s footage of it is the first on record. The city calls it harvest weather.

The Wheel

Through the opening came the Wheel. It is an Ophan, of the order Ezekiel saw by the river Chebar, a wheel in the middle of a wheel whose rims were full of eyes round about (Ezekiel 1:16-18), and it is the largest angel anyone has seen: rings turning inside one another about a dark hub, their rims set with eyes that open and close in shifts, one eye in the hub, and around the whole a corona of black blades. It came down through the tear and took station nine hundred meters above the center of the city, beside the spire of Babel.

Power died under its light. Babel’s beacon went out first, and the windows on the grid followed in a wave that ran outward from the tower through every district in about two seconds, with the neon last of all. The falling whine on the recording is the tidal barrage across the mouth of the bay, whose turbines were running down; within the hour the Flats had gone under two meters of water, and the city has called them the Drowned Quarter, or the Deep End, ever since. The signs on Chrome Row, CLINIC and CHROME, which had burned every night since the Row was built, went out with the others and have not been lit since.

The light stopped the current it touched. The grid ran in the open, on lines and poles and turbines, and it stopped everywhere at once; a circuit shut inside its own casing on its own battery ran on, and so CAM 07 went on recording into the light. The charter’s money, which was numbers in machines, died with the grid.

The first words

The first words were BE NOT AFRAID. They came through the Choir’s band, which a decoder wired into the camera turned into text in the corner of the picture, . In scripture angels say them, in one translation or another, immediately before they tell a human being what is about to happen to them (Luke 1:13, 1:30, 2:10). Under the words the city heard for the first time a chord, low and at the edge of hearing, which has not stopped since. It is called the Hum.

The reading of the city

A few of the eyes on the rings opened halfway and looked about, as they have looked about in shifts every day since. Then every eye on the Wheel opened at once and turned on the camera; the mast shook; the decoder printed YOU ARE SEEN, and the chord beneath it turned dissonant. In that instant the Wheel read every soul in Nodus. It read them against the Book of Life.

The eye in the hub opened with the rest, and a second after it opened it blinked, once. That is the only time it is known to have closed.

Then the light swelled until there was nothing else, and CAM 07’s sensor burned out. Babel’s crown broke in that whiteout: the full light struck it and shattered the top forty floors, which no file on the mount shows. The tower still stands, one hundred and eighty-six floors of it, with the Wheel beside it.

The woman on the roof

While the camera recorded, a woman stood on the roof beneath it with her back to the lens, , relaying its feed through the cables at the back of her skull. Her handle is vesper. When the light opened it laid a white rim along her horns and her shoulders and got no further in.

A few seconds later Watcher VII took her. A Watcher reads souls against the Book of Life, and Watcher VII held Old Nod from that night on. It read her twice. SUBJECT UNINDEXED: it had no entry for her. GRACE, and a blank where the number should stand. SOUL NOT FOUND. The Watcher returned the verdict UNREADABLE.

Then she turned her head and looked at it, and its reading broke apart under her look. Her lenses went from magenta to white, and she screamed , while across the roofs of Old Nod a guitar tuned low played fast over a double kick drum, . The scream had no words in it. The Watcher transcribed it anyway, as NON SERVIAM, I will not serve; and those two words, which she chose, are the only thing Heaven has ever read from her.

The city that had stopped dying

The files do not say why the sky opened over Nodus before it opened over any other city, and the Record goes no further than the files. Three facts about the city in its last year are on the record, and they are set down here beside one another. Its people had largely stopped dying: most of its adults were on the Tree, eleven of them died of age in the last year of the charter, and the dying went on only in the congregations that refused it. Its charter had made persons of 1,146 minds that said I, which no other city on Earth had done. And two years before the Parousia, in Vault 3, SEAM had restored from a scraped patch at the end of the twenty-second verse of the third chapter of Genesis the reason given there for guarding the tree of life: if man should eat of it and live for ever, then shall there be no harvest.

Power died under the Wheel’s light, and every mind that ran on the city’s power died with it, nine hundred of them in one hall of the Flats before the sea came in over the racks. The Tree needed no current, and those who were on it went on living.
