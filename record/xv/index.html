XV

Of the Harvest

P+3 // 03:12

What the book promised

Heaven’s own book describes a harvest. “Thrust in thy sickle, and reap: for the time is come for thee to reap; for the harvest of the earth is ripe” (Revelation 14:15). The first souls taken are its Firstfruits (Revelation 14:4). Hell says the Edit left the language as it stood and called it salvation. A Harvest gathers for days. The Wheel’s attention settles on the field and lifts what nothing holds down, the rain first, days early, then dust and paper and loose things, and on the night it lifts the ripe the Watchers have marked, still kneeling. Nobody comes back, and no bodies are found. Most of the Faithful believe that the Rapture is paradise and that their children will be safe inside it.

The Harvest came at the hour of the Parousia, three years to the second after it, and at the hour at which the Seam had been sealed two years before that. In the Revelation, the book that describes the harvest, the twelfth verse of the third chapter reads: “Him that overcometh will I make a pillar in the temple of my God, and he shall go no more out” (Revelation 3:12).

Parish 31 and its Deacon

Parish 31 stands at the southern edge of Zion Heights, facing Old Nod across one street, and in the week of the Harvest it counted 2,006 souls, of whom 311 were ripe. Its Deacon was Ruth Calder, vesper’s older sister, whose Grace was 944.

THE SHAMBLES

The night of the Harvest

The Harvest came to Parish 31 at Matins, three years to the second after the Parousia. At twelve minutes past three the parish knelt on its thresholds with the doors gone from behind it and every halo lit. Every window on the street was sealed and glowing behind its cross of stone, except one. Far off the Pillar stood on the city, the Wheel’s light narrowed into a column, and came toward them, and the roar of it grew as it came.

Watcher VII read the street. It counted the ripe, 311 of 2,006, and it narrowed onto Ruth, who turned her head toward the window beside her door, the only open window on the street, dark, with a candle burning on the sill; the Watcher followed her look, checked the window, and found nothing against it. Then the light came down the facade from above, and the prayers stopped, and every other sound with them.

At 03:12:45 the ripe rose off their thresholds, still kneeling. Ruth went up with her head turned toward the window, and the small cross she had been holding slipped from her hands and fell to the step. Over the city a horn the size of the sky groaned out, the sound scripture calls “the trump of God” (1 Thessalonians 4:16), the second of Heaven’s seven Trumpets; a choir came in on one chord and turned dissonant, and the Watcher’s ledger counted the ripe as they went. On the next step a man rose and his wife and children stayed, and the children looked up. The ones who screamed were the ones who were left.

The light moved on. The doorway was empty but for the cross, fallen on its side on the step, and the window was still open and the candle still lit. Over the weeping street the Watcher wrote its verdict, FIRSTFRUITS; Parish 31’s had gone first.

The roof

vesper had come to get her sister out, and she reached a roof in Old Nod seventeen seconds late. From there, over the white roofs of Zion Heights, she watched the Pillar standing on the city and points of light streaming up into it from every side, as the Harvest crossed Nodus parish by parish between Matins and Lauds. Parish 31’s ripe had gone first and were the highest. Her lens hunted through them and held one point, which it tagged ruth, in her own color, cutting everything else to black around it until the light was too close to separate her. Across the sky the yield of the whole Harvest came up, 88,610 measures, written there as the reaping began, since Heaven counts before it reaps. Then the point she was holding went into the column, the brackets blinked, lost, and her lenses burned from magenta to white.

By Lauds 2.1 million of Nodus’s nine million were gone.

The morning after

In the morning the city counted its empty rooms, the halls of the Pews by the book and the rest of Nodus as it could, by the places left at the table and by who did not come to work. The parishes up the slope of Zion Heights stood almost empty, and Faithful families from the Wards moved up into their terraces within the year. Parish 31, at the foot of the slope across from the Stones, had kept most of its people. Of those who were awake that night, some say that the rising made no sound at all, some that it sounded like wind in the stairwells, some that the horn went on until Lauds. At its next meeting the Harbor and Freight Union read its roll, as it does at every meeting, and twenty-two of its members did not answer. The hall has read their names at every meeting since, and nobody answers to them. After the Harvest the Union put up a row of twenty-two stones for them at the Acre, with nothing under them, and the parishes say the taken need no stones, since they are not dead.
