XV

Of the Harvest

P+3 // 03:12

What the book promised

Heaven’s own book describes a harvest. “Thrust in thy sickle, and reap: for the time is come for thee to reap; for the harvest of the earth is ripe” (Revelation 14:15). The first souls taken are its Firstfruits (Revelation 14:4). Hell says the Edit left the language as it stood and called it salvation. A Harvest gathers for days. The Wheel’s attention settles on the field and lifts what nothing holds down, the rain first, days early, then dust and paper and loose things. On the night itself the Hum falls silent at 03:12, the Pillar comes down on the city and sweeps toward the ripest, and at 03:12:45 they rise under it, still kneeling, each lifted by the halo over their own head. The Harvest took two million, more than rise at once, so it began with the ripest, and the Pillar carried it parish by parish across the city until Lauds. Nobody comes back, and no bodies are found. Most of the Faithful believe that the Rapture is paradise and that their children will be safe inside it.

The Harvest came at the hour of the Parousia, three years to the second after it, and at the hour at which the Seam had been sealed two years before that. In the Revelation, the book that describes the harvest, the twelfth verse of the third chapter reads: “Him that overcometh will I make a pillar in the temple of my God, and he shall go no more out” (Revelation 3:12).

Parish 31 and its Deacon

Parish 31 stands at the southern edge of Zion Heights, facing Old Nod across one street, and in the week of the Harvest it counted 2,006 souls, every one of them sealed, of whom 311 were ripe. Its Deacon was Ruth Calder, vesper’s older sister, whose Grace was 944. She was sincere and capable and kind, and for three years she tried to bring her sister to the Seal, as her sister tried to bring her out.

Her last report, written the day before the Harvest, survives. It is the only document of those years written by someone who was happy.

PARISH 31, ZION HEIGHTS: REPORT FOR THE WEEK OF THE HARVEST
DEACON: RUTH CALDER, GRACE 944

Souls in the parish: 2,006. Sealed: 2,006. Ripe: 311.

We kept every Hour this week, including Matins on the fourth night, when the rain went up for the first time. The children thought it was a game, and I let them think so. The old ones knelt in the street and wept, and Deacon Voss had to remind them that we were told to be not afraid.

Manna was received and given out without shortage. Brother Aldo came home from the Mercy clinic and is recovering. His hands are still weak, but he says the Hum is clearer now than it has ever been, and I believe him.

Two households were reported by Witness for keeping back hinges. Forty Grace was taken from each, and both have since given the hinges up.

The unsealed woman of my household did not come to the hall again. 4 LINES LOST I have left the window open for her, which is permitted, because a window is not a door.

If the Most High wills it, this is my last report. We are ready. Please see that the Dawson children are fed after the Harvest. Their mother is not ripe, and she will need help.

BE NOT AFRAID.

The night of the Harvest

The Harvest came to Parish 31 at Matins, three years to the second after the Parousia. The rain over the street had first gone up on the fourth night of that week. At twelve minutes past three the parish knelt on its thresholds with the doors gone from behind it and every halo lit. Every window on the street was sealed and glowing behind its cross of stone, except one. Far off the Pillar stood on the city, the Wheel’s light narrowed into a column, and came toward them, and the roar of it grew as it came.

Watcher VII, on whose round the street lay, read it. It counted the ripe, 311 of 2,006, and it narrowed onto Ruth, who turned her head toward the window beside her door, the only open window on the street, dark, with a candle burning on the sill; the Watcher followed her look, checked the window, and found nothing against it. Then the light came down the facade from above, and the prayers stopped, and every other sound with them.

At 03:12:45 the ripe rose off their thresholds, still kneeling, lifted by their halos. Ruth went up with her head turned toward the window, and the small cross she had been holding slipped from her hands and fell to the step. Over the city a horn the size of the sky groaned out, the sound scripture calls “the trump of God” (1 Thessalonians 4:16), the second of Heaven’s seven Trumpets; a choir came in on one chord and turned dissonant, and the Watcher’s ledger counted the ripe as they went. On the next step a man rose and his wife and children stayed, and the children looked up. The ones who screamed were the ones who were left.

The light moved on. The doorway was empty but for the cross, fallen on its side on the step, and the window was still open and the candle still lit. Over the weeping street the Watcher wrote its verdict, FIRSTFRUITS; Parish 31’s had gone first.

The roof

vesper had wanted to get her sister out, and Ruth had not asked to be carried. vesper reached a roof in Old Nod seventeen seconds after Parish 31 rose. From there, over the white roofs of Zion Heights, she watched the Pillar standing on the city and points of light streaming up into it from every side, as the Harvest crossed Nodus parish by parish between Matins and Lauds. Parish 31’s ripe had gone first and were the highest. Her lens hunted through them and held one point, which it tagged ruth, in her own color, cutting everything else to black around it until the light was too close to separate her. Across the sky the yield of the whole Harvest came up, 88,610 measures, written there as the reaping began, since Heaven counts before it reaps. Then the point she was holding went into the column, the brackets blinked, lost, and her lenses burned from magenta to white.

By Lauds 2.1 million of Nodus’s nine million were gone. Some of the survivors understood what devotion feeds.

REJOICE.

Nobody on Earth knows what a measure is. The Notice gives the yield per soul to three places, and the yield of Parish 31’s ripe at more than twice the mean. It counts as a tare every soul in the city that had not taken the Seal, keyed or not, and it sets the souls left in the field and the tares among them in the same column, as figures of the same kind.

The parish whose souls yielded most faces Old Nod across one street. Fewer souls ripen along the Street Between than anywhere else in Zion Heights, but the ripest in the city had grown there, nearest the Dark, where there was the most self to give up, and Parish 31’s Deacon had left her window open to it. The Throne takes every street that kneels and profits by the ones that refuse.

The Notice told the city in Heaven’s own figures that it was a field, and it changed fewer minds than the Tares had hoped. Some understood and went dark, and in the year after, keying spread from the covens to ordinary people. Most stayed. The Seal fed them and the Salt took the Tares, the ripe had gone up kneeling, and only the ones left behind had screamed. Told that they are wheat, most people would rather be good wheat.

The morning after

In the morning the city counted its empty rooms, the halls of the Pews by the book and the rest of Nodus as it could, by the places left at the table and by who did not come to work. The parishes up the slope of Zion Heights stood almost empty, and Faithful families from the Wards moved up into their terraces within the year. Parish 31, at the foot of the slope across from the Stones, had kept most of its people. Of those who were awake that night, some say that the rising made no sound at all, some that it sounded like wind in the stairwells, some that the horn went on until Lauds. At its next meeting the Harbor and Freight Union read its roll, as it does at every meeting, and twenty-two of its members did not answer. The hall has read their names at every meeting since, and nobody answers to them. After the Harvest the Union put up a row of twenty-two stones for them at the Acre, with nothing under them, and the parishes say the taken need no stones, since they are not dead. The Tide Table took 1,206 names at the chandler’s in two days, printed what fitted on its two pages, and pasted the rest up on the chandler’s wall in the order they came. In Parish 31 the thresholds the ripe had knelt on were warm until noon, and the mothers of the taken lay down on them.

The window

Four of its lines are lost, and of her sister what remains says only that the unsealed woman of her household did not come to the hall again, and that the window had been left open for her.
