XV

Of the Harvest

P+3 // 03:12

What the book promised

Heaven’s own book describes a harvest. “Thrust in thy sickle, and reap: for the time is come for thee to reap; for the harvest of the earth is ripe” (Revelation 14:15). The first souls taken are its Firstfruits (Revelation 14:4). The language was left as it stood and called salvation. A harvest gathers for days. The Wheel’s attention settles on the field and lifts what nothing holds down, the rain first, days early, then dust and paper and loose things, and on the night it lifts the ripe the Watchers have marked, still kneeling. Nobody comes back, and no bodies are found. Most of the Faithful believe that the Rapture is paradise and that their children will be safe inside it.

The Harvest came at the hour of the Parousia, three years to the second after it, and at the hour at which the Seam had been sealed two years before that. In the Revelation, the book that describes the harvest, the twelfth verse of the third chapter reads: “Him that overcometh will I make a pillar in the temple of my God, and he shall go no more out” (Revelation 3:12).

Parish 31 and its Deacon

Parish 31 stands at the southern edge of Zion Heights, facing Old Nod across one street, and in the week of the Harvest it counted 2,006 souls, of whom 311 were ripe. Its Deacon was Ruth Calder, vesper’s older sister, whose Grace was 944.

The night of the Harvest

The Harvest came to Parish 31 at Matins, three years to the second after the Parousia. At twelve minutes past three the parish knelt on its thresholds with the doors gone from behind it and every halo lit. Every window on the street was sealed and glowing behind its cross of stone, except one. Far off the Pillar stood on the city, the Wheel’s light narrowed into a column, and came toward them, and the roar of it grew as it came.

Watcher VII read the street. It counted the ripe, 311 of 2,006, and it took Ruth, who turned her head toward the window beside her door, the only open window on the street, dark, with a candle burning on the sill; the Watcher followed her look, checked the window, and found nothing against it. Then the light came down the facade from above, and the prayers stopped, and every other sound with them.

At 03:12:45:00 the ripe rose off their thresholds, still kneeling. Ruth went up with her head turned toward the window, and the small cross she had been holding slipped from her hands and fell to the step. Over the city a horn the size of the sky groaned out, the sound scripture calls the trump of God (1 Thessalonians 4:16); a choir came in on one chord and turned dissonant, and the Watcher’s ledger counted the ripe as they went. On the next step a man rose and his wife and children stayed, and the children looked up. The ones who screamed were the ones who were left.

The light moved on. The doorway was empty but for the cross, fallen on its side on the step, and the window was still open and the candle still lit. Over the weeping street the Watcher wrote its verdict, FIRSTFRUITS; Parish 31’s had gone first.

The roof

vesper had come to get her sister out, and she reached a roof in Old Nod seventeen seconds late. From there, over the white roofs of Zion Heights, her lenses recorded the Pillar standing on the city and points of light streaming up into it from every side, as the Harvest crossed Nodus parish by parish between Matins and Lauds. Parish 31’s ripe had gone first and were the highest. Her lens hunted through them and held one point, which it tagged ruth, in her own color, cutting everything else to black around it until the light was too close to separate her. Across the sky the yield of the whole Harvest came up, 88,610 measures. Then the point she was holding went into the column, the brackets blinked, lost, and her lenses burned from magenta to white.
