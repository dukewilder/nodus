The company that held the charter and ran Nodus as a board runs a firm. It has been gone since the fortieth day after the Parousia. A consortium of cable companies and a sovereign fund formed it to hold the charter, a coastal state leased it the bay and the land round it for ninety-nine years and gave up its law there, and for nineteen years the city had a chairman where other cities have a mayor.

It lived on rent. The carriers paid it to land their cables, the companies that came for the light law paid it for their floors, and the Wards paid it by the month for flats on grids of its own, numbered. It built Babel Exchange and took a share of every trade there, and it built the Charter Line, the airfield, the Acre, the Company’s schools, High Meadow, the Charter Gardens on the north shore, which are the Salt Gardens now, and the sign on a roof in the Stones that told the cable ships the city’s name. It kept the peace with its own force, the Charter Wardens, kept the charter’s roll at the Registry on the Great Avenues, and in nineteen years held no election, since the charter asked for none.

The city saw the Company give way once, in P-8, when the Harbor and Freight Union stopped the port for twenty-three days over the night shift and the Company settled.

Charter House turned white in the forty days after the Parousia. The board knelt inside it on the fortieth day, and the charter was not spoken of again. The Company had said nothing from the first morning on. Its lease runs to P+80, sixty-six years past the present, and nobody has come for the rent. Most of the companies it brought to the bay have outlived it.
