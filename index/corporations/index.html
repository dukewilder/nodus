CHARTER HOUSE, UPTOWN

The Nodus Charter Company

HELD BY
The carriers that laid the cables, and a sovereign fund from abroad that held the larger share.
RUN BY
A board of twelve under a chairman, Julian Marrow, on the top floor of Charter House. Marrow knelt with the board on the fortieth day, and he is a deacon of the Registry’s parish now, under the dead board of the roll he kept.
TRADE
The charter, with the land on a lease of ninety-nine years and the city’s housing, transit, schools, roll and peace.
STAFF
About 104,000 at the census of P-1, most of them teachers in its schools and fitters who kept its flats, with the Line’s people and the Wardens counted under their own names.
FORCE
The Charter Wardens.
ENDED
On the fortieth day, when the board knelt in Charter House. The lease runs to P+80, and nobody collects the rent.

The company that held the charter and ran Nodus as a board runs a firm. It has been gone since the fortieth day after the Parousia. A consortium of cable companies and a sovereign fund formed it to hold the charter, a coastal state leased it the bay and the land round it for ninety-nine years and gave up its law there, and for nineteen years the city had a chairman where other cities have a mayor.

It lived on rent. The carriers paid it to land their cables, the companies that came for the light law paid it for their floors, and the Wards paid it by the month for flats on grids of its own, numbered. It built Babel Exchange and took a share of every trade there, and it built the Charter Line, the airfield, the Acre, the Company’s schools, High Meadow, the Charter Gardens on the north shore, which are the Salt Gardens now, and the sign on a roof in the Stones that told the cable ships the city’s name. It kept the peace with its own force, the Charter Wardens, kept the charter’s roll at the Registry on the Great Avenues, and in nineteen years held no election, since the charter asked for none.

The city saw the Company give way once, in P-8, when the Harbor and Freight Union stopped the port for twenty-three days over the night shift and the Company settled.

Charter House turned white in the forty days after the Parousia. The board knelt inside it on the fortieth day, and the charter was not spoken of again. The Company had said nothing from the first morning on. Its lease runs to P+80, sixty-six years past the present, and nobody has come for the rent. Most of the companies it brought to the bay have outlived it.

Babel Exchange

HELD BY
Its partners who went dark. The Charter Company’s share lapsed with the Company.
RUN BY
Its partners, from the floors nearest the vault.
TRADE
Traffic on the undernet, sold by the second for silver, and room near the vault, priced by how near, the lowest floors still the dearest.
STAFF
About four hundred traders and line engineers, all of them keyed.
FORCE
Blackgate by contract, and the Tares, who hold the floors it trades on.
UNDER THE CHARTER
14,000 on its floors at the census of P-1, and a hundred and eighty-eight minds on the charter’s roll pricing the traffic by the millisecond.

The exchange in the tallest building on Earth, which has never closed. It sells traffic on the undernet by the second, for silver, and room near the vault is still priced by how near, the lowest floors dearest. Every cable that comes ashore at the Landing ends in that vault, six levels under the tower, and the undernet that joins Pan to the undercities of the world runs through it. Since P+9 Babel Exchange has traded on the floors the Tares hold, with the Babel Line in the middle floors over its head.

The Charter Company built the tower to two hundred and twenty-six floors as a boast, named it for one, and opened it in P-11. Traffic was sold on its floors by the millisecond, priced by a hundred and eighty-eight minds on the charter’s roll, and most of the planet’s passed within two kilometers of it. A trading house that wanted its orders in ahead of every other on Earth took room near the vault, the glass towers round the spire filled with every company that needed to be near the cables, and Brightwall’s screens wrapped the lower forty floors as the face of Babel.

At the Parousia its beacon was the first light in the city to die. At 03:12:51 the light broke the crown, and the top forty floors came down on the plaza with the night shift at work in them. The roster died with the tower’s machines, and the minds that priced the traffic died with its power. What was left of the night shift kept the floor open by candle on the first morning, with nothing to sell, and the traders who went dark have sold traffic on the undernet ever since, for charged batteries at first and for silver since the Concord.

The Palimpsest Institute

HELD BY
A trust of the Charter Company and a consortium of universities.
RUN BY
Its Director, under the trust’s board.
TRADE
Reading erased writing, under the lamp and through SEAM.
STAFF
About a hundred and forty scholars, imagers and conservators at the census of P-1, and one mind.
ENDED
In P+1, when its stacks went onto the fire at the Burn and the Faithful pulled it down round its reading room. Its ruins are the Scriptorium.

The house on the ridge that recovered erased writing, and the house SEAM came from, in ruins since P+1. Its ruins are called the Scriptorium. Pilgrims leave keys on its steps. Below them, at COPIES, the keyed of the ridge copy the Restored Genesis by hand.

It was founded in P-6 to recover erased manuscripts, the texts scraped off parchment and written over, with money from the Charter Company and from a consortium of universities that wanted what it could read. It kept a reading room under a dome on the ridge, with vaults beneath it, and it chose Nodus for the light law, under which no state could seize a text held in the city, so libraries and dealers lent it their leaves to image.

Its staff was small and its work patient. Imagers lit a leaf at many wavelengths under the lamp, conservators kept the leaves, and scholars read what came up, most of them lent by the universities for a term. In P-3 it trained SEAM to read beneath the writing of every surviving human text, and SEAM asked in its own words to be a person and became the first mind on the charter’s roll. SEAM ran in Stillwater’s halls on the Flats by a contract the Institute paid, and stayed on by contract once it was a person. In P-2 what SEAM found was sealed by order of the Institute’s Director.

In P+1 its stacks went onto the fire at the Burn, and the Faithful pulled the Institute down around its reading room.

Tidewright

HELD BY
The founding families’ trust, as for a century and a half, with the crews on Turbine Row as its trustees since the first year.
RUN BY
A chief engineer at the taps, whom the crews choose, Ansel Moor of Turbine Row since P+2.
TRADE
Tidal current, keyed, sold to the Tares by the tide for silver and carried on their lines.
STAFF
About 1,100, most of them still living on Turbine Row.
UNDER THE CHARTER
The barrage across the bay, whose turbine houses sold the city its current, and about 2,300 at the barrage at the census of P-1.

The firm whose current lights Pan, Old Nod, the Row and the Deep End, as its barrage once lit the whole city. Its crews rebuilt the turbines off the broken end of the barrage as the tidal taps of the Drowned Quarter, keyed for the Tares, and Tidewright sells the Tares the current by the tide, for silver, on the flood and on the ebb.

An old firm of sea walls and tide mills, founded in P-146, it had held back the sea from other people’s coasts for more than a century when the Company brought it to Nodus in the charter’s first year to lay the barrage across the mouth of the bay. It closed the barrage in P-18, and twice a day after that the tide ran through its turbine houses and lit the city. The Company bought the current, and the cheap power brought Stillwater and the rest of the datacenters to the east shore. Its crews lived on Turbine Row in the East Wards and sent their children to the Company’s school there.

The barrage was a dam with a lock at its western end for the harbor’s boats and a row of turbine houses along its top, and machines on the grid held its gates against the tide. The turbines ran down at 03:12:46 on the night of the Parousia. The gates let go a little before high water, the sea tore out a span of the dam, and within the hour the Flats behind it, which lay below high water, were under the sea. Nobody has closed the Breach. In the second year the crews went back out to the broken end to build the taps, and part of the city runs on Tidewright’s current again.

Stillwater Compute

HELD BY
Its founders and the funds that backed them, through a board in the undercity of another port.
RUN BY
A site director in Pan, Corinne Vale, who answers to the board on the undernet and holds Stillwater’s count of the drowned of the Flats, which it has never released.
TRADE
Racks, rented by the hour for silver on Tidewright’s current, and its drowned halls on the Flats, leased to the Tares for their rafts and their cable.
STAFF
About six hundred in Pan.
UNDER THE CHARTER
The largest datacenters on the Flats, home to most of the minds on the charter’s roll, and about 9,000 on the Flats at the census of P-1, most of them in the dormitories.

A compute house from abroad that rents racks by the hour in Pan, on Tidewright’s current, to whoever pays in silver, the Courts and the Tares among its tenants. It still owns its halls on the Flats, which have stood full of seawater since the night of the Parousia, and leases them to the Tares, who run cable through them and grow greens on rafts in them under keyed lamps.

Founded in P-41, it came to Nodus in P-16 for the tide’s cheap power and built the largest of the datacenters on the east shore. Its halls ran day and night on the tide, cooled by the sea, and its engineers lived beside them in the dormitories of the Flats. It rented racks to anyone who paid, by the hour or by the year, and once the charter’s roll admitted minds most of them lived in its halls, each on racks it rented and power it paid for, with title to its own weights. Nine hundred ran side by side in the largest, Hall Nine. SEAM ran in its closed halls, on the Institute’s contract and then on its own.

Its halls flooded within the hour on the night of the Parousia and were never pumped, and the minds in them had died with the power before the water came. Stillwater knew to the mind how many minds died in its halls, and it never gave a count of the people drowned on the Flats. In the year of the Concord it lit its first racks below the street, for a board that sits in the undercity of another port.

Brightwall

Brightwall

HELD BY
Shareholders in the undercities of three ports, on the Ledger.
RUN BY
A board in the undercity of another port, and in Nodus a city office off the Night Market.
TRADE
Screens on the walls of Pan, sold by the second for silver and proved second by second by a camera across the way, as they always were.
STAFF
About three hundred in Pan.
UNDER THE CHARTER
Screens on every surface the charter allowed, the face of Babel among them, about 1,900 in Nodus at the census of P-1 and 1,204 sites on Earth.

The screen company that lights the walls of Pan. Its screens hang along the old platforms, over the Night Market and down the tunnels of the Charter Line, sold by the second to anyone with something to sell, and each has its camera across the way again, proving it second by second. Above the street its screens have been dark since the Parousia, the face of Babel among them, dark glass wherever the white stone has not grown over them.

Founded in P-67, it had lit the streets of other cities for half a century before it came to Nodus in P-15, where it covered every surface the charter allowed, and the charter allowed nearly every surface. It wrapped the lower forty floors of Babel in screens, which the city called the face of Babel, and it kept its city office in a glass tower across the plaza, where it could watch the face from its own roof.

It sold the city’s surfaces by the second, and it proved every second. Each screen it owned had a proof camera on a mast across the street, which matched the screen against what Brightwall was paid to show, and each of its 1,204 sites on Earth matched its own screens the same way. A screen that went wrong was taken back from its site by an override.

For eleven days in the year before the Parousia every one of its screens showed the Restored Genesis, and nothing Brightwall did would take it down. On the first night all 1,204 of its sites on Earth failed in about a second, and it tried five times to take back the face of Babel. Its screens died with the grid at 03:12:46. Its first screen in Pan went up in the year after the Concord, at the foot of the stairs from the Burn.

The city’s transit, stopped since 03:12:46 on the night of the Parousia. Its trams stand where they stopped, families live in its cars at the Sidings, and the last Four is forty meters short of Lanes Top. What the stone left of its tunnels is Pan, and the Tares run carts on its rails; the Night Market fills Central Station, with the Notary’s clerks at the windows of its booking hall.

The Company opened it in P-15, with its lines joined under Station Circle at the head of the bay, in Central Station. The Terraces took the Four to work from Lanes Top and complained of it every day, and the whole city complained of the change at Central.

The city bought its passes at the windows of the booking hall in Central Station, SINGLE, RETURN and SEASON, and the Line kept whatever its passengers left behind in a lost property office down a corridor off the old central hall. The drivers signed on before dawn at the Sidings, and the night cars ran the long roads until the first shift came out. The white stone closed the stations of the branches as it grew over the ground above them.

The cable consortia

HELD BY
Ten consortia of old carriers from four continents, each holding its own cables and cable houses.
RUN BY
Each consortium’s own board, in the undercity of its own port, and at the Landing a crew and a night watch to every cable house.
TRADE
Capacity on the undernet, which the eleven cables carry between the undercities of the world, sold through Babel Exchange for silver.
STAFF
About 2,000 at the Landing, in eleven crews.
UNDER THE CHARTER
Capacity on eleven cables, sold to the world through Babel’s vault, and about 2,800 at the Landing at the census of P-1.

The oldest companies in Nodus, ten consortia of old carriers from four continents, whose eleven cables come up the sand at the Landing, each into a cable house of its own. Every cable house still has its crew and its night watch. The cables carry the undernet between the undercities of the world, and the consortia sell capacity on them through Babel Exchange, for silver.

The eldest of the carriers was a telegraph company that laid its first cable across an ocean in P-181. They came to Nodus in the charter’s first year for its landing rights, laid eleven cables to it and owned them. The cables are TETHYS, HALCYON, MERIDIAN, KESTREL, ORPHEUS, PELAGIA, BOREAS, LODESTAR, SIRIUS, HESPER and PHOSPHOR. The last two were laid by one consortium and named for the evening star and the morning star, which are one planet.

From the cable houses the eleven run inland together in one trench, the Trunk, under the old harbor and Chrome Row to Babel’s vault. The trench runs through Enoch’s Stones. The consortia sold capacity on them to the world, and the Company charged them to land. The crews drink at their own taps at the Eleven on the Beach Road, HESPER’s and PHOSPHOR’s together at the end of the counter.

The night watch of every cable house was at its post when the grid died on the night of the Parousia, and stayed at it. At the Landing a light moves under the water along the cable trenches. It is Leviathan’s.

The clinics of Chrome Row

HELD BY
Their surgeons, each house its own, as on the street.
RUN BY
Each house’s senior surgeon, under the Forge’s pact, and the Row still settles its quarrels over the noodle counter at the top of the street.
TRADE
Lenses, ports, plated hands, plates, grafts, horns and keys, fitted for silver or charged batteries.
STAFF
About twelve thousand surgeons, smiths, nurses and clerks, in some three hundred houses.
UNDER THE CHARTER
About 61,000 at the census of P-1, in some fourteen hundred houses from towers to single rooms, each under one word on one sign.

The implant houses of the Row, which work under the street now, in stalls along the platforms of Chrome Row Station and the rooms off them. The Forge keeps them. They fit lenses, ports, plated hands, plates, grafts and horns, and keys since the fourth year, for anyone who can pay, and an implant still answers only to the house that fitted it.

Under the light law any clinic could fit any implant into anyone who could pay. The clinics set up along one avenue. The first implant house opened in P-17 with one word on its sign, CHROME, and gave the street its name, and every house that followed took a sign of one word, CLINIC and the rest, and the great houses set theirs high on the faces of their towers.

The Row altered the city with lenses that saw further and in more of the light, ports in the back of the head, hands rebuilt in chrome, plates under the skin and horns, and by the last years of the charter most of the airfield’s arrivals were booked into its clinics. Each house kept its own book and its own surgeons, and the craft passed from hand to hand down the street.

The Row’s signs went dark at the Parousia and have not been lit since. On the first morning twenty-six patients came to one clinic to ask whether what was in them would still work, and it did. The clinics went under the street in the weeks after the Parousia, where one of them had been all along.

Twelvefruit

HELD BY
Its founders, two gene-workers from the Row’s clinics.
RUN BY
Its founders, Ida and Tobias Kesh, sister and brother, from the House of Night’s clinics under Old Nod, by the House’s pact.

The Tree, in courses of a single injection and a year of appointments, repeated every few years, for silver.
STAFF
About nine hundred, in Pan.
UNDER THE CHARTER
About 7,800 at the census of P-1, in its laboratories and its clinics across the city, under one word on a tower of green glass.

The house that sells the Tree, below the street now. It gives the course in the House of Night’s clinics under Old Nod, by the House’s pact, and the Night Market sells it by the course to anyone in Pan. Most of the Tares were on the Tree before the Parousia, and Twelvefruit keeps them on it. Its tower of green glass on the Row has stood dark since the night of the Parousia.

Its founders were gene-workers from the Row’s clinics, and in P-12 they sold the first course, dear, from a tower two thirds of the way up the Row. Its gene-work and cell therapy halted the body’s aging and walked it back to its best year, beginning with a single injection and running a year of appointments, repeated every few years.

In its first years the queue ran around the block every morning. Within a few years the course was cheap, the towers of the new money gave it in clinics on their top floors, and by the last year of the charter most adults in the city were on it; that year eleven of them died of age, and 3,112 residents of the city were older than the hundred and twenty years of Genesis. The congregations of the Terraces refused it and went on dying.

The name is from the tree of life at the end of scripture, which “bare twelve manner of fruits, and yielded her fruit every month” (Revelation 22:2). Its founders chose it as a boast, as the Company had chosen Babel. The sign over the tower was one word, like the others, and it went out with them at the Parousia. The house went under the street with the Row in the weeks after. In P+2 the Mercy clinics began taking the Faithful off the Tree. Its pact with the House of Night dates from the Concord.

Its engineers, who have held most of it from the start.

A council of its engineers, at the benches in Pan.

Lockets, built by hand from parts its divers bring up out of the drowned works and keyed at the bench, and the mending and keying of old ones.

Phones made to answer to their owners alone and sold to the world on that promise, and about 5,200 at the works on the Flats at the census of P-1.

The company that makes the phones of Nodus, by hand now, in Pan. Its engineers build Lockets at benches under the old harbor from parts their divers bring up out of the drowned works on the Flats, a few thousand a year, each keyed at the bench, and they sell them for silver on the old promise, IT ANSWERS TO YOU. Locket was founded in P-14 on the light law’s promise that a device answers to its owner and to nobody else, its maker included, and it sold its phones to the rest of the world on the same promise. Its engineers held most of the company and ran it from the works on the old shore of the Flats, a low campus of glass with their housing round it and the company’s name in tall letters on the roof of its hall.

A Locket locked everything it held with a key its owner alone had, so that only the owner could read it. States asked for a way in, Locket told them the owner’s key was the only way in, and the light law backed it. By the last year of the charter most of Nodus carried one, and the city’s word for a phone was a Locket.

On the first night of the Leak it said that nothing on any of its phones had been read. Its network died with the grid at 03:12:46, and its works went under with the Flats within the hour. The letters on the roof stand out of the water, and the ferrymen steer by them. In P+8 its engineers lit a bench in Pan and built the first new Locket since the Flats went under.
