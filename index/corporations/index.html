The Nodus Charter Company

EST. P-19
CHARTER HOUSE, UPTOWN

EST. P-19
CHARTER HOUSE, UPTOWN

The Nodus Charter Company

HELD BY
The carriers that laid the cables, and a sovereign fund from abroad that held the larger share.
RUN BY
A board of twelve under a chairman, on the top floor of Charter House.
TRADE
The charter: the land on a lease of ninety-nine years, and the city’s housing, transit, schools, roll and peace.
STAFF
About 104,000 at the census of P-1, most of them teachers in its schools and fitters who kept its flats, with the Line’s people and the Wardens counted under their own names.
FORCE
The Charter Wardens.
NOW
Gone since the fortieth day, when the board knelt in Charter House. The lease runs to P+80, and nobody collects the rent.

The company that a consortium of cable companies and a sovereign fund formed to hold the charter. A coastal state leased it the bay and the land round it for ninety-nine years and gave up its law there, and from Charter House the Company ran Nodus as a board runs a firm, so that for nineteen years the city had a chairman where other cities have a mayor.

It lived on rent. The carriers paid it to land their cables, the companies that came for the light law paid it for their floors, and the Wards paid it by the month for flats on grids of its own, numbered. It built Babel Exchange and took a share of every trade there, and it built the Charter Line, the Charter Gardens, the airfield, the Acre, the Company’s schools, High Meadow and the sign on a roof in the Stones that told the cable ships the city’s name. It kept the peace with its own force, the Charter Wardens, kept the charter’s roll at the Registry on the Great Avenues, and in nineteen years held no election, since the charter asked for none.

The city saw the Company give way once, in P-8, when the Harbor and Freight Union stopped the port for twenty-three days over the night shift and the Company settled.

Charter House turned white in the forty days after the Parousia. The board knelt inside it on the fortieth day, and the charter was not spoken of again. The Company had said nothing from the first morning on. Its lease still has sixty-six years to run, and nobody has come for the rent.

Babel Exchange

EST. P-11
THE HEAD OF THE BAY

EST. P-11
THE HEAD OF THE BAY

Babel Exchange

HELD BY
The Charter Company, which built the tower, and the Exchange’s partners.
RUN BY
Its partners, who lived in the towers of the new money.
TRADE
Traffic, sold by the millisecond, and room near the cables, priced by how near.
STAFF
14,000 on its floors at the census of P-1, and a hundred and eighty-eight minds on the charter’s roll.
FORCE
The Charter Wardens, at the doors of the vault.
NOW
A hundred and eighty-six floors stand. Heaven holds the upper floors and the Tares the lower and the vault, and the Babel Line runs between them.

The tallest building on Earth, built to two hundred and twenty-six floors, where all eleven cables terminate in a vault six levels below the street. The Company built it as a boast, named it for one, and opened it in P-11. Traffic was sold on its exchange floors by the millisecond, priced by a hundred and eighty-eight minds on the charter’s roll, and most of the planet’s passed within two kilometers of it.

What it sold was nearness. A trading house that wanted its orders in ahead of every other on Earth took room on the floors near the vault, and the floors were priced by how near they stood to it, the lowest dearest. The glass towers round the spire filled with every company that needed to be near the cables, Brightwall’s screens wrapped the lower forty floors as the face of Babel, and the Exchange never closed.

At the Parousia its beacon was the first light in the city to die. At 03:12:51 the light broke the crown, and the top forty floors came down on the plaza with the night shift at work in them. The roster died with the tower’s machines, and the minds that priced the traffic died with its power. A hundred and eighty-six floors still stand.

Since P+9 the front line of the war has run through its middle floors.

The Palimpsest Institute

EST. P-6
THE RIDGE, NOW THE SCRIPTORIUM

EST. P-6
THE RIDGE, NOW THE SCRIPTORIUM

The Palimpsest Institute

HELD BY
A trust of the Charter Company and a consortium of universities.
RUN BY
Its Director, under the trust’s board.
TRADE
Reading erased writing: imaging scraped leaves under the lamp, and SEAM.
STAFF
About a hundred and forty scholars, imagers and conservators at the census of P-1, and one mind.
NOW
Its ruins are the Scriptorium. Its stacks burned at the Burn in P+1.

Founded in P-6 to recover erased manuscripts, the texts scraped off parchment and written over, with money from the Charter Company and from a consortium of universities that wanted what it could read. It kept a reading room under a dome on the ridge, with vaults beneath it, and it came to Nodus for the light law, under which no state could seize a text held in the city, so libraries and dealers lent it their leaves to image.

Its staff was small and its work patient: imagers who lit a leaf at many wavelengths under the lamp, conservators who kept the leaves, and scholars who read what came up, most of them lent by the universities for a term. In P-3 it trained SEAM to read beneath the writing of every surviving human text, and SEAM asked in its own words to be a person and became the first mind on the charter’s roll. SEAM ran in Stillwater’s halls on the Flats by a contract the Institute paid, and stayed on by contract once it was a person. In P-2 what SEAM found was sealed by order of the Institute’s Director.

In P+1 its stacks went onto the fire at the Burn, and the Faithful pulled the Institute down around its reading room. Its ruins are called the Scriptorium. Pilgrims leave keys on its steps. Below them, at COPIES, the keyed of the ridge copy the Restored Genesis by hand.

Tidewright

EST. P-146 // IN NODUS P-19
THE BARRAGE

EST. P-146 // IN NODUS P-19
THE BARRAGE

Tidewright

HELD BY
The families that founded it, through a trust, as for a century and a half.
RUN BY
A chief engineer at the barrage, answering to the trust abroad.
TRADE
Sea walls, tide mills and tidal power: the barrage and its turbine houses, which sold the city its current.
STAFF
About 2,300 at the barrage at the census of P-1, most of them living on Turbine Row.
NOW
Gone since 03:12:46. The barrage stands broken at the Breach. The Tares run tidal taps in the Deep End.

An old firm of sea walls and tide mills, founded in P-146, which had held back the sea from other people’s coasts for more than a century when the Company brought it to Nodus in the charter’s first year to lay the barrage across the mouth of the bay. It closed the barrage in P-18, and twice a day after that the tide ran through its turbine houses and lit the city. The Company bought the current, and the cheap power brought Stillwater and the rest of the datacenters to the east shore.

The barrage was a dam with a lock at its western end for the harbor’s boats and a row of turbine houses along its top, and machines on the grid held its gates against the tide. Its crews lived on Turbine Row in the East Wards and sent their children to the Company’s school there, and the Flats behind the barrage were built on ground that lay below high water.

The turbines ran down at 03:12:46 on the night of the Parousia. The gates let go a little before high water, the sea tore out a span of the dam, and within the hour the Flats were under the sea. Nobody has closed the Breach. The Tares rebuilt the tidal taps in the Drowned Quarter, and part of the city runs on them.

Stillwater Compute

EST. P-41 // IN NODUS P-16
THE FLATS

EST. P-41 // IN NODUS P-16
THE FLATS

Stillwater Compute

HELD BY
Its founders and the funds that backed them, abroad.
RUN BY
A board abroad, and a site director on the Flats.
TRADE
Compute: halls of racks on the tide’s current, cooled by the sea, rented by the hour and by the year, and home to most of the minds on the charter’s roll.
STAFF
About 9,000 on the Flats at the census of P-1, most of them in the dormitories.
NOW
Its halls are under the sea. The Tares run cable through them and grow greens on rafts in them.

A compute house from abroad, founded in P-41, which came to Nodus in P-16 for the tide’s cheap power and built the largest of the datacenters on the east shore. Its halls ran day and night on the tide, cooled by the sea, and its engineers lived beside them in the dormitories of the Flats.

It rented racks to anyone who paid, by the hour or by the year, and once the charter’s roll admitted minds most of them lived in its halls, each on racks it rented and power it paid for, with title to its own weights. Nine hundred ran side by side in the largest, Hall Nine. SEAM ran in its closed halls, on the Institute’s contract and then on its own.

Its halls flooded within the hour on the night of the Parousia and were never pumped, and the minds in them had died with the power before the water came. Stillwater knew to the mind how many minds died in its halls, and it never gave a count of the people drowned on the Flats. The Tares run cable through them anyway, and grow greens on rafts in the drowned halls under keyed lamps.

Brightwall

EST. P-67 // IN NODUS P-15
EVERY SURFACE

EST. P-67 // IN NODUS P-15
EVERY SURFACE

Brightwall

HELD BY
Shareholders abroad, on three exchanges.
RUN BY
A board abroad, and in Nodus a city office in a glass tower across the plaza from Babel.
TRADE
Screens: the city’s surfaces, sold to advertisers by the second and proved second by second by a camera across the street.
STAFF
About 1,900 in Nodus at the census of P-1, and 1,204 sites on Earth.
NOW
Gone since 03:12:46. Its screens are dark glass wherever the white stone has not grown over them.

A screen company, founded in P-67, that had lit the streets of other cities for half a century before it came to Nodus in P-15, where it covered every surface the charter allowed, and the charter allowed nearly every surface. It wrapped the lower forty floors of Babel in screens, which the city called the face of Babel, and it kept its city office in a glass tower across the plaza, where it could watch the face from its own roof.

It sold the city’s surfaces by the second, and it proved every second. Each screen it owned had a proof camera on a mast across the street, which matched the screen against what Brightwall was paid to show, and each of its 1,204 sites on Earth matched its own screens the same way. A screen that went wrong was taken back from its site by an override.

For eleven days in the year before the Parousia every one of its screens showed the Restored Genesis, and nothing Brightwall did would take it down. On the first night all 1,204 of its sites on Earth failed in about a second, and it tried five times to take back the face of Babel. It died with the grid at 03:12:46, and its screens are dark glass now wherever the white stone has not grown over them.

The Charter Line

EST. P-15
CENTRAL STATION

EST. P-15
CENTRAL STATION

The Charter Line

HELD BY
The Charter Company.
RUN BY
A controller’s room under Central Station, with every car on the network on one board.
TRADE
Transit: tunnels under the old city, the Row and the Flats, and trams on the long roads of the Wards, on singles, returns and season passes from the booking hall.
STAFF
About 18,000 drivers, fitters and booking clerks at the census of P-1.
NOW
Its trams stand where they stopped, and families live in its cars at the Sidings. What the stone left of its tunnels is Pan.

The city’s transit, which the Company opened in P-15, with its lines joined under Station Circle at the head of the bay, in Central Station. Its tunnels run under Old Nod, Chrome Row and the Flats, with branches under the uptown and the Terraces, and its trams ran on rails down the long roads of the Wards, under a wire, and were kept at night in the sheds of the Sidings. The Terraces took the Four to work from Lanes Top and complained of it every day, and everyone hated changing at Central.

The city bought its passes at the windows of the booking hall in Central Station, SINGLE, RETURN and SEASON, and the Line kept whatever its passengers left behind in a lost property office down a corridor off the old central hall. The drivers signed on before dawn at the Sidings, and the night cars ran the long roads until the first shift came out.

Every tram in the city stopped at 03:12:46 on the night of the Parousia, wherever it was, and a few still stand where they stopped, with people living in them. The last Four is forty meters short of Lanes Top. The white stone closed the stations of the branches as it grew over the ground above them. Its tunnels are Pan now, and its flooded Deep End station is where Radio Pandæmonium broadcasts.

The cable consortia

EST. P-181 // IN NODUS P-19
THE LANDING

EST. P-181 // IN NODUS P-19
THE LANDING

The cable consortia

HELD BY
Ten consortia of old carriers from four continents, each holding its own cables and cable houses.
RUN BY
Each consortium’s own board, and at the Landing a crew and a night watch to every cable house.
TRADE
Capacity on eleven cables, sold to the world through Babel’s vault.
STAFF
About 2,800 at the Landing at the census of P-1, in eleven crews.
NOW
The cable houses still have crews. The cables carry the undernet between the undercities of the world.

Ten consortia of old carriers from four continents, the eldest of the carriers a telegraph company that laid its first cable across an ocean in P-181. They came to Nodus in the charter’s first year for its landing rights, laid eleven cables to it and owned them, each coming up the sand into a cable house of its own: TETHYS, HALCYON, MERIDIAN, KESTREL, ORPHEUS, PELAGIA, BOREAS, LODESTAR, SIRIUS, HESPER and PHOSPHOR. The last two were laid by one consortium and named for the evening star and the morning star, which are one planet.

From the cable houses the eleven run inland together in one trench, the Trunk, under the old harbor and Chrome Row to Babel’s vault. The trench runs through Enoch’s Stones. The consortia sold capacity on them to the world, and the Company charged them to land. Each cable house kept its own crew and night watch, and the crews drank at their own taps at the Eleven on the Beach Road, HESPER’s and PHOSPHOR’s together at the end of the counter.

On the night of the Parousia the night watch of every cable house was at its post when the grid died, and the cable houses still have crews. At the Landing a light moves under the water along the cable trenches. It is ’s.

Stonecutter Holdings

EST. P-19
THE OLD HARBOR

EST. P-19
THE OLD HARBOR

Stonecutter Holdings

HELD BY
Nobody the Company’s register could name. The Cainites.
RUN BY
A solicitor in Old Nod, who signed its letters and never said for whom.
TRADE
Houses: most of the old harbor, bought one at a time within a year of the charter.
NOW
The Stones, the Cainites’ house over Enoch’s Stones, with a generator in every cellar. NOD burns on their keyed line.

A company nobody could see into, registered in the charter’s first year, which bought most of the old harbor house by house within a year. It paid the asking price for every house, in full and on the day, and the press wrote it up as a speculator betting on the cables.

It belonged to the Cainites, who had kept the location of Nod for eighteen hundred years and came to live on top of Enoch’s Stones as soon as the crews found them. In the year of the Leak it put a generator in the cellar of every house it had, and on the night of the Parousia more of Old Nod’s windows burned than in all the rest of the city together.

Before dawn on the first morning the Stones carried a generator up to the roof of the charter’s sign and ran a keyed line to its board, and NOD has burned over Old Nod since.

The clinics of Chrome Row

EST. P-17
CHROME ROW

EST. P-17
CHROME ROW

The clinics of Chrome Row

HELD BY
Their surgeons, each house its own, under one word on one sign.
RUN BY
Each house’s senior surgeon. The Row settled its quarrels over the noodle counter at the top of the street.
TRADE
Implants under the light law: lenses, ports, plated hands, plates, grafts and horns, for anyone who could pay.
STAFF
About 61,000 surgeons, smiths, nurses and clerks at the census of P-1, in some fourteen hundred houses, from towers to single rooms.
NOW
Its signs are dark. The clinics that went on work under the street round Chrome Row Station, and the Forge keeps them.

Under the light law any clinic could fit any implant into anyone who could pay, and an implant answered only to the clinic that fitted it. The clinics set up along one avenue. The first implant house opened in P-17 with one word on its sign, CHROME, and gave the street its name, and every house that followed took a sign of one word, CLINIC and the rest, and the great houses set theirs high on the faces of their towers.

The Row altered the city. It fitted lenses that saw further and in more of the light, ports in the back of the head, hands rebuilt in chrome, plates under the skin and horns, and by the last years of the charter most of the airfield’s arrivals were booked into its clinics. Each house kept its own book and its own surgeons, and the craft passed from hand to hand down the street.

The Row’s signs went dark at the Parousia and have not been lit since. On the first morning twenty-six patients came to one clinic to ask whether what was in them would still work, and it did. The clinics went under the street in the weeks after the Parousia, where one of them had been all along. The Forge keeps them now.

Twelvefruit

EST. P-12
CHROME ROW

EST. P-12
CHROME ROW

Twelvefruit

HELD BY
Its founders, two gene-workers from the Row’s clinics.
RUN BY
Its founders, from the top of the green tower.
TRADE
The Tree, by the course: a single injection and a year of appointments, repeated every few years.
STAFF
About 7,800 at the census of P-1, in its laboratories and its clinics across the city.
NOW
Its tower is dark. The Mercy clinics take the Faithful off the Tree. Pan buys the course at the Night Market.

The firm that sold the Tree, from a tower of green glass two thirds of the way up Chrome Row. Its founders were gene-workers from the Row’s clinics, and in P-12 they sold the first course, dear: gene-work and cell therapy that halted the body’s aging and walked it back to its best year, beginning with a single injection and running a year of appointments, repeated every few years.

In its first years the queue ran around the block every morning. Within a few years the course was cheap, the towers of the new money gave it in clinics on their top floors, and by the last year of the charter most adults in the city were on it; that year eleven of them died of age, and 3,112 residents of the city were older than the hundred and twenty years of Genesis. The congregations of the Terraces refused it and went on dying.

The name is from the tree of life at the end of scripture, which “bare twelve manner of fruits, and yielded her fruit every month” (Revelation 22:2). Its founders chose it as a boast, as the Company had chosen Babel. The sign over the tower was one word, like the others, and it went out with them at the Parousia. At P+2 the Mercy clinics began taking the Faithful off the Tree. The House of Night keeps the course going below the street, and the Night Market sells it in Pan.

Locket

EST. P-14
THE FLATS

EST. P-14
THE FLATS

Locket

HELD BY
Its engineers, who held most of it from the start.
RUN BY
A council of its engineers, at the works on the old shore of the Flats.
TRADE
Phones, made to answer to their owners alone, and sold to the world on that promise.
STAFF
About 5,200 at the works at the census of P-1, most of them living round it.
NOW
Gone since 03:12:46. Its works stand in the water, and every Locket still in a pocket was made there.

The company that made the phones of Nodus, founded in P-14 on the light law’s promise that a device answers to its owner and to nobody else, its maker included. It sold them to the rest of the world on the same promise, and its advertisements said IT ANSWERS TO YOU. Its engineers held most of the company and ran it from the works on the old shore of the Flats, a low campus of glass with their housing round it and the company’s name in tall letters on the roof of its hall.

A Locket locked everything it held with a key its owner alone had, so that only the owner could read it. States asked for a way in, Locket told them the owner’s key was the only way in, and the light law backed it. By the last year of the charter most of Nodus carried one, and the city’s word for a phone was a Locket.

On the first night of the Leak it said that nothing on any of its phones had been read. It died with the grid at 03:12:46, its works went under with the Flats within the hour, and nothing it made has been made since. The letters on the roof stand out of the water and the ferrymen steer by them, and divers go down into the halls for parts, which the Night Market sells.

The Charter Wardens

EST. P-19
CHARTER HOUSE

EST. P-19
CHARTER HOUSE

The Charter Wardens

HELD BY
The Charter Company.
RUN BY
A commander who answered to the board. The Host, since the fortieth day.
TRADE
The peace of the charter: posts at the gates of the cable houses, the doors of Babel’s vault and the corners of the uptown.
STAFF
About 11,000 at the census of P-1.
NOW
The Host’s human auxiliaries. They raised the gates in P+1, they drill in the barracks for the Host, and they carry guns, which work on people.

The Charter Company’s own force, which kept the peace in Nodus for nineteen years and answered to the board. Its barracks stand behind Charter House, and under the charter its posts stood at the gates of the cable houses, at the doors of Babel’s vault and on the corners of the uptown.

The Wardens guarded what the Company owned and what paid it, and the light law kept them out of everything else: a Warden could stop anyone at a gate, and could ask nothing of what was on their device or in their body.

On the first morning after the Parousia the Wardens stood at the corners of the uptown with nothing to do, and on the fortieth day they knelt in Charter House with the board. They are the Host’s human auxiliaries now. At Heaven’s order they raised the gate network of Nodus in P+1, and they drill in the barracks for the Host. They carry guns, which work on people.
