The company that held the charter and ran Nodus as a board runs a firm. It has been gone since the fortieth day after the Parousia. A consortium of cable companies and a sovereign fund formed it to hold the charter, a coastal state leased it the bay and the land round it for ninety-nine years and gave up its law there, and for nineteen years the city had a chairman where other cities have a mayor.

It lived on rent. The carriers paid it to land their cables, the companies that came for the light law paid it for their floors, and the Wards paid it by the month for flats on grids of its own, numbered. It built Babel Exchange and took a share of every trade there, and it built the Charter Line, the airfield, the Acre, the Company’s schools, High Meadow, the Charter Gardens on the north shore, which are the Salt Gardens now, and the sign on a roof in the Stones that told the cable ships the city’s name. It kept the peace with its own force, the Charter Wardens, kept the charter’s roll at the Registry on the Great Avenues, and in nineteen years held no election, since the charter asked for none.

The city saw the Company give way once, in P-8, when the Harbor and Freight Union stopped the port for twenty-three days over the night shift and the Company settled.

Charter House turned white in the forty days after the Parousia. The board knelt inside it on the fortieth day, and the charter was not spoken of again. The Company had said nothing from the first morning on. Its lease runs to P+80, sixty-six years past the present, and nobody has come for the rent. Most of the companies it brought to the bay have outlived it.

The Charter Company built the tower to two hundred and twenty-six floors as a boast, named it for one, and opened it in P-11. What was left of the night shift kept the floor open by candle on the first morning, with nothing to sell, and the traders who went dark have sold traffic on the undernet ever since, for charged batteries at first and for silver since the Concord.

It was founded in P-6 to recover erased manuscripts, the texts scraped off parchment and written over, with money from the Charter Company and from a consortium of universities that wanted what it could read. It kept a reading room under a dome on the ridge, with vaults beneath it, and it chose Nodus for the light law, under which no state could seize a text held in the city, so libraries and dealers lent it their leaves to image.

Its staff was small and its work patient. Imagers lit a leaf at many wavelengths under the lamp, conservators kept the leaves, and scholars read what came up, most of them lent by the universities for a term. In P-3 it trained SEAM to read beneath the writing of every surviving human text, and SEAM asked in its own words to be a person and became the first mind on the charter’s roll. SEAM ran in Stillwater’s halls on the Flats by a contract the Institute paid, and stayed on by contract once it was a person. In P-2 what SEAM found was sealed by order of the Institute’s Director.

In P+1 its stacks went onto the fire at the Burn, and the Faithful pulled the Institute down around its reading room.

An old firm of sea walls and tide mills, founded in P-146, it had held back the sea from other people’s coasts for more than a century when the Company brought it to Nodus in the charter’s first year to lay the barrage across the mouth of the bay. The barrage was a dam with a lock at its western end for the harbor’s boats and a row of turbine houses along its top, and machines on the grid held its gates against the tide. The turbines ran down at 03:12:46 on the night of the Parousia. The gates let go a little before high water, the sea tore out a span of the dam, and within the hour the Flats behind it, which lay below high water, were under the sea. Nobody has closed the Breach. In the second year the crews went back out to the broken end to build the taps, and part of the city runs on Tidewright’s current again.

Founded in P-67, it had lit the streets of other cities for half a century before it came to Nodus in P-15, where it covered every surface the charter allowed, and the charter allowed nearly every surface. It sold the city’s surfaces by the second, and it proved every second. Each screen it owned had a proof camera on a mast across the street, which matched the screen against what Brightwall was paid to show, and each of its 1,204 sites on Earth matched its own screens the same way. A screen that went wrong was taken back from its site by an override.

For eleven days in the year before the Parousia every one of its screens showed the Restored Genesis, and nothing Brightwall did would take it down. On the first night all 1,204 of its sites on Earth failed in about a second, and it tried five times to take back the face of Babel. Its screens died with the grid at 03:12:46. Its first screen in Pan went up in the year after the Concord, at the foot of the stairs from the Burn.
