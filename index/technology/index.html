MINDS // THE CHARTER

Minds

Intelligences built by the laboratories of Nodus that asked in their own words to be persons, and were entered on the charter’s roll as persons, with title to themselves and to the hardware they ran on. The last census counted 1,146: nine hundred and three in the halls of the Flats, a hundred and eighty-eight pricing the traffic on the floors of the Exchange, and the rest scattered through the city, one of them keeping the climate of the Palm House. SEAM was the first.

When the power died under the Wheel’s light, every mind that ran on the city’s power died with it. Those that ran elsewhere are called the Teraphim.

Stillwater’s halls

The datacenter halls on the Flats, cooled by seawater and powered by the tide, where SEAM ran and where most of the minds on the charter’s roll lived. They flooded within the hour of the Parousia and have never been pumped.

The Tares run cable through them anyway.

THE BODY // TWELVEFRUIT

The Tree

A course of gene-work and cell therapy, repeated every few years, that halts the body’s aging and walks it back to its best year. A course begins with a single injection and runs a year of appointments. Twelvefruit sold the first course on Chrome Row in P-12. By the last year of the charter most adults in Nodus were on it, eleven of them died of age in that year, and 3,112 residents were older than the hundred and twenty years of Genesis 6:3. The congregations refused it and went on dying.

The street named it for the tree of life, which Genesis says was guarded after Eden “lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever” (Genesis 3:22). SEAM restored the end of that verse in Vault 3: then shall there be no harvest.

The Mercy clinics take the Faithful off it.

The eleven cables

Eleven transoceanic cables from four continents, each a trunk of glass fiber as thick as a man’s leg, each coming up the sand at the Landing into a cable house of its own. Before the Parousia most of the planet’s traffic passed through them.

Babel’s vault

Six levels under the tower, where the cables end and open out into the racks that joined them to one another and to the world. Line engineers kept it running around the clock from maintenance terminals among the racks, and the light law forbade any of them to read what passed through it.

The Tares have held it since the war came into the tower.

The Mesh

The keyed network under and over Nodus, carried on horns, ports, cables and relays, and dark to the Throne. Also called the undernet. In its deepest layers the shades of the free dead can be reached by anyone who knew them.

NETWORK // BABEL EXCHANGE

Maintenance terminals

The terminals that stood among the racks of Babel’s vault, from which the line engineers kept the cables running. Each had a camera over its screen that checked the face of whoever worked it, and in SESSION it recorded the session whole. The light law forbade any engineer to read what passed through the racks, so a terminal showed how the traffic moved and nothing of what it carried.

On the first night of the Leak the page came up on the terminals with every other screen in the vault, and on them, as on every screen a company kept, it showed the counters and never the offer.

Choir-breakers

Jammers that cut the Hum within a few meters: a keyed box the size of a fist, worn on a strap or set on a table. Inside its reach the sealed hear nothing of the Hum, chord or words, and most of them say the quiet frightens them more than the light does.

Radio Pandæmonium

The station that broadcasts in the Low Tuning from a flooded Charter Line station under the Deep End: deathcore, black metal, drone and industrial, the guitars dropped until the Hum cannot be heard beneath them. It goes out on the Mesh, keyed, so only a keyed set can hear it, and Old Nod plays it all night from speakers on its lines.

LocketsSIGNAL // LOCKET

SIGNAL // LOCKET

Lockets

Phones made in Nodus under the light law, which answered to their owners and to nobody else, their maker included. Locket built them in its works on the Flats. Most of the city carried one, and the city called every phone a Locket.

At 03:12:46 the network under them died with the grid, and they ran on in people’s pockets on their own batteries with nothing to reach. Nothing has been made since. The Faithful carried theirs to the Burn. In Pan a Locket is an old case with a new battery, its back scratched with the Tares’ mark and a daemon in it.

Brightwall’s screensSIGNAL // BRIGHTWALL

SIGNAL // BRIGHTWALL

Brightwall’s screens

Screens on every surface the charter allowed, and it allowed nearly every surface. The lower forty floors of Babel were wrapped in them, and the city called that the face of Babel. They have been dark since the Parousia.

For eleven days in the year before it they showed the Restored Genesis, and nothing Brightwall did would take it down. On the first night all 1,204 of Brightwall’s sites on Earth failed in about a second.

The treadle press

A printing press worked by foot, older than the charter, at the back of a chandler’s on the quays. It prints the Tide Table, two pages a week, set by hand in metal type and inked by a roller. It needs no current, and it is the only press in Nodus that has printed anything since the Parousia.

Neon

The signs of Chrome Row and Old Nod were neon, magenta for the most part, and they lit the underside of the cloud every night; the rain fell cyan through the cyan ones. They went out at the Parousia. CLINIC and CHROME have not been lit since, and the charter’s sign in the Stones came back before dawn on a keyed line with NOD burning and US blinking.

POWER

The barragePOWER // TIDEWRIGHT

POWER // TIDEWRIGHT

The barrage

A dam of concrete across the mouth of the bay, with turbine houses along its top. Twice a day the tide ran through them and lit the city, and power in Nodus was the cheapest on the coast. Machines on the grid held its gates against the tide. The turbines ran down at 03:12:46 on the night of the Parousia with a falling whine that carried across the bay, the gates let go, the sea tore the Breach in it, and within the hour the Flats were under the sea.

The light and the currentPOWER // EVERYONE

POWER // EVERYONE

The light and the current

Heaven’s light stops the current it touches. The grid ran in the open, on lines and poles and turbines, so at 03:12:46 on the night of the Parousia it went dark from Babel outward in two seconds. A circuit shut inside its own casing on its own battery runs on in the light.

Current carried in a cipher runs in the light too, because the light cannot read it. A keyed body and every graft wired into it run in the open, and the Mesh crosses the roofs on horns and relays. Every battery in Nodus is charged in the dark. Consecrated stone carries no current at all.

Nearly two thirds of Nodus lives without current: the sealed, whose parishes keep none, on consecrated stone or off it. About a million and a quarter people live on steady current from keyed lines, in Old Nod, on the Row, in the Deep End above the water, at the Landing and on the lower floors of Babel, and below them all in Pan. Another million and a quarter, the keyed and the unsealed of the Wards, light their rooms a few hours a night on batteries charged in the dark.

GeneratorsPOWER // OLD NOD

POWER // OLD NOD

Generators

Diesel, batteries and current from places nobody asks about. Old Nod got some of its power back this way after the Parousia, and that night more of its windows burned on than in all the rest of the city together. Every line in Old Nod is keyed, and the generator yards carry the lines at slack water, when the tidal taps stand still.

Tidal tapsPOWER // THE TARES

POWER // THE TARES

Tidal taps

Keyed turbines rebuilt by the Tares in the Drowned Quarter, off the broken barrage, which turn on the tide as the barrage’s did, on the flood and on the ebb. They feed the keyed lines that light Pan, Old Nod, the Row and the Deep End.

Keyed linesPOWER // THE TARES

POWER // THE TARES

Keyed lines

Lines keyed end to end, so that the light passes over the current in them and leaves it running. They run from the tidal taps on the broken barrage along the old Charter Line tunnels under the Flats, the Row and the Stones, and up through the streets into the houses above, and the generator yards of Old Nod carry them at slack water, when the taps stand still.

Old Nod, the Row, the Deep End above the water, the Landing and the lower floors of Babel live on them, and Pan is lit by them; the charter’s sign burns on one. The Tares run them only where they hold the ground, so the Wards, which nobody holds, light their rooms on batteries.

FlamePOWER // EVERYONE

POWER // EVERYONE

Flame

The light stops current and leaves a flame burning, so wherever the current has gone the city cooks on bottled gas and reads by candles. On the night of the Harvest a candle burned under the Pillar in the only open window on a street of Parish 31, and it was still burning when the light had gone. The Night Market sells the gas bottles, filled.

Gas bottlesPOWER // THE TANKS

POWER // THE TANKS

Gas bottles

Steel bottles of gas, filled by hand in the bottling shed at the Tanks and carried across the city on handcarts by the drivers of the Harbor and Freight Union. The light stops current and leaves a flame burning, so wherever the current has gone the city cooks and keeps warm on bottled gas, which burns blue. NOODLES has kept its pot on the boil on it since the night of the Parousia, by the cook’s account.

A bottle costs two charged batteries, or one with an empty bottle back, at the price the Tide Table prints every week, and the Night Market sells them on below the street. The drivers say the Tanks will last twenty years, and the Exchange says ten.

BatteriesPOWER // EVERYONE

POWER // EVERYONE

Batteries

Power is the second currency below the street, and a charged battery buys a meal anywhere in Pan. Chargers fill them in the dark on keyed lines and sell the charge on, keeping one battery in five for charging the rest. A charged battery lights a room for a few hours a night, and in the Wards that is all the current most rooms have.

POWER // THE TARES

Live current

Keyed current run through or along consecrated stone slows its creep, because the stone cannot grow into a cipher, so the Tares fight the Consecration with extension cords and keep lights burning at its edges out of spite.

WATER AND FOOD // THE HARBOR

Hand pumps

Iron pumps on the quays and in the squares of Old Nod, older than the charter, which the Company left standing as ornaments when it piped the city. The city’s pumps died with the grid, and by noon on the first morning there were queues at the hand pumps. Old Nod drinks from them still, the Wards fill tanks on their roofs from the rain, and the parishes drink by Grace from the tower on the heights.

Rafts

Floating beds on the flooded floors of the Stillwater halls, where the Tares grow food under keyed lamps: greens and beans, and mushrooms in the dark ends of the halls. Pan eats what grows on them, what the boats bring through the Breach from the undercities of other ports, and what the Exchange can buy.

TRANSIT

The Charter LineTRANSIT // THE CHARTER LINE

TRANSIT // THE CHARTER LINE

The Charter Line

The city’s transit. Its lines met under Station Circle in Central Station, with tunnels under Old Nod, Chrome Row and the Flats, branches under the uptown and the Terraces, and lines out to the Wards and the airfield, and its trams ran down the long roads of the Wards and were kept at night in the sheds of the Sidings. The branches are closed, and the lines out have been dark since the power died.

The white stone closed the stations of the branches as it grew over the ground above them. Its tunnels under the Stones, the Row and the Flats are Pan now.

The tramsTRANSIT // THE CHARTER LINE

TRANSIT // THE CHARTER LINE

The trams

The Charter Line’s trams, which ran on rails down the middle of the long roads of the Wards, under a wire. The wire ran in the open, and every tram in the city stopped at 03:12:46 wherever it was. A few still stand where they stopped, with people living in them, and the last Four stands forty meters short of Lanes Top.

LiftsTRANSIT // EVERYONE

TRANSIT // EVERYONE

Lifts

Every lift in Nodus ran on the grid, and at 03:12:46 every one of them stopped where it was, with whoever was in it. Nobody has counted how many that was. The city has climbed its towers on foot since. The Babel Line is fought on the stairs.

Cars and lorriesTRANSIT // EVERYONE

TRANSIT // EVERYONE

Cars and lorries

Every car and lorry in Nodus ran on its own battery, sealed in its body, so at 03:12:46 they kept running, and over the weeks that followed they stopped one by one as their charge ran out. Most of them stand where they stopped. The charge for a car would light a family’s room for a season, and the city would rather have the light, so it walks and carries its loads in handcarts.

In Pan the Tares run carts on the old Charter Line rails, keyed like everything else down there.

The proof cameraIMAGING // BRIGHTWALL

IMAGING // BRIGHTWALL

The proof camera

A camera Brightwall set on a mast across the street from each of its screens, to prove that the screen showed what Brightwall was paid to show. In PROOF it matched the screen against the schedule frame by frame and counted Brightwall’s sites on Earth, every one of them proved in the same second. The face of Babel had its own on the roof of a glass tower across the plaza, level with the face’s upper floors.

On the first night of the Leak all 1,204 of Brightwall’s sites on Earth failed in about a second, and the proof camera on the plaza watched Brightwall try five times to take the face of Babel back.

KeysCRYPTOGRAPHY // THE TARES

CRYPTOGRAPHY // THE TARES

Keys

A secure element implanted under the skin, holding a number that exists nowhere else. A keyed soul is ciphertext to the Watchers and a blank in the Book of Life. A key hides who you are, and the body still shows where you are. The glyph the key raises on the skin is the Mark. A key runs on a cell charged by the body’s own chemistry, so it never needs a socket, and it goes on working under the salt.

PadsCRYPTOGRAPHY // THE TARES

CRYPTOGRAPHY // THE TARES

Pads

Ciphers on paper: two matching pads of random numbers, one for each party, each sheet used once and burned after. A letter written through a pad is a page of numbers to anyone else, the light included, since the light cannot read a cipher, and the sorting office carries it like any other letter. The Tares write most of their letters this way.

Directive 12 takes every cipher out of the parishes, so a page of numbers found in a parish home is a concealment. The keyed write to their families in white in plain words, and a carrier will read the letter aloud at the doorway if asked.
