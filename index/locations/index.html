Old Nod

SHOW ON THE MAP

The oldest ground in the city, above the old harbor on the west shore of the bay, with narrow streets that follow no plan anyone has found, and magenta light. The narrowest of them, in the west of the district, are called the Narrows. It was the harbor town before the charter, and on the night of the Parousia more of its windows burned on than in all the rest of the city together. Every line in it is keyed, and its windows burn all night. CAM 07 still stands on its mast above one of its roofs.

The city calls it the Stones, after the black stones the crews found under the harbor when they dug the cable vaults. Within a year of the charter most of it belonged to the Cainites, who had kept the location of Nod for eighteen hundred years and came to live on top of it. Every cable laid into Nodus runs through the Stones.

The Stones are the ground of the Fowlers, the Forgers and the Ridden, the Tide Table is printed on the quays, Lazarus keeps its first station behind a door in the Stones, Lintel’s Dooryard hangs the parishes’ old doors on the Stones’ houses, and the Naming House keeps its door open on the Street Between.

The House of Night’s quarter under Old Nod, a warren of the old harbor’s vaults hung with keyed lamps, where its clubs, its birth clinics and the rooms of the Ridden are. It is the loudest place in Pan and the most open, and anyone who walks in is welcome until they break a pact.

The vaults held the harbor’s bonded wine and tobacco under the charter, and the Cainites owned them through Stonecutter. The House of Night took them by pact in the year of the Concord.

Nepenthe sells its doses here, the Return is round the corner, and every child of the Tares born in Pan is keyed in its first week in a clinic two vaults in from the stair.

The station was the busiest on the harbor side under the charter. Its smiths set up their first anvil on the down platform in P+5, beside the trackbed.

A wall of the forge carries every blade the Nephilim brought back bent, each hung with the floor of Babel it bent on.

HALL FOUR
