Hell’s only law. GleaningHEAVEN

HEAVEN

Gleaning

The lesser harvest taken from the corners of the field every year since P+7, at 03:12 on the anniversary of the Harvest. Heaven names it for the law of the corners and the gleanings, which forbids the owner of a field to reap them and leaves them “for the poor and stranger” (Leviticus 19:9-10), and the Gleaning is the owner’s. Heaven left the field three years after the Harvest and took the first Gleaning in the fourth, by the law in the same chapter on the fruit of a newly planted tree, which says “in the fourth year all the fruit thereof shall be holy” (Leviticus 19:23-24). It gathers in one night, every parish in the same second. At 03:12 the Hum stops dead, the rain goes up, the Pillar comes down where the Wheel looks and the fourth Trumpet sounds, and the Hum stays silent until 03:12:45, when the ripe of every parish rise at once, lifted by their own halos. Trumpets, the

Seven tones heard across the whole city, each an order (Revelation 8). The first gave the Covenant at Lauds on the first day of P+1, the second sounded over the Harvest of P+3, the third named the key the Mark of the Beast in P+4, the fourth called the first Gleaning in P+7 and sounds again over every Gleaning, the fifth sent the Seraphim down on Babel at Matins in P+9, and the sixth brought Michael down at Lauds the same day. Pan calls each Trumpet a fart from the Ringpiece and counts them on its fingers, and the parishes kneel to the sound as to their own names called.

Wake, the

The rite at a death. Wards, theEVERYONE

EVERYONE

Wards, the

The housing the Charter Company put up fast in the boom years, north and west of the Terraces and east beyond the Flats, block after block on grids of its own, numbered and then named by the street, which calls them the Kilns, the Sidings, Nine Acres, Westfield and the Top Road in the North Wards, and Turbine Row, the Tanks, Airside and Longacre in the East. They held half the city at the census and hold more than half of it now. Most of their people took the Seal, and every block holds someone who refused or someone keyed, so the stone has never taken a block of them, only the ground-floor flats of the Rookery on the White’s edge, which knelt whole and went white whole. Nobody holds them, the Tares run no lines there, and their rooms are lit a few hours a night on batteries charged in the dark. To the parishes the Wards are the mission field, and Deacons go up the dark stairwells with manna for the sealed and tracts for the rest, and the tracts come back down the stairwell as arse paper.

Watcher

An angel that reads. The Watchers are numbered, and each holds a district of the city. By day they hang in a ring about the Wheel, and at Compline each goes out low over its district and moves through it all night, reading souls through their eyes against the Book of Life within about three hundred meters and seeing every Seal in the district as a point of light through any wall but mesh. The sealed undress before their Watcher without shame, since it reads them in the bath already, and the Kilns hang their underwear off the roofs at Vespers, crotch to the sky, for it to read.

Whiteout, the

The full light of the Wheel, resting on the city’s center at 03:12:50 on the night of the Parousia, two seconds after the flash that read the city. Where it rested it burned by sheer heat.
