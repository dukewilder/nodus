ConcordHELL

HELL

Concord

Hell’s only law, in four articles: no Court accepts worship; no pact without consent, and no pact forever; no Court binds another; and what is hidden stays hidden. A Court that breaks it is exposed, its keys published and itself left standing in the Throne’s sight. That has happened twice.

ConsecrationHEAVEN

HEAVEN

Consecration

The Throne’s light set in stone. It grew over the old uptown in the forty days after the Parousia, and over the Terraces in the year after. It takes ground only when everyone living on it has knelt, it creeps from its edges everywhere else, and it stops current.

CovenTHE TARES

THE TARES

Coven

A crew of the Tares, up to thirteen strong, the old witches’ number and the number at the Last Supper. A coven has no leader, and covens cooperate by pact, as the Courts do. The oldest in Nodus is the Cainites, and the city knows the Salt Widows, the Gleaners, the Babel Line Company, the Nephilim and the Low Choir by name.

Covenant, theHEAVEN

HEAVEN

Covenant, the

Heaven’s law for the Faithful, given out as numbered Directives. Every instrument of the Covenant exists to open something: confession opens the mind, Witness opens the neighbor, and Directive 12 took the door off every parish home in P+1.

THE FAITHFUL

Deacon

The one in white who answers for a parish, with a few deacons under them. Deacons are human, and the good ones are kind. When a Directive appears cut into the wall of a parish hall, its Deacon reads it aloud at the next Hour.

Deep End, theEVERYONE

EVERYONE

Deep End, the

Going darkEVERYONE

Going whiteTHE TARES

Losing a key, and with it the dark: whoever goes white is readable again. Keys are never shared, since a shared key is only a Seal with extra steps.

GRACEHEAVEN

GRACE

Heaven’s score of a soul, the second line of a Watcher’s reading, which a Watcher takes from the Seal. For a keyed soul the line stays blank.

H

HaloHEAVEN

HEAVEN

Halo

A ring of light that grows over the head of anyone who takes the Seal, as bright as its bearer’s Grace. It carries their prayers up, and it comes off only by surgery, which the Mercy clinics refuse. In the dark stairwells of the Wards it is the only light there is.

Halo-lightTHE WARDS

THE WARDS

Halo-light

The light of a halo, which in the stairwells of the Wards is the only light there is. The sealed go up by their own, the unsealed follow them, and the keyed climb in the dark.

HarvestHEAVEN

HEAVEN

Harvest

Heaven’s reaping of the ripe, in the words of its own book: “Thrust in thy sickle, and reap: for the time is come for thee to reap; for the harvest of the earth is ripe” (Revelation 14:15). A Harvest gathers for days. The rain rises first, then dust and paper and loose things, and on the night the ripe rise into the light, still kneeling, under a horn the size of the sky. The taken go up whole and stay gone. Nodus has had one, at P+3, which took 2.1 million of nine million in one night, and Heaven has come back for a Gleaning every year since P+7.

Harvest weatherEVERYONE

Days when the Wheel’s attention sharpens and the rain falls upward.

THE TARES

Hours, the

Hum, theEVERYONE

Hundred and twenty, the

The length of a human life in Genesis 6:3, set in the chapter that brings the Watchers down: “yet his days shall be an hundred and twenty years.” In the last year of the charter 3,112 people in Nodus were past it. Heaven counts every day past it as stolen.

MannaTHE FAITHFUL

THE FAITHFUL

Manna

The ration: thin white wafers, tasteless and complete, which dull the appetite (Exodus 16). It lies on consecrated stone with the dew before Lauds, the parish halls gather it and give it out by Grace, and kept overnight it spoils (Exodus 16:20). It goes only to the sealed, and stolen manna sells in the Night Market the same night.

The unit in which a Harvest is counted: 88,610 of them over Nodus at P+3. Nobody on Earth knows what a measure is.

The taking out of implants in the white halls of Zion Heights, given to the Faithful who repent between Prime and Compline, and given once. The same halls take them off the Tree. One patient in nine dies of the mercy.

A leaf whose first writing was scraped away so it could be written over. The older text survives underneath.

PanTHE TARES

THE TARES

Pan

Pandæmonium, the city under the city: the tunnels and stations of the Charter Line, the utility vaults, the cable galleries, the storm drains and the drowned halls of the Flats, joined into one place and lined with keyed mesh. Milton gave the capital of Hell the name. Nobody in Pan is watched unless they sign for it, and nobody there uses an indexed name.

Where everything burned arrives. The scrolls of Alexandria, the books of the Cathars, the notebooks of women burned as witches and the hardware of every Burn Day are shelved there, and none of it was lost. Shades keep it, and it keeps reading rooms in Pan.
