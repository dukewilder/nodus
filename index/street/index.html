How Nod lives day to day, what it pays and earns, how it keeps the year, and the stories it tells.

LIVING

A day by the Hours

LIVING

A day by the Hours

Nodus keeps Heaven’s clock, whatever side it is on. Lauds brings the light and the manna, and the sealed kneel at every Hour after it, at Prime, Terce, Sext, None and Vespers, wherever they stand, in the street, at a bench or on a stair. By day the white streets and the parishes are Heaven’s, the keyed keep to Pan and to rooms lined with mesh, and the Wards go about their work among the sealed with their heads down.

Compline at nine is the turn. The Pillar comes down on the Burn and begins its round, the parishes go in, Pan comes up, and the Stones light their windows. Matins at midnight finds the Night Market at its loudest and the Gleaners in the parishes. Most of the city sleeps between Matins and Lauds, and the rest is working.

Food and drink

LIVING

Food and drink

The Faithful eat manna, white and complete, and the Covenant asks nothing else of them at table. The Wards eat what they can buy, Leaven’s bread, eel and mussels from the bay, rice and beans the Breachers bring, greens from the Tares’ rafts, and wafer when the week runs short. Pan eats better than the surface and pays for it in batteries.

Noodles at the top of the Row, from a pot that has not been emptied since P-17, are the city’s one dish everybody eats. The drink of the Wards is rain gin, distilled in the Kilns from whatever ferments, and Pan drinks what the Breachers land. A meal in Pan is a battery, a loaf of Leaven half of one.

Where people live

LIVING

Where people live

Nodus climbs its stairs. Every lift stopped at 03:12:46 and none has run since, so the high floors of the Wards went cheap and the low floors dear, and a family’s place in a block is counted from the street. The sealed go up the stairwells by their own halo-light, and the rest follow them or climb in the dark.

The Sidings live in the Charter Line’s cars, Hangar Town in the airfield’s hangars, the Deep End on the floors above the water and the Rookery a floor lower every year. The parishes live with no doors. Pan lives in the stations, vaults and drains under the city, in rooms lined with mesh, and it has never been full.

Water, power and fire

LIVING

Water, power and fire

Water comes from Cistern by the jug, from the parishes’ blessed cisterns or straight off the roof. Power comes in three kinds. Current runs only where it is keyed, on Tidewright’s lines, the Tappers’ taps and Darkwell’s batteries, and the light passes over it there and leaves it running. Gas from the Tanks burns anywhere, by day and under the Pillar, since the light leaves a flame alone. Tallow’s candles light the rest.

About four and a half million people in Nodus live without current, by halo-light and flame. A block of the Wards on the Tappers’ current glows at night on its seams, and a parish glows by its own halos.

MONEY AND WORK

Prices

MONEY AND WORK

Prices

Silver is the Exchange’s money, kept on the Ledger, and a thirty is the standard bundle. A charged battery trades at about one silver and buys a meal anywhere in Pan, so the Wards price everything in batteries and Pan in silver. The Tide Table prints the week’s rates, and the Flotilla says they are always wrong.

A job

MONEY AND WORK

A job

Work worth silver in Nodus is a pact. Whoever wants something done goes to a surety at the Undertow, the surety names a price and finds a runner, the terms are read twice and entered on the Ledger, and the surety takes a tenth. The work can be a battery run across the Penumbra, a door in Babel, a Teller’s name or a crate off a Lowtide hull.

A runner who delivers is paid at the bar. Failure leaves the runner owing the surety and the client both, and the Bailiffs call. The Wards say a job is the only thing in Nodus read twice before it kills you.

Work

MONEY AND WORK

Work

The sealed work where their Grace assigns them, at Bethel Works, in the parish halls, on the Wardens’ gates or in the Granary, and they are paid in Grace. Everyone else works at whatever pays. The Wards’ trades are charging, carrying, baking, bottling gas, fitting keys, mending Lockets, ferrying, diving and running, and the Union carries the city on foot.

Most people in the Wards work three trades and sleep in shifts. A runner works for sureties, a Spare for the Shambles, a Quarryman for the Concourse and a Teller for a Deacon.

Debt

MONEY AND WORK

Debt

Debt in Pan is a pact, and every pact is on the Ledger, so every debt in Pan is known. A debtor works it off, sells it on to a surety or a Spare’s buyer, or pays it with a term of service, which the Concord allows for a fixed term and never forever. The Bailiffs collect what the terms name and nothing more.

Debt above the street is Grace. A sealed household that falls behind on it loses its rooms, its work and its place in the parish order, and the Wards say a white owes more than anyone in Pan and never sees the bill.

THE WEEK AND THE YEAR

Seven days

THE WEEK AND THE YEAR

Seven days

The first day of the week is the Lord’s Day, when every parish kneels in its hall at Terce and hears its Grace in Tongues. The second is market day at the Concourse and the Flotilla. The sixth is Burn Day, when the Faithful carry the week’s surrendered hardware to the Burn from Vespers and the fire burns from Compline to Lauds, and the Kindlers work the smoke and the Pit opens at the Tanks.

The Tide Table goes up on the walls of the Stones on the seventh, over the week before.

A year in Nod

THE WEEK AND THE YEAR

A year in Nod

The year turns on Heaven’s dates and the Tares’. The anniversary of the Harvest brings the Gleaning at 03:12, when the parishes kneel on their thresholds and the ripe go up, and the Gleaners work the weeks before it. Leak Night, when the Restored Genesis is read aloud in Pan, and Cain’s Day, when nobody asks anyone where anyone is, are the Tares’ own.

The Salt Widows read their book of the salted on the Sea Steps on Cain’s Day. The Babel Line Company re-signs its pact at the foot of the Long Wet at midwinter, and the Wards keep the night of the Parousia with candles in every window, sealed and unsealed alike.

NEWS AND NAMES

News

NEWS AND NAMES

News

Nodus learns what happened from three places. The Tide Table, pasted up on the walls every week, prints the tides, the Pillar’s hours, the prices and the missing and the dead. Radio Pandæmonium, on every keyed set after Compline, plays the Low Tuning and reads what the Tares want known. The walls of Pan, Brightwall’s screens along the platforms, sell what Pan has to sell between the notices.

The parishes hear their news from the Deacon at the Hour, and in Tongues on the Lord’s Day. Everyone hears the rest in the queue for bread.

Names Pan knows

NEWS AND NAMES

Names Pan knows

Every city has its famous, and Nodus’s are in the dark. Pan knows the Babel Line’s fighters by their handles, the Pit’s champions by the names painted on the tank, Odile Marsh by her pacts and Yusuf Carr by his runs. Above the street the Faithful know Sister Agathe Rook of the Threshers, and the Wards know the Deacons by street, the kind ones first.

A handle is lowercase, as the Tares write theirs, and a name painted white in the Pit is a name for the dead.

LEGENDS

Floor forty-five

LEGENDS

Floor forty-five

The Rookery has forty-four floors. The block says that on some nights its stairwell has a forty-fifth, above the stone, with a light under the door, and that whoever knocks is answered by their own voice. Nobody who says they have climbed to it will say what the voice said.

Last bottle

LEGENDS

Last bottle

The Tanks say one bottle in the shed has never been empty since the first winter. It is passed from hand to hand on the nights the farm runs dry, and it lights one burner and one oven every time. The Tankmen will not say which bottle it is, or whether it exists.

A man asking the way

LEGENDS

A man asking the way

Old Nod has had no Watcher since P+5. The Narrows say that one walks them still as a man, very tall and very polite, who stops people at corners and asks the way to a street that is not there anymore, and thanks them by name.

White children

LEGENDS

White children

The Wards that face the White say that on clear nights children are seen walking into the stone, in single file, and walking out of it at Lauds pale to the hair. Nobody has lost a child that way and everybody knows a family that has. The Quarrymen do not laugh at the story.

A dry room

LEGENDS

A dry room

Pan tells of a room somewhere under the Burn whose walls are lined with keyed mesh and whose ceiling the Pillar has been seen to light, once, through a crack in the street above. Whoever sleeps in it, they say, wakes with their key gone silent and their name in the Book of Life. Every crew in Pan has looked for it, and every one says it has found the wrong room.

The Fortieth Warden

LEGENDS

The Fortieth Warden

The story in Blackgate is that on the fortieth day one Warden stood in Charter House while the board knelt round him, and that the light came down on him and left him standing, unread and unsealed, and that he walked out through the forecourt past the Cherub at the gate. Every recruit is told he founded Blackgate. Its officers say nothing either way.

OUTSIDE

Other ports

OUTSIDE

Other ports

Nodus is one of many cities with an undercity. The cables land elsewhere too, and the Exchange’s silver is spent in the dark under other ports, which the Breachers and Lowtide Line reach through the Breach and the undernet reaches through Babel’s vault. Their boards hold Stillwater and Brightwall, their grafts come in on Lowtide’s hulls, and their news comes in on the Mesh.

What the world outside is like above its streets, Nodus knows mostly from the Vagabonds and the Breachers, and they disagree.

The Bounds

OUTSIDE

The Bounds

The line the charter drew round the bay and the land about it, marked by stones on every road out of the city. The Wheel’s station is over the center of the city and its Pillar walks the streets, and outside the Bounds there are farms, old roads, the Vagabonds’ camps and a quiet that the Wards find hard to sleep in.

People do leave. The Vagabonds carry them out from the Waystation for silver, and some write back.

Why people stay

OUTSIDE

Why people stay

Most of Nodus has never been anywhere else and the rest came for the light law, and both have family here, in white or below the street or in the Gardens. Pan has silver, work and a war, and the Wards say that a city the Throne watches this hard must have something in it worth watching. The Faithful stay because the Throne is here.

The Tares’ answer is shorter. Nod was the first city, and they mean it to be the last one standing.
