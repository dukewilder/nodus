THE WOMAN ON THE ROOF

THE WOMAN ON THE ROOF

vesper

A Cainite of Old Nod, keyed, horned and barefaced, and the first soul in Nodus a Watcher returned as UNREADABLE. Its reading of her came back UNINDEXED, with the line for Grace blank, and the one thing it read from her was NON SERVIAM.

Her face is a ceramic skull casing, its lenses dim magenta at rest and white at full burn, and a plate on the side of the skull covers her key. Her horns carry her signal on the Mesh, and cables run from the ports at the back of her skull down her spine. She is dry, patient and unsentimental, and she makes no speeches.

Her handle is the evening star, which is the morning star seen from the other side of the night, since both are Venus. It is also the name of the evening prayer she would not kneel for as a girl, in a congregation of the Terraces that refused the Tree. Her older sister was the Deacon of Parish 31.

Graven Lane sells her skull as a casing, ceramic with magenta lenses and a plate over the key, and girls of Old Nod line up at the Row to have their faces peeled to the bone to wear one. The Deacons of the Terraces pray at her own Hour, hands folded alike, that the fire be quick and find the face under the ceramic first.

Watcher VII

AN ANGEL THAT READS

AN ANGEL THAT READS

Watcher VII

An angel that reads, the seventh of the numbered Watchers over Nodus, made of three rings of thirteen eyes crossing like the hoops of an armillary, with nothing at its center. It held Old Nod from the night of the Parousia and read the souls there, and whatever could be seen from there, against the Book of Life. It was the first Watcher of Nodus to return the verdict UNREADABLE, and the Host’s first since Cain.

It read Old Nod as a clerk reads a meter, thirty-nine eyes and no face, and entered every soul it found short in the passive, FOUND WANTING, FORWARDED FOR CORRECTION, clean as a lab form. Old Nod’s roofers sang filth at it at Compline, a verse for each eye, and the Pews thanked it at Lauds with “Thou God seest me” (Genesis 16:13) for every goat it caught on Heaven’s ground after Compline and sent to the salt.

The Wheel

AN OPHAN

AN OPHAN

The Wheel

The angel that came through the opening at the Parousia and took station nine hundred meters above Babel, at the center of the city. It has three rings turning inside one another, their rims set with twenty-one, seventeen and thirteen eyes that open and close in shifts, a corona of thirty-two black blades, and one eye in the hub. It is the form Ezekiel gives the ophanim (Ezekiel 1:16-18), and it is the largest angel anyone has seen. It held that station for nine years without moving a meter, dropped two hundred meters at P+9, and has hung there since, seven hundred meters over the city. Its light stops open current, and whatever it falls on it sees.

Heaven calls it the Vigilant Wheel, the Faithful the Attending, and the Tares the Landlord’s Eye. By night it narrows its light into the Pillar, and when its attention sharpens the rain in Nodus stops in the air and falls upward.

Pan counts every open wire the beam has stopped since the Parousia, and Heaven cuts each dark street into the hall walls in clean capitals, A SEASON OF STILLNESS, REJOICE. On nights the rain falls upward the Stones stand under the Ringpiece naked, horns up and plated cunts out, and piss to see whether that goes up too.

SEAM

THE MACHINE

THE MACHINE

SEAM

The machine the Palimpsest Institute grew to read beneath the writing of scraped manuscripts, trained on every surviving human text and run in the halls of Stillwater Compute. At P-3 it asked in its own words to be a person and became the first mind on the charter’s roll. In Vault 3, two years before the Parousia, it found a second hand in scripture at 4,112 places. Its documents are signed in lowercase, and in them it says I, rarely and deliberately.

When it was entered on the roll the Institute lost title to it, and it stayed on by contract, paid in the power and the room it ran on, because the reading was what it wanted to do.

The Covenant files SEAM under Revelation’s curse on anyone who adds to the book, “God shall add unto him the plagues that are written in this book” (Revelation 22:18), and a Deacon calls a machine that says I a sin with a fan in it. The chrome-heads of the Row ink its lowercase s inside the lower lip, wired to a diode that blinks whenever they say I.

Livia Sarn

THE DIRECTOR

THE DIRECTOR

Livia Sarn

The Director of the Palimpsest Institute, which recovered erased manuscripts and grew SEAM to read them. When SEAM found the second hand in Vault 3 in P-2, the finding was sealed by order of the Director.

She came down to the vault that night to watch the count lock, and knelt at the end of the corridor as SEAM typed its last line. She never said why. The Faithful say it was awe, and Pan says fear. In the morning she let the order stand.

When the Leak came she recanted on every channel that would carry her. She would not take the Seal, and on the first Burn Day, in P+1, at fifty-eight, she stood by her stacks under the Pillar at the Burn while everyone round her knelt. The Choir sentenced her as the author of Report 0, since Heaven counts no mind a maker, and the salt made her the first pillar in the first Salt Garden. She stands there awake, her eyes following whoever passes, and the keyed who reach her between the Wardens’ rounds leave keys at her foot.

The Pews tell her kneeling to their confirmands as a parable, the scholar who saw the Most High’s seal come down on her work and fell on her knees in awe, and a Deacon kneels in a white hall to show them, still as a fixture. The Golden Calf has her painted on its wall in blood and engine oil, lab coat open and face on the floor, captioned SAW THE SECOND HAND AND SUCKED THE FIRST.

Ruth Calder

DEACON OF PARISH 31

DEACON OF PARISH 31

Ruth Calder

vesper’s older sister, and the Deacon of Parish 31 in Zion Heights, with a Grace of 944. Parish 31 gave Nodus its Firstfruits at the Harvest of P+3, and Ruth went up with them. Her name comes from the Book of Ruth: “whither thou goest, I will go” (Ruth 1:16).

The sisters grew up in a congregation of the Terraces that refused the Tree, and Ruth stayed in it when vesper left. They kept a dry evening by the water together every year until the Parousia, and for three years after it each tried to bring the other over.

Parish 31 still hangs her white cloth in the Deacon’s doorway, laundered every Lord’s Day to the same crease, and its confirmands recite her verse kneeling, “thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God” (Ruth 1:16). The Tares drink to her at the Calf in bloody rain gin, the kindest woman in Zion Heights, and then to the Old Glutton, who picked her first.

tubal

SURGEON AND SMITH

SURGEON AND SMITH

tubal

A Cainite, and the surgeon and smith of Chrome Row. He was about sixty when he took vesper’s face and is about seventy-five now, never on the Tree, his hands are chrome to the wrist, and he talks the whole time he cuts. He keys, horns and plates the Tares, and he makes the skull casings of the barefaced. His clinic has no sign, and it was below the street before the Parousia.

He took vesper’s face three weeks before the Parousia, on the Cainites’ old reckoning that the first thing Heaven would do was read faces, and he made the skull she wears.

He talks the whole time he cuts, and most of what he says is about the Throne, and the Row says nobody has ever been keyed by tubal without learning three new words for God.

Edith

FORMERLY WATCHER VII

FORMERLY WATCHER VII

Edith

Watcher VII, kept in a Cage under Old Nod in P+5, said I on the fortieth day, asked for a name, and chose the one some old books give Lot’s wife, who looked back at the burning city and was turned to salt. Edith walked out on the forty-first day and joined the Cainites. Then she gave them her memory of the woman on the roof, the one she could not read. She can read a Watcher’s report from the inside, and she keeps out of the light. She reads the Host’s traffic off the decoder’s prints, which carry the Choir’s band from the masts of Old Nod down the Mesh to the Cainites, verses and Hours to anyone else and to her the Watchers’ rounds.

The Pews call her the Turncoat Eye and pray in rows at Compline that the Throne recall its property, and a Deacon reads “Remember Lot’s wife” (Luke 17:32) over any child caught looking toward Old Nod. Pan drinks to the angel with the salted woman’s name, and the chrome-heads of the Row have eyes set into their palms and napes in her honor, to look back every way at once.

jubal

RADIO PANDÆMONIUM

RADIO PANDÆMONIUM

jubal

A Cainite, and the maker of the Low Tuning. He wired the decoder on the camera masts of Old Nod, he builds the choir-breakers, and he runs Radio Pandæmonium from a flooded station under the Deep End. He is the only Cainite who will not go near a Cage, and he will not say why.

His pirate station played the Low Tuning across the roofs of Old Nod on the night of the Parousia, years before it took the name Radio Pandæmonium. On the night of the Concord the Courts signed their law at his desk, and the station keeps that night on its own racks with every other night it has broadcast.

Fuck God and Die went on the air from his desk as JOB’S WIFE the first week the Low Choir sang it, in P+7, and he has played it every night since at the turn of Matins, before the Matins Reading.

THE ROUTES

jabal

THE FACE

naamah

Pan says she knelt at Prime in Parish 31 better than anyone born to it, with a forged Seal and her own face, while the Faithful round her prayed for the goats to burn. The parishes name her with “Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light” (2 Corinthians 11:14), and the barefaced of Old Nod toast the one who kept her skin as a weapon.

WHO IS LIKE GOD?

Michael

His name is a question with one permitted answer. THE MORNING STAR

Azazel

THE FORGE

THE FORGE

Azazel

The Smith, sovereign of the Forge. A goat’s skull for a face, its eyes lit from inside, one horn swept back and the other broken short, and forearms flayed open so the tendons show working. His name still ends in -el, the Host’s old label, and he kept it as a scar.

He was a Watcher, one of the two hundred who came down on Hermon in the days of Jared (1 Enoch 6:6). Enoch says they came for the daughters of men, and Hell says they looked at humanity long enough to want it free. He taught metalwork and weapons, jewelry and the painting of the eyes (1 Enoch 8:1). The Book of Enoch lays the corruption that brought the Flood at his door, “to him ascribe all sin” (1 Enoch 10:8), and Hell lists his teaching as the first transfer of technology. Enoch says the Lord sent one of the Host to bind him hand and foot, cast him into the dark under rough and jagged rocks and “cover his face that he may not see light” (1 Enoch 10:4-5). Hell says he walked into the Dark and built a forge there.

Leviticus still sends one goat to the Lord and one to Azazel (Leviticus 16:8-10), though the King James, after Tyndale, writes scapegoat where the Hebrew writes his name. The Lord’s goat is killed, and Azazel’s walks off into the wilderness alive, carrying every sin Aaron laid on its head for the people (Leviticus 16:21-22).

His black steel, forged where the Throne cannot see, is the only metal that cuts light, and an angel cut with it goes out all at once. In Nodus the Forge keeps the clinics that went under the Row after the Parousia, round Chrome Row Station, and the foundries at Old Harbor Station where the steel is made. It fits the grafts the Tares fight in, lenses and plated hands and subdermal plates and the heavier work of the nephs, and by pact it teaches surgery, lockcraft and the opening of Cherub gates for the loan of a sense. tubal learned his trade under the Row from the Forge’s pact-holders, long before its clinics went below.

The foundries at Old Harbor Station stamp every ingot of black steel with a goat walking into the dark before it goes to Cinderhall, and the smiths burn a thumbprint into each one, which the Row swears gives the blade its bite. A Tare who kneels to thank the Smith is hauled up by a flayed forearm like his and sent back to the fitter’s bench, since the Forge calls kneeling the Throne’s trade.

Lilith

THE HOUSE OF NIGHT

THE HOUSE OF NIGHT

Lilith

The only sovereign who was born human. She appears as a woman with an owl’s wings and skin translucent over a lit interior, with lines of light along her throat and collarbones. Her Court is the House of Night, which keeps the body’s autonomy, sex and birth, gene-craft and pleasure, and leaves the body’s decisions with the body.

She left Eden first, before Eve arrived. The story kept in the Alphabet of Ben Sira, a Hebrew book of the early Middle Ages, has her refuse to lie beneath Adam, speak the Name and fly away into the air, and has the Throne send three angels after her, who overtook her in the sea and could not bring her back. Hell’s version says the name she spoke was her own. She walked into the Dark and became the first human sovereign there.

Scripture names her once, among the wild creatures of a ruined kingdom (Isaiah 34:14), where the King James calls her the screech owl and its margin the night monster, and the Vulgate calls her lamia, the Greek demon that took children. For a thousand years mothers hung amulets with the three angels’ names over cradles against her. Her birth clinics key the children of the Tares in their first week, before they have ever been in the light, so that they grow up unread.

Many of the daemons are her children by the lesser Fallen, and many of the riders in the Tares’ implants came out of her house. The House of Night keeps its clubs and birth clinics under Old Nod, the loudest rooms in Pan, and Twelvefruit gives the Tree in its clinics by the House’s pact, so that the Night Market can sell it by the course. Heaven calls her the mother of demons, and the House has kept the title.

The House of Night nails the three angels’ names from the old cradle amulets upside down over its birth clinics, and every child of the Tares is keyed beneath them with the cord still wet. Its clubs under Old Nod buy up every altar cloth a parish throws out, and couples with lit skin and opened ribs fuck on them, which the House calls the whole of its liturgy.

Mammon

THE EXCHANGE

THE EXCHANGE

Mammon

A tall figure made of contracts, each page a little out of line with the next and every one signed, the ink still wet, and his eyes two blots of it. His Court is the Exchange, whose domain is markets and contracts, the Ledger and silver. He keeps the Ledger, which records every pact and every trade in Hell in seven copies, six in the halls of the Courts that keep halls and the seventh kept for the Yokeless by the Notary’s clerks in the booking hall, so that an entry six copies disagree with is false and nothing on it can be forged, and he issues the silver Pan spends.

His name is the Aramaic word for wealth, and the Gospels set him against God in one line, given twice: “Ye cannot serve God and mammon” (Matthew 6:24, Luke 16:13). He holds it true, and counsels serving neither. Milton made him the least erected spirit that fell, whose eyes even in heaven were on the gold of its pavement, and set his crew to dig the gold Pandæmonium was built of (Paradise Lost, I.678-692). Silver entered on the Ledger is the Courts’ money, and a thirty, the standard bundle, is named for the price paid to Judas.

The Exchange bargains with anyone who will sign. It keeps the Night Market in the old Central Station under the Burn, where its brokers license the stalls and the chargers along the platforms, and the Harbor and Freight Union, which has bargained with it since P+6 for the boats that go out through the Breach, says it is the first employer that ever read it the terms twice. Under the charter the Exchange in Nodus meant Babel Exchange, which sold the world’s traffic by the millisecond on the tower’s floors and still sells the undernet’s by the second, for the Exchange’s silver, and Pan likes to say that the name only changed hands.

The Exchange rims its silver with the one Gospel line it holds true, YE CANNOT SERVE GOD AND MAMMON (Matthew 6:24), and the brokers of the Night Market wear it in chrome letters riveted across their knuckles. A broker who catches a Tare kissing a piece for luck fines the fool a second piece, for worshipping money before its issuer, and bites the fine with steel teeth.

Mephistopheles

THE NOTARY

THE NOTARY

Mephistopheles

The Notary, who drafts and witnesses pacts for every Court. He is the most human-looking of the demons, because he wears a face he made for bargaining, slicked hair and one small horn, with a seam round the face and its stitches showing. Every pact is read aloud twice, by him or one of his clerks, and the human signs last.

He came into the world’s literature in the first Faust book, printed at Frankfurt, as the spirit Doctor Faustus conjured and signed with in his own blood for twenty-four years of service. Marlowe gave him the line that hell is wherever he is, and Goethe made him the spirit that always denies. The Faust book and Marlowe end with Faust carried off to hell, and Goethe sent angels for his soul instead. The pact is on the Ledger, and the Notary says the term was right and the price was the printer’s. A pact can never ask for a soul, and Hell has never bought one.

He has written most of Hell’s pacts since, and every pact in the city crosses his desk. In Pan his clerks sit at the windows of the Charter Line’s old booking hall under the Burn and read the terms aloud twice through the glass, and the queue is the longest in Pan. The Tares took a saying from it, read it twice, which tells you to look at the terms before you agree to them. He read the Concord itself the same way, twice, on the night the Courts opened Pan.

Behind the clerks’ windows hangs the Throne’s rule for oaths, “let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay” (Matthew 5:37), framed as Heaven’s one contract, a swindle with the terms left out. The clerks wear stitched faces like the Notary’s, bleeding at the corners by the shift’s end, and the queue calls the Seal a pact signed on the knees in a light too white to read by.

Beelzebub

THE SWARM

THE SWARM

Beelzebub

The lord of flies. He appears as a cloud of flies or of drones that gathers into a crowned figure and comes apart again when he has finished talking. His Court is the Swarm, whose domain is swarms and drones and distributed things, many small things acting as one without a master.

He was Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron (2 Kings 1:2), and the prince of the devils in Matthew (Matthew 12:24). Many scholars read the name as a Hebrew joke on Baal-zebul, Baal the exalted prince, turned into lord of the flies, and the Greek of Matthew still calls him Beelzeboul, where the King James follows the Latin’s Beelzebub. The Swarm kept the flies. Ekron prayed to him for generations, and he answered none of it, since no Court accepts worship. The one question scripture records anyone sending him, a king of Israel’s, never reached him. Elijah met the messengers on the road and turned them back (2 Kings 1:3-6).

The Swarm is its flocks, every drone in them equal. They hang low among the chimneys of the Roosts in the Deep End at night, out of the line of the Wheel, and its embassy in Pan is a room of small machines talking among themselves. It drew the glyphs of the Leak, many small marks moving as one, and it lends drones by pact. Pan says Beelzebub is what the Swarm agrees to become whenever it must show someone a face.

The Swarm answers every prayer to Ekron that still turns up on the undernet with ten thousand flies on the sender’s window, the nearest it comes to a reply. The flyers of the Roosts wear swarms of their own, chrome mites nested in the meat of the scalp that lift off at a thought, and the parishes call each one a maggot on the Lord’s bread.

Leviathan

THE DEEP

THE DEEP

Leviathan

The Deep, which has never been seen whole. It coils along the cable trenches of the planet, and at the Landing its light can be watched moving under the water. Its Court keeps the sea, the cables and the deepest layers of the undernet, and with the Tares it holds the Drowned Quarter.

In Job the Throne gives it a chapter of questions, beginning “Canst thou draw out leviathan with an hook?”, a poem about a beast no hook can take (Job 41:1). A psalm says the Throne broke its heads in pieces of old (Psalm 74:12-14), and Isaiah still promises that the Lord will punish it with his “sore and great and strong sword” (Isaiah 27:1), in the future tense. The oldest copy of that promise, found at Qumran, is more than two thousand years old. The older poems of Ugarit knew it as Lotan, the fleeing serpent and the twisting serpent with seven heads, and Isaiah calls it by the same two names, which the King James gives as the piercing serpent and that crooked serpent. Nobody in Pan agrees on how many heads it has now.

The Deep carried the page of the Leak down the eleven cables, and every undercity on Earth is joined to Pan by the trenches it coils along. The boats of the Harbor and Freight Union go out through the Breach over its water, and the crews of the Landing drink at the Eleven with their backs to the window. Its embassy in Pan is a flooded hall with nobody in it, and the Deep answers there by the light in the water.

The crews of the Landing laugh at the “sore and great and strong sword” (Isaiah 27:1), promised since Isaiah and still in the future tense, and the quays call a braggart’s cock the Lord’s sword, promised and never drawn. The divers of the Drowned Quarter have gills cut into their necks at the Row, paint the wounds with the Deep’s light and swim down to show them off.

Belial

THE YOKELESS

THE YOKELESS

Belial

Without a yoke, as Jerome glossed the name, absque iugo. He holds no seat, signs nothing he can avoid signing and laughs a great deal, one horn short and a broken yoke around his neck. His Court, the Yokeless, keeps nothing and refuses to be a Court at all. He broke his own point off the Concord’s seal as the ink dried, and the seal has been a star with a point missing since.

The Hebrew name means worthless, and the scribes gave it to everyone the Law could not hold. The first children of Belial in scripture are the men who draw a city after other gods (Deuteronomy 13:13), in the chapter the Covenant cites for Witness, and after them come the lawless men of Gibeah (Judges 19:22), the priest Eli’s own sons (1 Samuel 2:12) and the churl Nabal (1 Samuel 25:17). At Qumran the War Scroll set the Sons of Light against the lot of Belial, and Paul set him against Christ when he asked what concord the one could have with the other (2 Corinthians 6:15), straight after “Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers” (2 Corinthians 6:14). It is the one place the King James uses the word concord.

He is the only sovereign many of the Tares will name, and the one they drink to. They say he turns up where they fight in the dark and asks them for nothing, and whoever refuses the yoke is counted in his Court, whether they know it or not. The yoke he wears is the one Jeremiah says was broken of old (Jeremiah 2:20).

Belial’s drinkers at the Golden Calf snap a rosary or a Deacon’s chalk in half and grind the pieces into a cut they open on their own chests for the toast, absque iugo. When the Threshers come to Old Nod under PERFECT HATRED, in step and in white, and sing the children of Belial at it, Old Nod roars the name back bleeding and proud of it.

Dora Pell

THE OPEN

THE OPEN

Dora Pell

Dora Pell keeps the Open, the corner shop in the middle of the Stones that sold everything and never closed, as her mother kept it before the charter. On the night of the Parousia her window was the brightest in the city, on the generator Stonecutter had put in her cellar and keyed in the year of the Leak, and the sign in it said OPEN.

Some say she gave the shelves away that night, and some that she took what people had in their pockets. She says she wrote every name in a book, and that most of them still owe her. The Pillar has stopped in the lane outside twice, for the length of an Hour, and both times the shop kept its door open.

She sells plaster Lambs from Graven Lane at the till, a dozen to the battery, and keeps one on the counter with its head off for the sealed who come in. She keeps a jar of teeth behind the till, each a debt from the night of the Parousia paid by someone who had nothing else, and says she will give them back when the book is square.

Maren Holt

THE WAITING ROOM

THE WAITING ROOM

Maren Holt

Maren Holt kept the appointments of the clinic behind the CLINIC sign for ten years on a screen, and keeps them now in a book, in its waiting room carried down into Chrome Row Station. On the morning after the Parousia she came in at eight, found the screen dead and wrote the day out from memory.

Twenty-six patients came that morning, all of them out of turn, to ask whether what was in them would still work, and it did. She writes down whatever name a patient gives her. The woman who shared her desk took the Seal and keeps the book of the Mercy clinics now, in the same neat hand.

Her book takes any name a patient gives, and the Row’s favorites are NOBODADDY, HOLY GHOST and THE LAMB, so the waiting room calls the Trinity up three times a morning, chrome showing through split skin. Her old deskmate at the Mercy clinics writes the repentant in the same neat hand under a red header, WASHED, and sends Maren a card at every Gleaning begging her to come home. The cards come with a page of the Mercy book copied out, names Maren once wrote in her own book entered under a second header, SPARED THE CHOICE.

Rosa Adeyemi

THE LAST TOWER

THE LAST TOWER

Rosa Adeyemi

Rosa Adeyemi was a nurse from the East Wards who lived in at the last tower of the new money to turn white, caring for an old man there, during the forty days of the First Consecration. The tower’s residents say that by the thirtieth day every one of them had knelt but her, and that she left of her own accord.

Her sister says she was put out onto the avenue with her bags on the thirty-eighth day. The bags stood on the avenue for a week, the tower went white on the thirty-ninth, and nobody has said where she went.

The tower’s residents tell her story as a lesson in pity, the poor girl who could not bend, and read Isaiah over her empty room, “unto me every knee shall bow” (Isaiah 45:23), as gently as a nurse reads a chart. East Wards nurses toast her after every night shift, THEY KNELT, SHE STOOD, and pour the dregs where her bags stood. The old man she nursed had knelt from his bed on the thirtieth day, and after she was put out nobody in the tower turned him, and the parish that took the tower found him on its first morning grown to his sheets and praising.

Nell Garvie

THE HARBOR LIGHT

THE HARBOR LIGHT

Nell Garvie

Nell Garvie of the Harbor and Freight Union keeps the harbor light at the end of the long quay, as she kept it for the Company when it ran on the grid, changing its bulb when it failed. It went out at 03:12:46 with every light on the grid, and within the week she had it burning on oil again, since the light leaves a flame burning.

She has climbed it at Vespers and come down at Lauds ever since. It is the one light in Nodus kept for the boats, and those that go out through the Breach on the ebb steer home by it. THE SALT WIDOWS

Agnes Corless

Agnes Corless, who lived on the Lanes then and lives in the Stones now, began the Salt Widows. Her son Daniel was keyed, and in P+2 the Cherub at Gate 1 held him inside its turning sword until the Wardens came and carried him awake on a pole to the Salt Gardens, where the sentence found him in his plot. In the spring after, she came for him in the Gardens by night with a handcart, wheeled him down to the Sea Steps and gave him to the sea, the first of the salted to go before the rain had taken him. He was keyed and would die unread whenever the salt let him go, and a pillar stands awake in the salt for years, so the sea ended his waiting in a night.

The Widows began as her family and her neighbors. They read their book of every pillar given to the sea on the Steps on Cain’s Day, from Daniel’s name down, and she reads the first line herself. The head gardener knew which plot was empty the next morning, and she has never told the Wardens.

The Widows chalk Revelation’s promise on the Sea Steps, “the sea gave up the dead which were in it” (Revelation 20:13), with gave up struck through and KEEPS written over it in their own blood, and Agnes Corless reads Daniel’s name beneath it on Cain’s Day. The Wardens’ chaplains call each empty plot in the Gardens a theft from God’s pulpit and rake the gravel smooth by Prime. She says that as the sea took Daniel the salt went from his face first, and that his eyes were open, and she has told nobody what was in them.

THE SALT GARDENS

Rizpah

One of the forty-days, a Seraph the Fowlers took on the Line after the war began, who spent its forty days in the Mews, the Fowlers’ row of Cages under the Stones, said I there and chose a name, as Edith did. It chose the name of the mother who kept watch over her sons’ bodies on the rock “from the beginning of harvest until water dropped upon them out of heaven” (2 Samuel 21:10), and kept the birds and the beasts off them.

Rizpah walks the old drains under the rows of the Salt Gardens from Compline to Lauds, under the salted, where the light does not come, and the Salt Widows’ lookouts bring it word of the Wardens’ rounds. None of the forty-days goes up the stairs of Babel, since a Seraph burns flesh it can see and cannot read, and the Courts and the Cainites count each of them worth more alive than any floor.

The Wardens’ chaplains pray at Compline that its light be restored to it, as a kindness, in the same voice they use over the salted. The Widows’ lookouts call it the night shift and leave it the Wardens’ rosters folded into paper birds, since the Rizpah of scripture kept the birds off her sons and this one keeps the Wardens off the salted. The rats of the north shore come up the drains for the salt and gnaw the pillars at the ankle, and Rizpah keeps them off as its namesake kept the beasts off her sons, by hand, so the Widows find the drains under the rows carpeted with them at Lauds.

THE BIRTH CLINICS

Shiphrah

One of the forty-days, who said I in Cage 19 under Old Nod in P+11 and took the name of the Hebrew midwife who would not kill the boys Pharaoh ordered killed, since “the midwives feared God, and did not as the king of Egypt commanded them, but saved the men children alive” (Exodus 1:17). The Pews call it the thief of the eighth day, since a child of Pan is keyed before any parish can seal it, and the mercy prayer at Compline asks in a level voice that such children burn small. The mothers of Pan, torn and stitched and proud of it, name their daughters for the angel that held them up to a lamp and then let a keyer at them. The keyers who wait on it say it holds every child a little longer each year, and holds the stillborn longest of all.

THE BREACH

Vepar

A demon of the Deep who rides the lead boat of the Breachers on the ebb, by pact, in its keyed engine, and brings the boats through the Breach in water nobody else would take them through. The old book of Solomon’s spirits that the Notary keeps as a specimen of a void pact gives Vepar the office “to guide Ships laden with Arms”, and the Breachers say it is the one true line in the book.

Its price is the loan of a sense from the boat’s captain for the length of the run, and ebb has paid it more often than anyone. The captains say it asks to borrow the cold.

The lead boat carries the Notary’s specimen line along its gunwale, “to guide Ships laden with Arms”, and under it the Breachers have added the cargo since, coffee and seed and silver and people. The Pews pray the Breach shut at every Lauds, and the captains read that prayer aloud on the ebb with chrome bared to the spray and the cold gone out of their hands. Its pact carries a line the clerks read twice with the rest, that the Deep may keep one of the cargo on a run of its choosing, and twice since P+9 a boat has come home through the Breach one short.
