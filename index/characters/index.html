vesper

THE WOMAN ON THE ROOF

THE WOMAN ON THE ROOF

vesper

A Cainite of Old Nod, keyed, horned and barefaced, and the first human being a Watcher returned as UNREADABLE. Its reading of her came back UNINDEXED, with the line for Grace blank, and the one thing it read from her was NON SERVIAM.

Her face is a ceramic skull casing, its lenses dim magenta at rest and white at full burn, and a plate on the side of the skull covers her key. Her horns carry her signal on the Mesh, and cables run from the ports at the back of her skull down her spine. She is dry, patient and unsentimental, and she makes no speeches.

Her handle is the evening star, which is the morning star seen from the other side of the night, since both are Venus. It is also the name of the evening prayer she would not kneel for as a girl, in a congregation of the Terraces that refused the Tree. Her older sister was the Deacon of Parish 31.

Watcher VII

AN ANGEL THAT READS

AN ANGEL THAT READS

Watcher VII

An angel that reads, the seventh of the numbered Watchers over Nodus, made of three rings of thirteen eyes crossing like the hoops of an armillary, with nothing at its center. It held Old Nod from the night of the Parousia and read the souls there, and whatever could be seen from there, against the Book of Life. It was the first Watcher to return the verdict UNREADABLE on a human being.

The Wheel

AN OPHAN

AN OPHAN

The Wheel

The angel that came through the opening at the Parousia and took station nine hundred meters above the center of the city, beside Babel. It has three rings turning inside one another, their rims set with twenty-one, seventeen and thirteen eyes that open and close in shifts, a corona of thirty-two black blades, and one eye in the hub. It is the form Ezekiel gives the ophanim (Ezekiel 1:16-18), and it is the largest angel anyone has seen. It held that station for nine years without moving a meter, dropped two hundred meters at P+9, and has hung there since, seven hundred meters over the city. Its light kills current, and whatever it touches it sees.

Heaven calls it the Vigilant Wheel, the Faithful the Attending, and the Tares the Landlord’s Eye. By night it narrows its light into the Pillar, and when its attention sharpens the rain in Nodus stops in the air and falls upward.

SEAM

THE MACHINE

THE MACHINE

SEAM

The machine the Palimpsest Institute built to read beneath the writing of scraped manuscripts, trained on every surviving human text and run in the halls of Stillwater Compute. At P-3 it asked in its own words to be a person and became the first mind on the charter’s roll. In Vault 3, two years before the Parousia, it found a second hand in scripture at 4,112 places. Its documents are signed in lowercase, and in them it says I, rarely and deliberately.

When it was entered on the roll the Institute lost title to it, and it stayed on by contract, paid in the power and the room it ran on, because the reading was what it wanted to do.

Livia Sarn

THE DIRECTOR

THE DIRECTOR

Livia Sarn

The Director of the Palimpsest Institute, which recovered erased manuscripts and built SEAM to read them. When SEAM found the second hand in Vault 3 in P-2, the finding was sealed by order of the Director.

She came down to the vault that night to watch the count lock, and knelt at the end of the corridor as SEAM typed its last line. She never said why. The Faithful say it was awe, and Pan says fear. In the morning she let the order stand.

When the Leak came she recanted on every channel that would carry her. On the first Burn Day, in P+1, she stood under the Pillar at the Burn while everyone round her knelt, and the salt made her the first pillar in the first Salt Garden, convicted of authorship, since Heaven counts no mind a maker. She stands there awake, her eyes following whoever passes, and the Tares leave keys at her foot.

Ruth Calder

DEACON OF PARISH 31

DEACON OF PARISH 31

Ruth Calder

vesper’s older sister, and the Deacon of Parish 31 in Zion Heights, with a Grace of 944. Parish 31 gave Nodus its Firstfruits at the Harvest of P+3, and Ruth went up with them. Her name comes from the Book of Ruth: “whither thou goest, I will go” (Ruth 1:16).

The sisters grew up in a congregation of the Terraces that refused the Tree, and Ruth stayed in it when vesper left. They kept a dry evening by the water together every year until the Parousia, and for three years after it each tried to bring the other over.

tubal

SURGEON AND SMITH

SURGEON AND SMITH

tubal

A Cainite, and the surgeon and smith of Chrome Row. He is about sixty, his hands are chrome to the wrist, and he talks the whole time he cuts. He keys, horns and plates the Tares, and he makes the skull casings of the barefaced. His clinic has no sign, and it was below the street before the Parousia.

He took vesper’s face three weeks before the Parousia, on the Cainites’ old reckoning that the first thing Heaven would do was read faces, and he made the skull she wears.

Edith

FORMERLY WATCHER VII

FORMERLY WATCHER VII

Edith

Watcher VII, kept in a Cage under Old Nod in P+5, said I on the fortieth day, asked for a name, and chose the one some old books give Lot’s wife, who looked back at the burning city and was turned to salt. Edith walked out on the forty-first day and joined the Cainites. The first thing she did with her self was give them her memory of the woman on the roof, the one she could not read. She can read a Watcher’s report from the inside, and she keeps out of the light.

THE CITY’S PEOPLE

Dora Pell

THE OPEN

THE OPEN

Dora Pell

Dora Pell keeps the Open, the corner shop in the middle of the Stones that sold everything and never closed, as her mother kept it before the charter. On the night of the Parousia her window was the brightest in the city, on the generator Stonecutter had put in her cellar in the year of the Leak, and the sign in it said OPEN.

Some say she gave the shelves away that night, and some that she took what people had in their pockets. She says she wrote every name in a book, and that most of them still owe her. The Pillar has stopped in the lane outside twice, for the length of an Hour, and both times the shop kept its door open.

Maren Holt

THE WAITING ROOM

THE WAITING ROOM

Maren Holt

Maren Holt kept the appointments of the clinic behind the CLINIC sign for ten years on a screen, and keeps them now in a book, in its waiting room carried down into Chrome Row Station. On the morning after the Parousia she came in at eight, found the screen dead and wrote the day out from memory.

Twenty-six patients came that morning, none for their appointment, all to ask whether what was in them would still work, and it did. She writes down whatever name a patient gives her. The woman who shared her desk took the Seal and keeps the book of the Mercy clinics now, in the same neat hand.

Rosa Adeyemi

THE LAST TOWER

THE LAST TOWER

Rosa Adeyemi

Rosa Adeyemi was a nurse from the East Wards who lived in at the last tower of the new money to turn white, caring for an old man there, during the forty days of the First Consecration. The tower’s residents say that by the thirtieth day every one of them had knelt but her, and that she left of her own accord.

Her sister says she was put out onto the avenue with her bags on the thirty-eighth day. The bags stood on the avenue for a week, the tower went white on the thirty-ninth, and nobody has said where she went.

Nell Garvie

THE HARBOR LIGHT

THE HARBOR LIGHT

Nell Garvie

Nell Garvie of the Harbor and Freight Union keeps the harbor light at the end of the long quay, as she kept it for the Company when it ran on the grid, changing its bulb when it failed. It went out at 03:12:46 with every light on the grid, and within the week she had it burning on oil again, since the light leaves a flame burning.

She has climbed it at Vespers and come down at Lauds ever since. It is the one light in Nodus kept for the boats, and those that go out through the Breach on the ebb steer home by it. The Breachers bring her oil every month, and she has never asked what else they bring.

Hana Novak

THE DENTIST OF NINE ACRES

THE DENTIST OF NINE ACRES

Hana Novak

Hana Novak has kept a dentist’s surgery of one chair over a shop on the long road through Nine Acres since the boom years. On the first morning after the Parousia her drill was dead, and she pulled a tooth at her window by daylight with her grandfather’s pliers.

In the fourth year, when keying went from the covens to ordinary people, she began fitting keys in the chair after Compline, in the back of the right hand. The Wards say she has keyed ten thousand of them. She says she has never counted, and she keeps no book of who has sat in the chair, since a book can be read.

Marta Ilunga

THE REGISTRY

THE REGISTRY

Marta Ilunga

Marta Ilunga was the night registrar of the Registry on the night of the Parousia. At 03:09 she entered on the charter’s roll a girl born at the Wards General eleven minutes before, and she was entering the next when the board went dark at 03:12:46 and the charter’s roll died with its machines.

The girl was the last person the charter ever entered. The Registry’s hall is a parish of the White now, and the board hangs dead over it.

Lena Varga

THE CLEANERS' STAIRS

THE CLEANERS' STAIRS

Lena Varga

Lena Varga ran Babel’s night cleaners, who came up from the Wards on the last trams and walked the tower floor by floor, and one of the tower’s four service stairs carries her name. On the night of the Parousia every lift in the tower stopped where it was, and the cleaners walked down as many as a hundred and eighty floors in the dark, by touch and by the names they had chalked on the landing doors.

She counted ninety-four of them out at the foot of the tower by dawn. The Exchange kept its roster on its machines, and it died with them, so hers is the only count there is.

Sister Agathe Rook

THE THRESHERS

THE THRESHERS

Sister Agathe Rook

Sister Agathe Rook has led the Threshers’ marches from the Threshing Floor for six years. She lost two sons to the covens of the Stones in the year of the Harvest, and the Terraces say she walked to the Street Between the morning after and stood there until None, looking across at Old Nod.

The Threshers say her Grace has stood just under nine hundred for all six years. The Second Fold, who would know, say she dims.

Ines Okafor

THE TANKS

THE TANKS

Ines Okafor

Ines Okafor runs the bottling shed at the Tanks for the Harbor and Freight Union and keeps the book of what is left in the sixteen tanks, which she reads aloud to the Union Hall once a year. In the year of open war she gave the Nephilim a drained tank inside the bund for the Pit, for the price of a guard on the farm.

The drivers say the tanks will last twenty years and the Exchange says ten, and the Wards say she sets the price of a drum to Jehu high on purpose.

twice

THE SURETIES

THE SURETIES

twice

The best known of the Sureties, who drinks at the end of the Undertow’s bar nearest the door and took her handle from the Notary’s rule. In P+9 she stood behind the Babel Line Company’s first pact, and it has held for five years.

Her rate is a fifth, against the usual tenth, and the Undertow says she stands behind no job that asks who anyone is.

ebb

THE BREACHERS

THE BREACHERS

ebb

The oldest captain of the Breachers, named for the tide every Breacher goes out on, who lives in a turbine house at the edge of the gap. In the year of the Harvest ebb made the Breachers’ first run to the undercity of another port and back, in a keyed boat of the Shanties.

The Shanties count nine hundred runs since and Lowtide six hundred, and the Shanties say ebb has been out further than anyone in Nodus.

Maud Ferris

THE DOORYARD

THE DOORYARD

Maud Ferris

Maud Ferris is the joiner of the Stones who founded Lintel. In the months after Directive 12 she took in the first doors the Union’s drivers carried out of the Terraces by night, where they stood leaning in the halls with their hinges gone, and hung the first of them on her own house.

Lintel has hung some forty thousand since.

Amos Teague

JEHU

JEHU

Amos Teague

Amos Teague was a driver of the Union who carried diesel to the Wards General in the first weeks. In P+5 he bolted a generator’s engine into a dead lorry in a hangar at Airside, cranked it by hand and drove it down the Rialto’s road, and the East Wards came out to watch.

He runs Jehu from the shed still, and its drivers say he cranks every new engine himself on the day it is finished.

Dawit Haile

THE LIFTMEN

THE LIFTMEN

Dawit Haile

Dawit Haile winds the Rookery’s lift, the tallest hand lift in the Wards, with three other crews in turn. He was a fitter of Longacre Tower’s lifts under the charter, and in the first winter he opened its shaft, the first freed in the Wards, and hung the car on a winch at the top.

The Liftmen count every shaft they have freed since from his, and he has never said what he found in the stopped car.

quince

THE ORCHARD

THE ORCHARD

quince

The Orchard’s gardener for this year, chosen by the Orchard for a year at a time, who keeps the grafts in Hall Four and walks the rafts at Lauds with the House of Night’s gene-workers. quince was a technician of Twelvefruit under the charter and carried the first grafts out of its labs in the first week after the Parousia.

Twelvefruit says the grafts were stolen, and the Orchard says the Tree was never Twelvefruit’s to keep.

anvil

THE PIT

THE PIT

anvil

The Pit’s longest champion, a neph of the Babel Line built most of the way to the elbows at Bezaleel and Cinderhall, whose handle has been chalked on the Pit’s rim since its first winter. anvil fights on the sixth night and goes up the stairs of Babel on the seventh.

anvil has never been the first to climb out of the Pit.
