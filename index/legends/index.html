THE ROOKERY

FLOOR FORTY-FIVE

The Rookery has forty-four floors.

TOLD // THE ROOKERY

FLOOR FORTY-FIVE

The Rookery has forty-four floors. The block says that on some nights its stairwell goes up a flight past the forty-fourth, to a landing with a light under a door.

Those who say they have climbed to it agree that it is a parish door, locked from the inside, and disagree about the number chalked on it. The Liftmen’s car stops at the forty-fourth, and the winders say nobody has ever asked it to go higher.

THE TANKS

LAST BOTTLE

The Tanks say one bottle in the shed has never been empty since the first winter.

TOLD // THE TANKS

LAST BOTTLE

The Tanks say one bottle in the shed has never been empty since the first winter. It goes from hand to hand on the nights the farm runs short, and it lights one stove and one oven every time and comes back to the shed by Lauds.

Ines Okafor has read the tanks’ book to the Union Hall fourteen times, and the bottle is in none of them.

THE NARROWS

A MAN ASKING THE WAY

Old Nod has had no Watcher since P+5.

TOLD // THE NARROWS

A MAN ASKING THE WAY

Old Nod has had no Watcher since P+5. The Narrows say one walks them still as a man at night, very tall and very polite, who stops people at corners and asks the way to a street that is not there anymore, and thanks them by name.

Nobody in the Narrows has given it their name, and every one of them who tells the story says it used theirs.

THE WARDS THAT FACE THE WHITE

WHITE CHILDREN

The Wards that face the White say that on clear nights children are seen walking into the stone, one behind another, and walking out of it at Lauds pale to the hair, and that they never say where they have been.

TOLD // THE WARDS THAT FACE THE WHITE

WHITE CHILDREN

The Wards that face the White say that on clear nights children are seen walking into the stone, one behind another, and walking out of it at Lauds pale to the hair, and that they never say where they have been.

The Quarrymen, who cut into the White from below, keep a child’s shoe they found in a cut under a tower of the new money, white to the laces, on a nail in the station they work from.

PAN

A DRY ROOM

Pan tells of a room somewhere under the Burn whose walls are lined with keyed mesh and whose ceiling the Pillar has been seen to light, once, through a crack in the street above.

TOLD // PAN

A DRY ROOM

Pan tells of a room somewhere under the Burn whose walls are lined with keyed mesh and whose ceiling the Pillar has been seen to light, once, through a crack in the street above. Whoever sleeps in it, they say, wakes with their key gone silent and their name back in the Book of Life.

Every crew in Pan has looked for it to wall it up, and every one says it has found the wrong room.

THE GATEHOUSE

THE FORTIETH WARDEN

The story in Blackgate is that on the fortieth day one Warden stood in Charter House while the board knelt round him, and that the light came down and left him standing, and that he walked out across the forecourt between the Wardens kneeling in it and went below the street.

TOLD // THE GATEHOUSE

THE FORTIETH WARDEN

The story in Blackgate is that on the fortieth day one Warden stood in Charter House while the board knelt round him, and that the light came down and left him standing, and that he walked out across the forecourt between the Wardens kneeling in it and went below the street.

Every recruit is told on the first night that he founded Blackgate, and that he alone of its officers kept the Warden’s badge on his coat.

THE DEEP END

THE BOAT WITH NO LAMP

The Ferrymen say that on the ebb, on still nights, a flat boat with no lamp at its stern poles out from the dormitories of the Flats past the Flotilla, and is not seen again before Lauds.

TOLD // THE DEEP END

THE BOAT WITH NO LAMP

The Ferrymen say that on the ebb, on still nights, a flat boat with no lamp at its stern poles out from the dormitories of the Flats past the Flotilla, and is not seen again before Lauds.

Nobody has seen who poles it, and every ferryman of the Deep End can name the night they last saw it go.

HOTEL SPLENDID

THE THIRTY-SECOND KEY

Thirty-one of the Hotel Splendid’s guests still live in the rooms they booked on the night of the Parousia, and the night porter keeps their keys on the board behind the desk.

TOLD // HOTEL SPLENDID

THE THIRTY-SECOND KEY

Thirty-one of the Hotel Splendid’s guests still live in the rooms they booked on the night of the Parousia, and the night porter keeps their keys on the board behind the desk. The board has thirty-two keys on it.

The porter says the thirty-second is for a room whose guest went out on the first morning to see what had happened and has not come back yet, and that he will keep it until they do. Nobody on the Row knows which room.

THE RIALTO

THE TWENTY-FOURTH FILM

The Rialto has twenty-three films on the drive that was in the booth on the night of the Parousia, and it shows them in turn.

TOLD // THE RIALTO

THE TWENTY-FOURTH FILM

The Rialto has twenty-three films on the drive that was in the booth on the night of the Parousia, and it shows them in turn. The East Wards say that once a year, on a night nobody can name in advance, it shows a twenty-fourth.

Everyone who says they saw it describes a different film, and all of them say it ended. The projectionist says the drive holds twenty-three, and that he would like to see the twenty-fourth himself.

LONGACRE

THE DARK WINDOW

Every window in the White is sealed with light.

TOLD // LONGACRE

THE DARK WINDOW

Every window in the White is sealed with light. Longacre says that one is dark, high in a tower of the new money, and that from Compline to Lauds a candle burns in it, a point of flame in all that white.

The Quarrymen say the room is real, on a floor nobody has lived on since the forty days, and they have cut toward it from below for three winters.

THE WEIR

THE FOURTEENTH CHAIR

The Weir has thirteen chairs, and the Lot sits in them.

TOLD // THE WEIR

THE FOURTEENTH CHAIR

The Weir has thirteen chairs, and the Lot sits in them. The Stones say that whenever the Lot has sat over a death, there are fourteen chairs on the sill in the morning, and nobody carried the fourteenth down.

It is a different chair every time, a kitchen chair or a stool or a seat out of a tram, and the families of the dead say they know whose house it came from. The Lot leaves it where it stands until the water takes it.

PAN

THE TWO DOORS

Past the embassies of the Courts, in the tunnels under the Burn, the story goes that two doorways stand bricked shut with keyed brick, and that there is no Court’s sigil over either.

TOLD // PAN

THE TWO DOORS

Past the embassies of the Courts, in the tunnels under the Burn, the story goes that two doorways stand bricked shut with keyed brick, and that there is no Court’s sigil over either. Every embassy’s people walk past them on the way to the booking hall without turning their heads.

Runners dare each other to knock. The ones who say they did argue over whether anything knocked back, and nobody who works in an embassy will say which tunnel the doors are in.

HIGH MEADOW

THE LANDING THAT SAT ON A WARDEN

High Meadow says that in the first winter after the gates went up, a Warden who had chased a runner into one of its blocks lost him on the stairs, sat down on the twentieth-floor landing to get his breath, and found the landing sitting round him, and that the block heard his case there by candle from Compline to Lauds.

TOLD // HIGH MEADOW

THE LANDING THAT SAT ON A WARDEN

High Meadow says that in the first winter after the gates went up, a Warden who had chased a runner into one of its blocks lost him on the stairs, sat down on the twentieth-floor landing to get his breath, and found the landing sitting round him, and that the block heard his case there by candle from Compline to Lauds.

Every block in the Wards says it happened on its own stairs, and none of them agrees on the sentence. Some say the landing took his boots and some his gun, and the Kilns say it refused him its lamps, so that he went down the whole block in the dark.

THE REGISTRY

THE REGISTRY GIRL

At 03:09 on the night of the Parousia Marta Ilunga entered on the charter’s roll a girl born at the Wards General eleven minutes before, the last person the charter ever entered.

TOLD // THE REGISTRY

THE REGISTRY GIRL

At 03:09 on the night of the Parousia Marta Ilunga entered on the charter’s roll a girl born at the Wards General eleven minutes before, the last person the charter ever entered. She is fourteen this year, and three girls of fourteen say they are her, one sealed in the Pews, one keyed in the Stones and one of neither mark at the Sidings.

Each of them tells the night her mother told her, and the three nights contradict each other. Marta Ilunga has met all three girls, and she has never said which of them she wrote down.
