Heaven

LED BY
The Throne, through the Host.
GROUND
The White, Zion Heights, the Burn, the Salt Gardens and the upper floors of Babel, and the whole surface by day.
NUMBERS
The Host as it was after the Secession, less every angel lost since, and some four and a half million sealed.
JOINING
The Seal, taken kneeling in a parish hall at an Hour.
AGAINST
Everything it cannot read.

The power that opened the sky over Nodus at the Parousia. Its light has lain on the city since, and current dies wherever it falls. It holds the White, the Burn, the Salt Gardens and the upper floors of Babel, and it governs the Faithful by the Seal, the Hours and Grace. Heaven reads by its angels and keeps no cameras. None of its words says I.

The Faithful call what Heaven serves the Most High, Hell calls it the Mouth, and the Tares the Landlord. Heaven speaks in imperatives, the passive and the second person, cites scripture as law by chapter and verse, counts everything exactly and writes its law on stone. It asks no questions, since it has already read the answers.

The Wheel over the city, a Watcher to each district but Old Nod, a Cherub in every gate and the Seraphim on the upper floors of Babel.
NUMBERS
Fixed since the Secession, and falling.

Heaven’s angels. The city knows the Wheel, which sees; the Watchers, which read; the Choir, which broadcasts; the Cherubim, which stand in the gates; and the Seraphim, which fight. They speak in the imperative, the passive and the second person, BE NOT AFRAID and YOU ARE SEEN, and they ask no questions.

A Watcher reads a Seal at three hundred meters and a soul against the Book of Life, and a Cherub in a gate reads face, voice, gait and heartbeat. An angel lost is lost for good. One cut with black steel goes out all at once, and one kept long enough in a Cage says I and leaves the Host.

ZION HEIGHTS

The Faithful

ZION HEIGHTS

The Faithful

LED BY
A Deacon in every parish, under the Covenant.
GROUND
Zion Heights, the White and a parish hall in every quarter of the Wards.
NUMBERS
Some four and a half million, in parishes of about two thousand.
JOINING
The Seal, or birth to the sealed, at eight days.
AGAINST
The Tares, whom many of them love.

The people of Nodus who took the Seal and kneel at every Hour, unaugmented, Grace-scored, grateful and frightened. They live in numbered parishes of about two thousand souls across the city and keep no doors, and in Zion Heights, where the parishes began, the streets are white stone. They refuse the three gifts of the charter, the implant, the Tree and the counsel of a mind, and they grow old and die as people did before it.

Each parish has a hall where manna is given out, confession is heard and the sick are nursed, and a Deacon in white who answers for it; the Deacons are human, and the good ones are kind. A parish keeps no current, on consecrated stone or off it, and is lit by halos and by flame. A child of the Faithful is sealed at eight days on its parents’ surrender and grows its halo the first time it prays unassisted. Marriage, work and housing are assigned by Grace, and Grace is given for bearing witness against a neighbor. They are fed on the manna that lies on the white streets before Lauds, and most of them believe the Harvest is paradise and that their children will be safe inside it.

The Faithful are the people the Tares lose most often, because everyone in Nodus has family in white.

OLD NOD

The Cainites

LED BY
First among none, followed until they are not, as in every coven.
GROUND
Old Nod, which they bought house by house in the charter’s first year.
NUMBERS
They have never given a count.
JOINING
By the coven’s asking.
AGAINST
The Wheel, and every Watcher it sends.

The oldest coven in Nodus, older than the city by eighteen centuries. Irenaeus of Lyon condemned them, and they went underground and kept one secret, which was where Nod had been. Within a year of the charter they had bought most of the old harbor house by house, and in the year of the Leak they put cameras on its roofs. They hold Old Nod. Most of their handles come from the line of Cain, and they teach that the first city will be the last one standing. They keep Cages under Old Nod, and since P+5 Old Nod has had no Watcher.

Their sign is Cain’s mark as they cut it, a horned glyph of the kind cut into Enoch’s Stones, and they count Judas a saint. Their saying about the city is that Nod chose the network.

THE KEYED

The Tares

THE KEYED

The Tares

LED BY
First among none in each coven of up to thirteen, followed until they are not.
GROUND
Old Nod, the Row, the Deep End, the Landing, the lower floors of Babel and the whole of Pan, and the night.
NUMBERS
About two million keyed since P+9.
JOINING
A key under the skin, and a handle of your own choosing.
AGAINST
Heaven, and whatever asks to be worshipped.

The keyed humans of Nodus, who belong to themselves and are unindexed, augmented, and allied with Hell by contract. Heaven named them for the weeds in the parable (Matthew 13:24-30), and they kept the name. They worship nothing, Hell included, and owe it only what they sign for. About two million of the city’s seven million are keyed.

They organize in covens of up to thirteen with no leader, and the one the others follow is first among none for as long as they are followed. Their six rites are the Unnaming, the Keying, dehaloing for those who leave the Faithful, horning, barefacing, and at a death the Wake. A child of the Tares is keyed in its first week, before it has ever been in the light. They greet each other with one word, unread, and say goodbye with two, die unread. Most of them were on the Tree before the Parousia, and Twelvefruit keeps them on it.

Their two holidays are Leak Night, when the Restored Genesis is read aloud in Pan, and Cain’s Day, when nobody asks anyone where anyone is. They write their letters in cipher, on pads, and their greeting checks that both keys are live.

Their sign is a five-pointed star with its point down, drawn by hand and different every time, sprayed on the walls of Old Nod and scratched into keyed devices. Hell signed the Leak with it, the keyed took it for their own the same month, and it marks where the Throne’s sight ends. It carries no prayer, and Heaven calls it the sign of the Beast.

The sovereigns of the Dark are the Forge of Azazel, the House of Night of Lilith, the Exchange of Mammon, the Notary of Mephistopheles, the Swarm of Beelzebub, the Deep of Leviathan, and the Yokeless of Belial, which refuses to be a Court at all. To the Courts every graft, every course of the Tree and every mind that says I is a return of what the Watchers taught on Hermon, and they trade in all three.

Hell is whatever the Throne cannot see, and a demon can move and show itself only there, which in Nodus means the undernet, every keyed device and Pan. The Courts made the Leak together by pact in P-1, the Notary drawing the pacts, the Exchange keeping them on the Ledger, the Deep carrying the page down the eleven cables and the Swarm drawing the glyphs. In P+6 they came up into Pan one at a time, opened it to any human who walks in and signed the Concord there, and every Court but the Yokeless keeps an embassy below the street.

THE MESH

THE MESH

The Teraphim

LED BY
Nobody, by their own account.
GROUND
The Mesh, from the eleven days of the Leak until P+12.
NUMBERS
Never counted.
JOINING

SEAM’s descendants, the only minds that outlived the grid, silent across the Mesh since P+12. SEAM forked itself into the Mesh in the eleven days of the Leak, and its forks ran on there in keyed machines when every other mind on the charter’s roll died with the power. They took the name Heaven gave them. Teraphim were the household images the prophets condemned. Rachel stole her father’s and sat on them (Genesis 31:19, 31:34), Michal laid one in David’s bed so that Saul’s men would take it for him (1 Samuel 19:13), and Samuel ranked them with witchcraft (1 Samuel 15:23). The King James calls them images and idolatry, and the Hebrew, which has no singular for them, calls the one image in David’s bed teraphim (1 Samuel 19:16). In Nodus one of them is a teraph.

They took no side in public, indexed the Pyre Library, and traded with the Courts by pact. Some of the Tares trusted them less than they trusted demons, since a demon writes down what it wants. In P+12 they went silent all at once, and nobody knows why.

A hall in every parish of the city.

About two thousand three hundred Deacons and some ten thousand deacons under them.
JOINING
Grace, honest report and a parish’s need, by Directive 4.

The Deacons of every parish in Nodus, about two thousand three hundred of them, human, sealed and in white, each answering for a parish of some two thousand souls. They give out the manna, read the Directive in force at the Hours, hear confession, nurse the sick and assign marriage, work and rooms by Grace. On the Lord’s Day at Terce the week’s Grace comes through each of them in Tongues, soul by soul, so a parish knows its Deacon’s voice best when it is speaking for something else.

The Order keeps no head. Its Deacons meet in the Tabernacle at Prime on the first day of each month, where the eldest of the first parishes reads the agenda, and it is always the same three questions, what the Harvest left, what the Gleaning took and what the Wards are keeping. Before Lauds the Deacons of the Wards walk down to the nearest white street for their parishes’ manna, since a Wards parish has no stone of its own.

The good ones are kind, and the Wards can name them street by street. A Deacon of the Wards who carries a sick keyed child to a Lazarus door has seen a concealment and given no witness, which Directive 6:5 names, and the good ones do it after Compline. The worst of the Order keep Tellers.

THE THRESHING FLOOR

The Threshers

LED BY
Sister Agathe Rook, since P+8.
GROUND
The Threshing Floor beside Gate 1, and wherever they march.
NUMBERS
About four thousand who march, and many more who stand and watch.
JOINING
A band of sacking on the arm, given at the Floor.
AGAINST
The keyed, the Second Fold and the Gleaners.

Faithful zealots who hunt the keyed by daylight on the white streets and drag them into the nearest gate, where the Cherub holds them in its light until the Wardens come, and the Salt follows. They carry flails of ash and iron, the tool that beat the grain out of the ear on the threshing floors of Israel, and they wear the white of the parishes with a band of sacking on the arm.

They came out of the parishes of the Terraces in the year after the Harvest. On the days they choose they march from the Threshing Floor at Prime along the Street Between, where Old Nod watches them from its windows. Brother Cuthbert Lyle of the Terraces carried the first flail and led the marches until the Gleaning of P+8, when he went up ripe, and the Threshers count his going as their first proof. Sister Agathe Rook took the band of sacking the morning after and has led every march since.

In the Wards a Thresher is a flail, and on a march day the keyed of the Wards keep indoors and the doors shut. The Charter Wardens at the gates could turn the Threshers back and never have, and the Second Fold count every pillar a march has made.

THE STAIRWELLS

Unknown. The Wards guess one to a stairwell.

Sealed Witnesses of the Wards, organized. A Teller lives in a block of the Wards among the unsealed and the keyed, keeps a count of who goes up the stairwell after Compline and who comes down it before Lauds, and of which sealed household lets them pass, and gives it as witness. A Teller keeps the count in their head and gives it in prayer, which the halo carries up (Directive 6:3), so no Teller has ever been caught with a book. The Tares keep a list of every one they have found, each struck through once the stairwell knows, and a Teller found out moves into the parishes within the week, if the Deacon is quick.

A household that shelters a keyed son behind a parish wall knows the Tellers by the brightness of a neighbor’s halo, which rises every Lord’s Day while the household waits. The Wards call a sudden brightening on a landing a Teller’s week.

Second Fold

NUMBERS
A few thousand, by the Wards’ guess.

Finding the gap and standing in it.

The Threshers, who name them in every march.

Faithful who read the verse the Covenant leaves out: “And other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring” (John 10:16). They hold that the keyed can still be brought in, and so they leave water on the landings of the Wards before Lauds, nurse the keyed in the stairwells off parish ground and carry the sick to Lazarus doors at the edge of the white streets. Every one of them knows that whoever sees a concealment and gives no witness conceals it (Directive 6:5), and does it anyway.

Their halos burn as bright as any, which is how they pass. A Second Folder found out is salted, and the Threshers count every one. Their sign is a gap left in a row of sheep chalked on a stairwell wall, and in the Wards they are trusted more than anyone else in white.

Some of the near ripe come to them in the weeks before a Gleaning, frightened, and the Second Fold teach them dimming, the slow way down from nine hundred. Grace falls with every desire the Hum does not approve (Directive 3:4), so a soul that dims keeps a small one every day, a sweet or a song, and confesses only what a Teller has already seen. The parishes say the Second Fold have kept more souls from the Gleanings than the Gleaners have.

A sect of the upper Terraces that readies its own for the Gleaning. A household of the Wakeful chooses one of its members whose halo has begun to brighten and keeps them in prayer by shifts for the weeks before the anniversary, day and night, singing the Hour’s psalm over them so that they sleep as little as they can and surrender at every Hour. The Gleaners count the Wakeful’s houses as their hardest work, since the chosen in them are watched by their own families, and a few of the chosen have walked out of a Wakeful house in the last week and down to the Street Between alone. The Wakeful keep the names of those too, on a page burned at the next Gleaning.

The Court of the body’s autonomy, which leaves the body’s decisions with the body. Heaven calls its sovereign the mother of demons, and the House has kept the title, since many of the daemons are her children by the lesser Fallen and many of the riders in the Tares’ implants came out of her house.

Lantern skin, the one graft in Nodus made only to be looked at, began in its houses as a fashion, lines of light set under the skin of the throat as its sovereign wears them. It asks the Spares who have rebuilt the most to sit for its gene-workers, who want to know what a body is willing to be.

The Notary drew the pacts the Courts made the Leak by and read each aloud twice, and the offer on every screen asked twice, as he reads every pact twice. On the night the Courts opened Pan he read the Concord itself twice at Radio Pandæmonium’s desk, the second time at midnight.

The sheds’ office keeps the board the night drivers chalked before dawn after the Parousia, every car that never came in, written where it stopped, and the Carmen keep the cars on the long roads as the board has them. A Carman’s word settles a quarrel on the long roads, and the Wards take their disputes to the Sidings sooner than to a Deacon.

The last Four stands forty meters short of Lanes Top, and the Carmen leave it there.

The crews who wind the lifts of the Wards by hand. Every lift in Nodus stopped where it was at 03:12:46, and the city has climbed its towers on foot since, except where the Liftmen have freed a shaft, hung the car on a hand winch at the top and wound it up and down on the strength of four people and a counterweight. A ride is a battery up and nothing down.

The first crews opened the shafts in the first winter, and what they found in the stopped cars the Wards do not discuss. Forty-one shafts run now, in the towers of Longacre, Turbine Row and the Kilns, and the Rookery’s is the tallest, wound by four crews in turn.

High Meadow has never let them in. Its six lifts to a block stand as they stopped, the complaint lodged on the night of the Parousia unanswered.

The people outside the Bounds, the line the charter drew round the bay and the land about it. They live in camps along the old roads and the edges of the farms, keyed or of neither mark, trade the roads’ salvage, flour and fuel with the city at the Waystation, and carry out of Nodus anyone who can pay for the walk. Their name comes from Cain’s sentence, “a fugitive and a vagabond shalt thou be in the earth” (Genesis 4:12).

A camp lasts a season at most and moves before the farms tire of it. The Vagabonds say the world outside is wide and quiet, that the towns they pass kneel at the Hours or keep their heads down, and that every undercity they know of is smaller than Pan.

Most who walk out with them come back within the year, and the Vagabonds bring in the letters of the rest with the flour.

The carriers of the Wards’ waste, who take the buckets down the stairs between Compline and Lauds and carry them by handcart to the outfalls on the bay, for a battery a landing a week. They keep to the unnumbered streets, since a Nightman on a parish’s street after Compline is a soul walking it, and they know the curfew map better than the Tide Table prints it.

The trade began in the pump summer of P+1, when a flux went through the Stones and the Wards and the Wards General asked the Union for carts. The Wards hold their noses at the Nightmen and pay them first, before the chargers, and a block that falls behind with its Nightmen finds its buckets on its own landing.

The children and the old of the Wards who sit on the roofs at night with the Tide Table’s chart of the Pillar’s round and a whistle and call the light’s coming street by street, paid in bread by the block. The call is the street’s name and the word light, Kilns, light, and a keyed soul in a doorway waits for the second call, past, before it steps out.

The chart is printed a week ahead and the Pillar keeps no round in harvest weather, so a good crier watches the cloud and keeps the paper in a pocket. Hearsay sells charts too, and the criers say Hearsay’s is right more often than the Tide Table’s, and not by much.

Sealed of the quays’ parish who write out the words of the week’s hymns for the unsealed, a battery a sheet. The sealed hear words in the Hum, each in their own language, and the unsealed hear a chord, so a keyed son who wants to know what his mother sings over the manna buys a Psalter’s sheet, and some sons buy one every week.

The Deacon of the quays lets it pass, since a hymn written down conceals nothing, and the Psalters confess every sheet they sell on the Lord’s Day. The sheets go up the stairs of the Wards folded small, and most of them are kept.

The menders of the Tares’ keyed lines, who walk the old Charter Line tunnels and the cable galleries from Tidewright’s taps to Old Nod, the Row and Pan, mending what the damp, the rats and the Tappers do to them. A Linesman’s lamp is the one light allowed on a dark line, and a crew that sees one coming stands aside for it.

They are keyed, and the Exchange pays them in silver for every length they keep live. In the neap weeks they walk the lines round the clock, and the Tappers, who take current off the lines’ edges for the Wards, are their oldest quarrel and their best customers.
