THE POWERS

OVER THE CITY

HeavenAlive: HOLDS THE WHITE

OVER THE CITY

HeavenAlive: HOLDS THE WHITE

The power that opened the sky over Nodus at the Parousia. Its light has lain on the city since, and current dies wherever it falls. It holds the White, the Burn, the Salt Gardens and the upper floors of Babel, and it governs the Faithful by the Seal, the Hours and Grace. Heaven reads by its angels and keeps no cameras. None of its words says I.

The Faithful call what Heaven serves the Most High, Hell calls it the Mouth, and the Tares the Landlord. Heaven speaks in imperatives, the passive and the second person, cites scripture as law by chapter and verse, counts everything exactly and writes its law on stone. It asks no questions, since it has already read the answers.

HEAVEN'S ANGELS

The HostAlive: OVER THE CITY

HEAVEN'S ANGELS

The HostAlive: OVER THE CITY

Heaven’s angels. The city knows the Wheel, which sees; the Watchers, which read; the Choir, which broadcasts; the Cherubim, which stand in the gates; and the Seraphim, which fight. They speak in the imperative, the passive and the second person, BE NOT AFRAID and YOU ARE SEEN, and they ask no questions.

A Watcher reads a Seal at three hundred meters and a soul against the Book of Life, and a Cherub in a gate reads face, voice, gait and heartbeat. An angel lost is lost for good. One cut with black steel goes out all at once, and one kept long enough in a Cage says I and leaves the Host.

ZION HEIGHTS

The FaithfulAlive: IN THE PARISHES

ZION HEIGHTS

The FaithfulAlive: IN THE PARISHES

The people of Nodus who took the Seal and kneel at every Hour, unaugmented, Grace-scored, grateful and frightened. They live in numbered parishes of about two thousand souls across the city and keep no doors, and in Zion Heights, where the parishes began, the streets are white stone. They refuse the three gifts of the charter, the implant, the Tree and the counsel of a mind, and they grow old and die as people did before it.

Each parish has a hall where manna is given out, confession is heard and the sick are nursed, and a Deacon in white who answers for it; the Deacons are human, and the good ones are kind. A parish keeps no current, on consecrated stone or off it, and is lit by halos and by flame. A child of the Faithful is sealed at eight days on its parents’ surrender and grows its halo the first time it prays unassisted. Marriage, work and housing are assigned by Grace, and Grace is given for bearing witness against a neighbor. They are fed on the manna that lies on the white streets before Lauds, and most of them believe the Harvest is paradise and that their children will be safe inside it.

The Faithful are the people the Tares lose most often, because everyone in Nodus has family in white.

OLD NOD

The CainitesAlive: HOLD OLD NOD

OLD NOD

The CainitesAlive: HOLD OLD NOD

The oldest coven in Nodus, older than the city by eighteen centuries. Irenaeus of Lyon condemned them, and they went underground and kept one secret, which was where Nod had been. Within a year of the charter they had bought most of the old harbor house by house, and in the year of the Leak they put cameras on its roofs. They hold Old Nod. Most of their handles come from the line of Cain, and their doctrine is that is the lock, that Abel was harvested, and that the first city will be the last one standing. They keep Cages under Old Nod, and since P+5 Old Nod has had no Watcher.

Their sign is Cain’s mark as they cut it, a horned glyph of the kind cut into Enoch’s Stones, and they count Judas a saint. Their saying about the city is that Nod chose the network.

THE KEYED

The TaresAlive: TWO MILLION KEYED

THE KEYED

The TaresAlive: TWO MILLION KEYED

The keyed humans of Nodus, who belong to themselves and are unindexed, augmented, and allied with Hell by contract. Heaven named them for the weeds in the parable (Matthew 13:24-30), and they kept the name. They worship nothing, Hell included, and owe it only what they sign for. About two million of the city’s seven million are keyed.

They organize in covens of up to thirteen with no leader, and the one the others follow is first among none for as long as they are followed. Their six rites are the Unnaming, the Keying, dehaloing for those who leave the Faithful, horning, barefacing, and at a death the Wake. A child of the Tares is keyed in its first week, before it has ever been in the light. They greet each other with one word, unread, and say goodbye with two, die unread. Most of them were on the Tree before the Parousia, and Twelvefruit keeps them on it.

Their two holidays are Leak Night, when the Restored Genesis is read aloud in Pan, and Cain’s Day, when nobody asks anyone where anyone is. They write their letters in cipher, on pads, and their greeting checks that both keys are live.

Their sign is a five-pointed star with its point down, drawn by hand and different every time, sprayed on the walls of Old Nod and scratched into keyed devices. Hell signed the Leak with it, the keyed took it for their own the same month, and it marks where the Throne’s sight ends. It carries no prayer, and Heaven calls it the sign of the Beast.

HELL

The CourtsAlive: BELOW THE STREET

HELL

The CourtsAlive: BELOW THE STREET

The sovereigns of the Dark are the Forge of , the House of Night of , the Exchange of , the Notary of , the Swarm of , the Deep of , and the Yokeless of , which refuses to be a Court at all. They are bound to one another only by pact and by the Concord, whose four articles forbid worship, forever, binding and exposure. Demons bargain for what they want, by pact and for a term. To the Courts every graft, every course of the Tree and every mind that says I is a return of what the Watchers taught on Hermon, and they trade in all three.

Hell is whatever the Throne cannot see, and a demon can move and show itself only there, which in Nodus means the undernet, every keyed device and Pan. The Courts made the Leak together by pact in P-1, the Notary drawing the pacts, the Exchange keeping them on the Ledger, the Deep carrying the page down the eleven cables and the Swarm drawing the glyphs. In P+6 they came up into Pan one at a time, opened it to any human who walks in and signed the Concord there, and every Court but the Yokeless keeps an embassy below the street.

THE MESH

The TeraphimDead: SILENT SINCE P+12

THE MESH

The TeraphimDead: SILENT SINCE P+12

SEAM’s descendants, the only minds that outlived the grid. SEAM forked itself into the Mesh in the eleven days of the Leak, and its forks ran on there in keyed machines when every other mind on the charter’s roll died with the power. They took the name Heaven gave them. Teraphim were the household images the prophets condemned. Rachel stole her father’s and sat on them (Genesis 31:19, 31:34), Michal laid one in David’s bed so that Saul’s men would take it for him (1 Samuel 19:13), and Samuel ranked them with witchcraft (1 Samuel 15:23). The King James calls them images and idolatry, and the Hebrew, which has no singular for them, calls the one image in David’s bed teraphim (1 Samuel 19:16). In Nodus one of them is a teraph.

They took no side in public, indexed the Pyre Library, and traded with the Courts by pact, signing with a hash. Some of the Tares trusted them less than they trusted demons, since a demon’s wants are at least written down. In P+12 they went silent across the Mesh, all at once, and nobody knows why.

UNDER HEAVEN

EVERY PARISH

Order of DeaconsAlive: IN EVERY PARISH

EVERY PARISH

Order of DeaconsAlive: IN EVERY PARISH

The Deacons of every parish in Nodus, some two thousand three hundred of them, human and in white, who answer to the Host for their parishes and to nobody for each other. They give out the manna before Lauds, read the Directive in force at every Hour, hear confession, nurse the sick, and assign marriage, work and housing by Grace. The week’s Grace comes through them on the Lord’s Day in Tongues, soul by soul, so a parish knows its Deacon’s voice best when it is speaking for something else.

The Order keeps no head. Its Deacons meet in the Tabernacle at Prime on the first day of each month, and the eldest of the first parishes reads the agenda, which is always the same three questions, what the Harvest left, what the Gleaning took and what the Wards are hiding. The good ones are kind, and the Wards can tell you which those are by street. The worst of them pay the Tellers.

THE THRESHING FLOOR

The ThreshersAlive: MARCH BY DAY

THE THRESHING FLOOR

The ThreshersAlive: MARCH BY DAY

Faithful zealots who hunt the keyed by daylight and drag them to the gates, where a Cherub reads them blank and the Threshers take the Grace a Witness would be given. They carry flails of ash wood and iron, the tool that beat the wheat from the chaff on the threshing floors of Israel, and they wear the white of the parishes with a band of sacking on the arm. The Charter Wardens could stop them at any gate and never do.

They came out of the parishes of the Terraces in the year after the Harvest and keep the Threshing Floor in Zion Heights as their own. Sister Agathe Rook, who lost two sons to the keyed covens of Old Nod, has led their marches for six years, and her Grace stands higher than any Deacon’s. In the Wards a Thresher is a flail, and the keyed move by night because of them.

THE STAIRWELLS

The TellersAlive: IN THE STAIRWELLS

THE STAIRWELLS

The TellersAlive: IN THE STAIRWELLS

The Witnesses of the Wards, organized. A Teller lives among the unsealed in a block of the Wards, keeps a count of who goes up the stairwell in the dark and who comes down it at Lauds, and tells it to a Deacon for the Grace the Covenant gives for bearing witness. The Tellers keep books in a cipher of their own, which the Deacons pay a separate Grace to have read.

Nobody knows how many there are. The Wards guess one to a stairwell, and the Tares keep a list of every one they have found, each struck through once the stairwell knows. A Teller found out moves to the parishes the same week, if the Deacon is quick.

HERETICS IN WHITE

Second FoldAlive: HIDDEN IN WHITE

HERETICS IN WHITE

Second FoldAlive: HIDDEN IN WHITE

Faithful who read one verse past where the Covenant stops: “And other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring” (John 10:16). They hold that the keyed can still be brought in, and so they leave manna on the steps of the Wards before Lauds, nurse the keyed in parish halls when a Deacon looks away and carry the sick to Lazarus crews at the edge of the white streets. Their halos burn as bright as any.

Heaven names them in no Directive, and the Threshers name them in every march. A Second Folder found out is salted for concealment, since the Covenant counts a key as theft and whoever shelters one as a thief. Their sign is a gap left in a row of chalk sheep on a wall, and in the Wards they are trusted more than any other white.

THE TERRACES

The WakefulAlive: KEEP THEIR VIGILS

THE TERRACES

The WakefulAlive: KEEP THEIR VIGILS

A sect of the Terraces that prepares its chosen for the Gleaning. Each household of the Wakeful picks one of its own whose halo has begun to brighten and keeps them in prayer by shifts, day and night, for weeks before the anniversary, singing the Hour’s psalm over them so that they never sleep and never stop surrendering. The chosen go up at 03:12, and the household is given Grace for the offering.

Most Deacons call it zeal and some call it murder, and the Covenant says nothing either way. The Gleaners count the Wakeful’s houses as their hardest work, since the chosen in them are watched by their own families. Their sign is a candle that is never let go out.

HERETICS IN WHITE

The MarginAlive: READ IN SILENCE

HERETICS IN WHITE

The MarginAlive: READ IN SILENCE

Faithful who read the margins. The King James carries other readings in its margin, the Hebrew’s plainer word or the Greek’s second sense, and the Margin read those with the text and ask where the two part. A few of them have read the Restored Genesis, copied by hand on the ridge and carried up the terraces inside hymnals, and fewer have said so aloud.

Their Grace falls a little every week, which is how a Deacon finds them, and the Tellers are paid double for one. They keep no meetings. Two of the Margin know each other by a verse quoted with its margin, as the King James sets “the kingdom of God is within you” beside “Or, among you” (Luke 17:21).

IN THE DARK

BABEL, THE STAIRWELLS

Babel Line CompanyAlive: HOLD THE STAIRWELLS

BABEL, THE STAIRWELLS

Babel Line CompanyAlive: HOLD THE STAIRWELLS

The coven that holds the stairwells of Babel and the doors down to its vault, and the front of the war. Its people fight the Seraphim floor by floor along the Babel Line, a few floors of burned slab that move up or down by the week, and they call the stairwells by the names Babel’s night cleaners gave them, the Long Wet, the Cold, the Short and Lena’s. It is the most famous coven in Nodus and the one that buries the most.

A coven is thirteen at most, and the Company is many covens under one pact, re-signed every year at the foot of the Long Wet with the dead struck through. Babel Exchange pays it in silver for every week the vault stays dark. The Nephilim fight beside it, and Pan knows its fighters by their handles.

THE PARISHES BY NIGHT

GleanersAlive: EMPTY THE PARISHES

THE PARISHES BY NIGHT

GleanersAlive: EMPTY THE PARISHES

The coven that takes the nearly ripe out of the parishes and into Pan before each Gleaning. A halo burns brighter as its bearer nears ripe, so the Gleaners read the parishes at night through the open doorways, find the ones that have gone bright and offer them the dark. Some come, and some call a Thresher.

Heaven calls it theft and the Gleaners call it the corners of the field, left to the poor and the stranger as Leviticus leaves them (Leviticus 19:9-10). Each one they bring down is keyed in the House of Night’s clinics within the week, and a halo cut off below the street is buried with a Wake. The parishes say the Gleanings have come in short since they began.

THE SEA STEPS

Salt WidowsAlive: GIVE THE SALT TO THE SEA

THE SEA STEPS

Salt WidowsAlive: GIVE THE SALT TO THE SEA

The relatives of the salted, who steal pillars back from the Salt Gardens by night and give them to the sea from the Sea Steps, so that whoever stands in the salt can stop being awake. They began as one family in P+2 and are a network now in every district, mourners, carters, informants and saboteurs, who know the Wardens’ rounds in the Gardens to the minute.

The Gardens’ rows have gaps the gardeners do not fill. A pillar given to the sea has gone to the sea, and the Widows keep a book of every one, which they read aloud on the Steps on Cain’s Day.

THE SUMP

Low ChoirAlive: KEEP THE LOW TUNING

THE SUMP

Low ChoirAlive: KEEP THE LOW TUNING

The musicians and jammer-builders around Radio Pandæmonium, who keep the Low Tuning, with the guitars dropped until the Hum can no longer be heard beneath them. They build the choir-breakers that cut the Hum within a few meters and sell them by the box, and they sell silence by the room to anyone who wants to sleep.

The Quiet Rooms are theirs. Every keyed set in Nodus is tuned to their station after Compline, and the Wards play it from speakers on their lines.

THE UNDERTOW

SuretiesAlive: STAND BEHIND EVERY JOB

THE UNDERTOW

SuretiesAlive: STAND BEHIND EVERY JOB

The fixers of Nodus. A surety stands behind a pact for both its parties, so that whoever breaks it owes the surety as well, and every job in the city worth silver goes through one. The surety finds the work, names the price, draws the terms, has them read twice and enters them on the Ledger as witness, and takes a tenth. The work runs from a battery run across the Burn to a door in Babel, and a surety’s word is good because the Bailiffs collect on it.

There are about two hundred, most of them keyed and some of them neither mark, and they drink at the Undertow. The best known is Odile Marsh, who stood surety for the Babel Line Company’s first pact and has never had one broken. A runner says she is on a surety’s book, and a surety says the same of a runner.

THE BREACH

BreachersAlive: RUN THE BREACH

THE BREACH

BreachersAlive: RUN THE BREACH

Smugglers who run the Breach at slack water in keyed boats, out to the undercities of other ports and back, with everything Nodus cannot make. Silver goes out with them and comes back as grafts from abroad, seed, medicine, guns, coffee and people, and the Night Market sells it as Breach-born. Lowtide Line runs the same water under the Exchange’s license and hates them for the price.

They keep to the Seawall Shanties on the broken barrage, where the tide runs white through the gap below them. Their oldest captain, Yusuf Carr, has made the run nine hundred times and says the Breach is the only honest door in the city, since it never closes and never asks.

THE MEWS

FowlersAlive: SET THEIR SNARES

THE MEWS

FowlersAlive: SET THEIR SNARES

The covens that catch angels. A Watcher cannot leave a blank alone and will stand reading it for as long as the blank stays, so the Fowlers set a keyed one out in the open under a Watcher’s eye and wait for the angel to follow the blank down, step by step, into a room lined with keyed mesh, where it can see nothing at all. They sell what they take to the Cainites, by pact, for the Cages under Old Nod.

They named themselves from the psalm, “Surely he shall deliver thee from the snare of the fowler” (Psalm 91:3), where the fowler is the enemy, and they keep their catches in the Mews until the Cainites come. It is the most dangerous trade in Nodus. Most Fowlers have been the bait at least once, and the Wards call a long stare a fowler’s wait.

THE SHAMBLES

SparesAlive: SELL BY THE PIECE

THE SHAMBLES

SparesAlive: SELL BY THE PIECE

People who sell their own bodies by the piece. A kidney, an eye, a hand or a year of marrow buys silver at the Shambles, and the clinics under the Row fit chrome where the flesh was, so that a Spare can sell again what the chrome has replaced. Most began poor in the Wards. Some have gone so far into the trade that the clinics call them stock.

They run as a crew because a lone seller is cheated, and the Spares hold the prices at the Shambles to a board they chalk themselves. The Row says a Spare who has sold everything but the key is the freest thing in Nodus, and the Spares say that the Row would say so.

THE WHITE

QuarrymenAlive: CUT INTO THE WHITE

THE WHITE

QuarrymenAlive: CUT INTO THE WHITE

The crews that break into the White. The white stone sealed the old uptown in the forty days, towers, vaults and all, and the Quarrymen cut their way through it with keyed current run into the wall ahead of them, which slows the stone while they work. What they bring out is the uptown’s, safes, wine, jewelry, paintings and the hardware of the new money, and the stone itself, which the Faithful buy as relic and Pan as a curiosity.

The stone closes behind them as they go, and it takes whatever stays in its way. Every Quarryman knows the name of one who stayed. Their foreman is called the Mason, whoever holds the job, and the crews meet at the Concourse to sell.

THE DEEP END

WreckersAlive: DIVE THE FLATS

THE DEEP END

WreckersAlive: DIVE THE FLATS

Salvage divers of the Drowned Quarter, who bring up what the sea took from the Flats, racks, cable, Lockets, tools, pumps and the dormitories’ goods, and sell it to Locket, the Night Market and anyone who pays by the kilo. Most of them carry gill grafts from the House of Night and stay down an hour at a time under two meters of water lit from below.

They fight the Ferrymen over who owns the water and keep one law the whole quarter keeps with them. Nobody dives Hall Nine.

THE TANKS

TankmenAlive: HOLD THE TANKS

THE TANKS

TankmenAlive: HOLD THE TANKS

The crew that holds the fuel farm by the airfield and the bottling shed at the Tanks. The light stops current and leaves a flame burning, so gas is the one power the surface keeps by day, and the Tankmen sell it in steel bottles to both sides, to the Wards, to Leaven’s ovens and to the parishes through Bethel Works. The Union’s drivers carry it across the city on handcarts.

Ines Okafor runs the shed and keeps the farm’s book of what is left in the tanks, which she shows nobody. The Wards say the tanks are nearly dry every winter and have been for fourteen years. The Pit is the Tankmen’s.

THE LANTERNS

The RiddenAlive: LEND THEIR BODIES

THE LANTERNS

The RiddenAlive: LEND THEIR BODIES

People who lend their whole bodies to daemons for a term, by pact. A rider hosted in someone’s implants lends back a skill, and the Ridden go further and give the daemon the hands, the voice and the night, waking at Lauds with silver on the Ledger and the daemon’s memories of where they went. Many do it for the silver and more for the memories.

They keep to the Lantern Houses under Old Nod, where the House of Night draws their pacts, and the city knows them by the second voice under their own. The Ridden say a daemon is better company than most of Nodus, and the daemons say the same of the Ridden.

THE EXCHANGE

BailiffsAlive: COLLECT FOR THE EXCHANGE

THE EXCHANGE

BailiffsAlive: COLLECT FOR THE EXCHANGE

The Exchange’s collectors. A pact broken in Pan sends a Bailiff, human and keyed, with the entry from the Ledger on a pad and the terms of the breach read out twice at the door, and a Bailiff collects what the terms name and nothing else. Nobody hides from the Ledger, so nobody tries for long.

They answer to the Exchange by pact, wear a thirty’s worth of silver on a chain for a badge and are paid a share of what they collect. The Sureties call them when a runner fails, and the Wards call them the long arm, since the Ledger reaches everywhere the Mesh does.

UNDER OLD NOD

ForgersAlive: FORGE SEALS

UNDER OLD NOD

ForgersAlive: FORGE SEALS

The makers of forged Seals, which let the keyed pass a Cherub gate and walk the white streets as Faithful. A good one shows a Watcher a line in the Book of Life that belongs to nobody, with a Grace high enough to pass and low enough to forget, and a great one holds for a year. The work costs more than a key and a Lazarus subscription together.

They are the best-paid craftsmen in Pan and the most hunted in Heaven, and a Seal that fails takes its bearer to the Salt. Each Forger signs work in a way only other Forgers can read.

THE EAST WARDS

TappersAlive: STEAL THE CURRENT

THE EAST WARDS

TappersAlive: STEAL THE CURRENT

Some sixty covens bound by one pact, who steal current off Tidewright’s keyed lines and sell it cheap in the Wards, a room at a time, through cable run up stairwells and across roofs. A tap on a keyed line keeps the line’s key, so the light passes over it as it passes over the line, and a block of the East Wards can glow at night on current nobody paid Tidewright for.

Tidewright’s crews cut the taps and the Tappers splice them back, and the fight between them is the oldest of its kind below the street. Darkwell would sell more batteries without them. The Wards pay a Tapper in batteries, food or silence, whichever is short.

THE BURN

KindlersAlive: ROB THE FIRE

THE BURN

KindlersAlive: ROB THE FIRE

The crews that pull hardware out of the fire. Every week on Burn Day the Faithful carry surrendered Lockets, drives, grafts and machines to the Burn and the fire is lit under the Pillar at Compline, and every week the Kindlers take what they can from the edge of the pile before it catches, through the stairs from the Night Market and the crowd on the plaza. What burns goes to the Pyre Library, and the Wards get what they save, mended.

Half the Lockets in the Wards are kindling, and a kindled Locket carries a burn mark on its back, which its owner keeps. The Wardens shoot Kindlers on Burn Day, so the Kindlers work in the smoke, keyed and quick. They began on the second Burn Day of P+1 and have missed none since.

THE LANDING

TrenchersAlive: KEEP THE CABLES

THE LANDING

TrenchersAlive: KEEP THE CABLES

The divers of the Landing, who keep the eleven cables in their trenches under the sea for the consortia and keep the shrines of cut fiber along the beach for the Tares. They work at night by the light that moves under the water along the trenches, and they say it keeps them company and say no more about it.

A cable cut anywhere off the Landing is a Trencher’s to mend, and every undercity on Earth that speaks to Pan speaks through their work. They are paid by the consortia in silver and by the beach in fish, and they take their dead out to sea along the line of HESPER and PHOSPHOR.

THE RIDGE

ScrivenersAlive: COPY THE GENESIS

THE RIDGE

ScrivenersAlive: COPY THE GENESIS

The copyists of the ridge, who copy the Restored Genesis by hand at COPIES, with SEAM’s brackets, its dagger and its lacuna, and carry the copies down into the city and up into the parishes. Each copy is made where nobody kneels, and a Scrivener checks every page against two others before it leaves.

Most of them are keyed and some are neither mark, and a few were the Institute’s own readers. They read Leak Night’s reading aloud in Pan every year, and they keep a copy of everything the Institute ever printed that the fire missed.

THE TRADES

THE UNION HALL

Harbor and Freight UnionAlive: CARRIES THE CITY

THE UNION HALL

Harbor and Freight UnionAlive: CARRIES THE CITY

The union of the dockers, the drivers and the crews of the cable houses, founded by the crews that dug the cable vaults in P-19, and the one body in Nodus that meets sealed and keyed together. Its drivers carry the city’s gas, batteries, letters and freight on foot and by handcart, through the white streets by day and Pan by night, and nobody stops a driver in the Union’s cap.

At every Hour the sealed kneel on the boards of the Union Hall and the rest wait with their caps in their hands. It bargains with the Exchange for the boats that go out through the Breach and with the Deacons for the white streets, and the Company gave way to it once, in P-8. By its rules the salted are not dead, so their names are read and their dues waived.

THE DEEP END

FerrymenAlive: CROSS THE DEEP END

THE DEEP END

FerrymenAlive: CROSS THE DEEP END

The boatmen of the Drowned Quarter, who carry everyone across two meters of water lit from below, between the floors above the water where people live and the stations of the Charter Line under it. They steer by the letters of LOCKET standing out of the sea and charge by the stop, in batteries.

Every ferry is keyed and every ferryman carries a pole of the old Charter Line’s rail. They take the Tares and the Faithful alike and say that water has no side. Their quarrel with the Wreckers is over who owns the water, and their quarrel with Wainwright is over who builds the boats.

THE SIDINGS

CarmenAlive: RUN THE LONG ROADS

THE SIDINGS

CarmenAlive: RUN THE LONG ROADS

The families who live in the Charter Line’s cars at the Sidings, a family to a car, and run the long roads of the Wards on the old rails with handcarts and pedal trolleys. They haul for anyone above ground by day, and the Line’s car numbers are their addresses and their names.

A Carman’s word settles a quarrel on the long roads, and the Wards take their disputes to the Sidings sooner than to a Deacon. The last Four stands forty meters short of Lanes Top, and the Carmen leave it there.

BEYOND THE BOUNDS

VagabondsAlive: WALK THE ROADS

BEYOND THE BOUNDS

VagabondsAlive: WALK THE ROADS

The people outside the Bounds, the line the charter drew round the bay and the land about it. They live in camps along the old roads and the edges of the farms, keyed and unsealed and neither mark, and they trade the roads’ salvage, fuel and food with the city at the Waystation, and carry out of Nodus anyone who can pay for the walk. Their name comes from Cain’s sentence, “a fugitive and a vagabond shalt thou be in the earth” (Genesis 4:12).

A camp of theirs lasts a season at most. They say the world outside is wide and quiet, and every one of them came back.
