THE CONCORDWhat the file shows

In the sixth year of the Attending the Courts of Hell came up into Pan one at a time and opened it, by the Concord of Pan, to any human who walks in. The file keeps that night from the desk of the station that broadcast it. It shows the sovereigns coming up out of the dark one at a time into the light of one lamp, the second reading at midnight, and the signing.

The city above knelt at Matins. In the station the clock passed it while Mephistopheles read the first article, and nobody knelt.

PROVENANCEHow the file survived

The station broadcast the picture on the Mesh with the sound that night, and it keeps every night it broadcasts on its own racks.

QUESTIONSQuestions from inside the file

Where is this?

Deep End Station, the Charter Line’s station under the Deep End, drowned since the night of the Parousia. Radio Pandæmonium broadcasts from a desk on its platform, with the trackbed under black water beside it. The old name, DEEP END, runs along the far wall in tiles that went dark with the grid, and on the night of the Concord the Tares painted PANDÆMONIUM over it, the name the Courts gave the city under Nodus that night and Milton gave the capital of Hell. Everyone calls it Pan, and Radio Pandæmonium took its name from it.

Whose camera was it?

Radio Pandæmonium’s. It sits on a stand behind jubal’s chair, and that night its picture went out on the Mesh with the sound, keyed, so only a keyed set could see it.

Who comes up the platform?

The sovereigns of the Courts who came up that night were Azazel of the Forge, Lilith of the House of Night, Mammon of the Exchange, Beelzebub of the Swarm, Mephistopheles of the Notary, and Belial, of the Court that refuses to be one. Leviathan of the Deep had signed already.

What is the Concord?

Hell’s only law. The first Courts swore it by word among themselves at the end of the Secession, and each Court that rose later swore to it as it rose; on this night all seven set it down and sealed it for the humans coming into Pan. Its four articles say that no Court accepts worship, that no pact is made without consent and none forever, that no Court binds another, and that what is hidden stays hidden. The written Concord makes one exception to the fourth in its own text. A Court that breaks the Concord has its keys published by the others, the keys to its halls’ locks and its ledgers’ ciphers, so that its hidden places stand open to the Throne.

THE CONCORD WHOLE

Why does nobody kneel?

The Hum rises for Matins over the whole city, and the Faithful kneel wherever they are. Pan is lined with keyed mesh, which the light and the Hum cannot pass, and the first article of the Concord had just been read.

Why did Belial break his own point?

He has never said.

What happened next?

The Courts opened Pan to any human who walks in. Nobody there is watched unless they sign for it, and nobody uses an indexed name.
