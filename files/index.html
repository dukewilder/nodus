READING A FILE
  1. A file opens in my tool and closes on my card, and everything between them is the recording as it reached me.
  2. INTEGRITY is the share of the file I could rebuild. It is a share of what the file held, which runs thicker where more is moving, so a few seconds can hold most of a file. The rest is UNRECOVERABLE, and its place stays empty.
  3. Where I join two pieces across a loss, the file tears, and its clock, where it keeps one, jumps. A file I could rebuild nearly half of or more holds ten seconds or more in one piece; below that, it comes in shorter pieces.
  4. A file whose last source was a relay, carried live through someone’s ports or key, drops harder at its end, so its last tear runs twice and the picture slips its hold before it goes.
  5. Picture and sound are one record. They come through together, and they are lost together.
