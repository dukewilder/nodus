The Sheds Tell the Well No, Again

Eleven years of asking, one answer

Darkwell has bid again for the last independent chargers of the bonded sheds, after eleven years of trying, and the chargers have turned it down at the Union Hall again. They keep their one battery in five and a rate a little under the Well’s. This paper prints both rates. The Well can read.

TALLOW

Neap week? Light a candle.

Batteries go dear at the neaps, and our candles stay one price, to everyone. His light stops current and leaves a flame burning. He never thought it through.

THE CHANDLERY, TURBINE ROW

The Calf Read Our Grace Again

The Unmass, and the board by the door

At Terce on the Lord’s Day, while every parish knelt to hear its Grace, the Golden Calf read this paper’s list of the dead in a Choir voice, soul by soul, gave the Directive in force the finger and damned three Deacons of the Terraces by name, and the house sang Fuck God and Die until Sext. The Calf’s board by its door now carries forty-one Threshers who came down the stairs to look. Two stayed for the crackling.

The Threshers Sang 137 at the Gate

Old Nod sang back

The Threshers marched from the Floor at Prime on the fourth day, four thousand of them by our count, and sang the end of the hundred and thirty-seventh psalm across the Street Between at the Stones, the verse about the little ones and the stones, with the children of the Terraces singing loudest. The Pestilence opened its windows and played Fuck God and Die at them until the march turned. Two held at Gate 1 by Nones, and two new pillars in the Salt Gardens by Vespers. The Fan names one of them. We name him by his work, below, and the other’s people have asked us to wait.

Nine Hundred and Twelve Satisfied in the Terraces

The Fed, counted

The Deacons of the Terraces count nine hundred and twelve of their sealed past seven hundred this year, eating nothing, and two hundred and six past eight hundred, sleeping nothing. The parishes call them the Satisfied and are hanging white cloths for the Called. Pan calls them the Fed and counts the springs to the Gleaning. Three.

SEETHE

Thou shalt seethe

A kid in its mother’s milk, seethed from Lauds for a table of six, two silver. Open the seventh day only. Bring the appetite He forbade you.

THE LAST ROAD OF THE SIDINGS

The Stone Moves a Finger

The Rookery chalks its New Year mark

The Rookery marked the stone’s edge on its stairwell at the New Year, as it does every year, and this year’s mark sat a finger’s width from last year’s. The Tappers say their cable holds it. The oldest of the block say the stone was slowing before the cable came.

Twelvefruit’s pact comes up for reading

THE FISH MARKET

The Tide Table is always wrong

Today’s price is chalked over the scale, in charged batteries. Nothing off the Sea Steps, ever, whatever the Pews would like you to eat.

OLDER THAN THE CHARTER

The Union Says Twenty. The Exchange Says Ten.

The book of the tanks, read again

The book of the sixteen tanks was read to the Union Hall again and settled nothing again. The drivers say twenty years are left and the Exchange says ten. Jehu has begun trying its engines on the Kilns’ rain gin. Leave the Wards a glass.

TERMS

The Standing Pacts

  • Lowtide Line. Its license from the Exchange to sail the Breach at twice the Breachers’ price, a year from each midsummer.
  • Blackgate. Its company pact with the Exchange, three years from each Concord night. The gates still call it the deserters.
  • Twelvefruit. Its pact with the House of Night, read again this year, with the Orchard asking for a seat.
  • The Lantern Houses. The House of Night’s pact with Stonecutter, read again last year, the rent paid in keyings, free, for every Cainite child.

LAZARUS

Lazarus, come forth

THE WEEK

Burn Day Falls on the Sixth

  • The Unmass at the Golden Calf on the Lord’s Day at Terce, as every week. Bring a plaster Lamb from Graven Lane, break it on the hooves and drink free.
  • The Notary’s clerks read the Left Hand’s papers at the booking hall windows on the second day at Nones, twice each, as the rule is. Queue early. Bring something to throw.
  • The Threshers have called a march for Burn Day. The Pestilence has its speakers in the window already.
  • Burn Day on the sixth. The Faithful carry from Vespers, some of it bought on the Concourse to be burned, and the fire is lit under the Pillar when the Hum falls back from Compline.
  • SEETHE lights its fire at Matins on the seventh day. The kid is two silver a table and the house asks parties of six to come at Lauds and sit until it is gone. High Meadow reads out the menu at Vespers.
  • The Rialto runs the ninth of its twenty-three films this week, on the generator, from Vespers. The East Wards will say every line along with it.
  • The Gleaners’ runners are booked through the week before the Gleaning, as every year, and the barber’s on the Lanes has a queue.
  • The Liftmen’s car at the Rookery stops at the forty-fourth. The winders say nobody has ever asked it to go higher.

LEAVEN

Let them eat manna

And be eaten. The rest of us tear into Leaven. Three cuts across the crust, half a battery a loaf, warm by Lauds.

NOTICES

Notices

GRAVEN. Graven images, every one of them Himself in a posture he would hate. Plaster Lambs a dozen to the battery, for breaking, burning or pissing on. Wheels with the eye cut out. GRAVEN, ON GRAVEN LANE, UNDER OLD NOD.

THE NUMBER. Six hundred threescore and six, cut clean in the forehead or the back of the right hand. Count it yourself (Revelation 13:18). A Cherub reads it as nothing at all. THE NUMBER, ON GRAVEN LANE.

ABOMINATION. Pork, eel, crab, shrimp and blood, grilled on the skewer. Everything he won’t let you eat, and he can’t stop you. ABOMINATION, ON GRAVEN LANE.

SEETHE. The kid in its mother’s milk, the pig, the blood, the bat, the rat and the Lord’s own fat, all of Leviticus 11 at one table. Fire at Matins on the seventh day, out at Matins on the Lord’s Day. Six days we rest, like Him. SEETHE, THE LAST ROAD OF THE SIDINGS.

THE BRAY. Every night when the Hum falls back from Compline. Windows open on the Street Between. Bring your filthiest, in any language. The Pews are listening, on their knees. OLD NOD.

DOORS. Parish doors, out of the Terraces and onto your frame, the number still chalked on the back. Keyed latches the same morning. Their doors, your room. THE DOORYARD, IN THE STONES.

A ROOM. Low floor, Turbine Row, by floor-right. Door hung, latch keyed, few stairs. A thirty or more, and worth it. Ask on the landing.

THE LIFT. Forty-one shafts wound by hand in the Wards. A battery up. Down is free, and so are the stairs. THE LIFTMEN.

WANTED. A second spade, and a back to swing it. Asked for every year. THE SEXTON, AT THE ACRE, ON THE WEST ROAD.

A NIGHT'S SLEEP. A bunk with no Hum in it, a battery. Sleep the way the city slept under the charter. Full by Matins. THE QUIET ROOMS, IN THE STONES.

FOUND. A Locket, burn-marked, pulled off the pile on Burn Day with a family’s music still on it. The Faithful gave it to the fire. The Kindlers will give it back. Ask at the edge of the Burn.

BIRDS. Notes flown home over the white streets to the Loft on the customs house, in pad numbers the light can stare at. Ask at the Union Hall.

LETTERS. Letters into the parishes go open, as the parishes take them, and a carrier will read one aloud at the doorway if asked. Write accordingly. THE SORTING OFFICE.

PUT UP. The Breach-born put up on the barrage until they find a room. The Shanties shake at full flood. Sleep through it. ASK AT THE SHANTIES.

WATER. It rains most nights, and none of it is clean. Bring the jug. CISTERN, WESTFIELD.
