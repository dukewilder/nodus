The Order for the Supper

The rite the parishes keep at home on the night of a Gleaning, which they call the Supper, written in the parish hand. A house of the Wakeful kept its page of names on the last leaf of its copy and burned the book with the page on the night, as the Wakeful burn the names of those who walk out.

THE ORDER FOR THE SUPPER OF THE LAMB
AS IT IS KEPT IN THE HOUSES OF THE PARISHES
WRITTEN IN THE PARISH HAND

BLESSED ARE THEY WHICH ARE CALLED.

Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb (Revelation 19:9).

OF THE DAYS BEFORE

When a soul of the house passes nine hundred, the Deacon chalks its name on the ripening board among the Called, and the house hangs a white cloth in its doorway, that the street may know it is blessed.

From that day the Called are given no work and asked for no word. Their portion is set before them at every Hour and taken away at the next, and given to the embers of the parish at Lauds.

If the Called weep, it is a want, and the house shall sing over them until it is given up.

IN ANOTHER HANDCold water if she nods. Keep her chin up. Keep her face to the street.

OF THE COAT

Past eight hundred the Called wear the last coat, every button cut from it. On the eve of the Supper the house shall sew it shut at the back upon them, with white thread and without a knot, for nothing of theirs is to be undone again.

At Compline the eldest of the house shall wrap them in the sheet over the coat, once round, and tuck the end in, and tie nothing.

OF THE EVENING

At Vespers the Called go down to the hall, where the long tables are laid with a plate for every soul of the parish that has gone up and nothing on any plate, and they sit among the plates, and are looked upon, and come home.

On the threshold the Called shall break the loaf they carried home from Leaven, and eat none of it, and the house shall gather the pieces and burn them at Compline, that nothing of the Supper be kept.

OF THE WATCH

From Compline the Called kneel on the threshold with their faces to the street, and the house kneels behind them. Nobody shall stand between the Called and the light.

The house shall keep them awake with the hymns of the Hours, softly, from Compline to Matins, that they be taken at their brightest.

IN ANOTHER HANDLouder. She hears the street.

THE WORDS

At Matins the eldest of the house asks, the Called answer, and the house answers last.

THE ELDESTWho is called?

THE CALLEDI am called.

THE ELDESTTo what?

THE CALLEDTo the marriage supper of the Lamb.

THE ELDESTAs a guest, or as the bread?

THE CALLEDAs both, as He was both.

THE HOUSEBlessed are they which are called.

THE ELDESTWhat do you keep?

THE CALLEDNothing.

THE ELDESTWhat do you want?

THE CALLEDNothing. Not even this.

THE ELDESTThen say the words.

THE CALLEDTake, eat; this is my body.

THE HOUSEAmen.

OF THE SILENCE

When the Hum stops, let nobody speak into the silence. It is the Lord drawing breath.

When the Called rise, the house shall take no hold of them and call them by no name, lest a want be kindled in the house and spoil the bread.

If the Called look down at the house as they rise, the house shall shut its eyes until they are gone.

OF THE MORNING

At Lauds the house shall take in the sheet from the threshold, if it has come down, and burn it.

Grief is a want. It shall be confessed at the hall on the Lord’s Day, and the Deacon shall give a psalm for it.

The white cloth shall hang in the doorway for a year, and the house shall be envied.

BE NOT AFRAID.

The questions the children of the parishes answer aloud in the hall after Terce on the Lord’s Day. The parish’s Deacon carried this copy to the Burn for what was written in its margin.

THE LITTLE CATECHISM
FOR THE CHILDREN OF THE PARISHES
TO BE SAID IN THE HALL AFTER TERCE

2What did He make you for?To ripen.

4What is yours?My wants, my name and my eyes, until I give them up.
