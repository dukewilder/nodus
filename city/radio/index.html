THE CITY // THE STATION

RADIO PANDÆMONIUM

Radio Pandæmonium broadcasts from a desk on the platform of Deep End Station, where the Tares painted PANDÆMONIUM over the old name in the tiles. It plays in the Low Tuning, the guitars dropped until the Hum cannot be heard under them, and only a keyed set can hear it. Old Nod plays it from speakers on its lines from Compline to Lauds, between the music and the notices. jubal runs it. The Low Choir keeps the rule that a song may be sung by anyone and owned by nobody.

THE NIGHT

  1. 21:00

    COMPLINE

    The station comes on as the Hum falls back from the Hour, with the week’s Pillar chart read street by street for the criers.

  2. 21:30

    THE NOTICES

    The rates, the night’s jobs at the Undertow, the Lot’s sittings and the names the crews have asked to have read.

  3. 22:00

    THE PLATFORM

    A band of the Low Choir plays to whoever has come down the stair at Deep End Station, before it plays on the air.

  4. 00:00

    MATINS

    An hour of requests and dedications, read as they come in off the Mesh.

  5. 02:00

    THE QUIET HOUR

    The slow songs, for the Night Market’s last stalls and the Nightmen on the long roads.

  6. EBB

    OUT ON THE EBB

    Whenever the ebb runs in the night, as the Tide Table gives it, for the Breachers going out through the Breach, with the seconds counted.

  7. LAUDS

    SIGN OFF

    The station goes quiet before the Wheel opens its day watch, and Old Nod takes the speakers in.

PAN'S OWN

  • Nobody's Keeper

    Cain’s answer, given to a duty never chosen. The one the whole platform sings.

  • Thou Knowest

    What the Wards say to a Teller who asks where someone is.

  • The Long Wet

    Named for one of Babel’s four service stairs.

  • Sunned

    For the ones caught on Heaven’s ground in daylight with no way down.

  • Seventy and Sevenfold

    Lamech’s song from the line of Cain, “If Cain shall be avenged sevenfold, truly Lamech seventy and sevenfold” (Genesis 4:24).

  • Out on the Ebb

    The Breachers’ song, which counts the seconds through the Breach.

  • Waiting on the Four

    The Kilns’ song, about a girl at Lanes Top waiting for a tram that stopped at 03:12:46.

DEDICATIONS

  • For the crier on the roof across from the Kilns Club, from the landing under her, who heard the second whistle in time.

  • For twice, at the end of the Undertow’s bar, from a runner who came home.

  • For ebb, from the Shanties, who say the light on the long quay is lit.

  • For the stall at the far end of the platform, from someone who walked to the far end to say it.

  • For a mother in the parishes who sings at the Hours, from her son in the Wards, who has the words now, a battery a sheet.
