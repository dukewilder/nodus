THE NIGHT

  1. 21:00

    COMPLINE

    We come on as the Hum falls back from the Hour and the Pillar comes down on the Burn. First, the week’s Pillar chart, street by street, for the criers on the roofs.

  2. 21:30

    THE NOTICES

    The rates, the open jobs on the Undertow’s board, the Lot’s sittings and whom it has published, and the names the crews have asked us to read.

  3. 22:00

    THE PLATFORM

    A band of the Low Choir plays to whoever came down the stair at Deep End Station. If the platform stays, the band goes on the air. The platform is the only critic we keep.

  4. 00:00

    MATINS

    An hour of requests and dedications, read as they come in off the Mesh, while the Night Market is at its loudest. So are we.

  5. 02:00

    THE QUIET HOUR

    The slow songs, for the Night Market’s last stalls and the Nightmen carting the Wards’ buckets to the outfalls. Hold your nose and hum along.

  6. EBB

    OUT ON THE EBB

    Whenever the ebb runs in the night, as the Tide Table gives it, for the Breachers going out through the Breach. We count the seconds free of charge. Lowtide sells its readings.

  7. LAUDS

    SIGN OFF

    We go quiet before the Wheel opens its day watch. Old Nod takes the speakers in, and the city gets the Hum back. Die unread.

PAN'S OWN // THE PLATFORM'S SEVEN

  1. 01

    Nobody's Keeper

    Cain’s answer, “Am I my brother’s keeper?” (Genesis 4:9), thrown back at every duty nobody chose. The one the whole platform sings.

  2. 02

    Thou Knowest

    What the Wards say to a Teller who asks where someone is. Two words, and the door shuts.

  3. 03

    The Long Wet

    Named for one of Babel’s four service stairs, at whose foot the Babel Line signs again every midwinter with its dead struck through.

  4. 04

    Sunned

    For the ones caught on Heaven’s ground in daylight with no way down. For whoever lured them there, the Lot sits.

  5. 05

    Seventy and Sevenfold

    Lamech’s song from the line of Cain, “If Cain shall be avenged sevenfold, truly Lamech seventy and sevenfold” (Genesis 4:24). The Naming House sings it when one of the salted is avenged.

  6. 06

    Out on the Ebb

    The Breachers’ song, which counts the seconds through the Breach with the tide running white under the hull.

  7. 07

    Waiting on the Four

    The Kilns’ song, about a girl at Lanes Top waiting for a tram that stopped at 03:12:46, forty meters short. She is still waiting.

DEDICATIONS

  • For the crier on the roof across from the Kilns Club

    from the landing under her, who waited for past and lived to send this.

  • For twice, at the end of the Undertow's bar

    from a runner who came home with the terms kept and all ten fingers.

  • For ebb

    from the Shanties. Nine hundred runs by our count, six hundred by Lowtide’s, and the light on the long quay is lit.

  • For the stall at the far end of the platform

    from someone who finally walked the length of the platform to say it. Your move.

  • For a mother in the parishes who sings at the Hours

    from her son in the Wards, who bought the words off a Psalter, a battery a sheet. She will never hear this. Play it anyway.

THE STATION

Radio Pandæmonium broadcasts from a desk on the platform of Deep End Station, where the Tares painted PANDÆMONIUM over the old name in the tiles on the night of the Concord, and nobody has cleaned it off. The Notary read the Concord twice at this desk. Heaven’s Choir never shuts up, so ours plays under it, a fourth below the chord, the guitars dropped until the Hum is gone beneath them, on the Mesh, where only a keyed set can hear it. Old Nod plays us from speakers on its lines from Compline to Lauds, between the music and the notices. jubal runs it. The Low Choir keeps the rule that a song may be sung by anyone and owned by nobody.
